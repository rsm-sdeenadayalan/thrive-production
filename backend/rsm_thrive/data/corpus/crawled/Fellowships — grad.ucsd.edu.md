# Fellowships

Fellowships are one of the best ways to fund your graduate education and postdoctoral research. UC San Diego’s Division of Graduate Education and Postdoctoral Affairs encourages all graduate students and postdoctoral scholars to apply for fellowships.

Fellowships provide financial support for graduate students and postdoctoral scholars to enhance their professional development and develop into independent scholars with their own unique research agendas. Fellowships can provide mentored training experiences, fund independent research, support international travel, cover educational and living expenses, and much more. Fellowships vary in amount and support time, ranging from a couple of months to years; some awards are one-time and others are multi-year or eligible for renewal.

**Please note that each applicant is responsible for determining their own individual eligibility for any fellowship.** This includes but is not limited to reviewing which agencies participate in a funding opportunity announcement. Fellowship applicants should also be aware of the provisions of the award and the requirements of the opportunity. **Please be aware that if the fellowship opportunity requires that you list your mentor as the Principal Investigator (PI) on the project, you will need to work with the [Office of Contract & Grant Administration](http://blink.ucsd.edu/sponsor/ocga/index.html).**

## GEPA Fellowships

GEPA administers a variety of internal (intramural) and external (extramural) competitive fellowship opportunities each year. Fellowship details, including award amounts, funding duration, and eligibility requirements, are shared with department administrators, graduate students, and postdoctoral scholars at the beginning of each nomination cycle. Because these details may vary from year to year and funding details are not finalized until the start of the nomination cycle, we encourage prospective applicants to connect with their department if they are interested in a GEPA fellowship opportunity and believe they may be eligible.

When a GEPA fellowship opportunity is open for submissions, it will be posted on [UC San Diego's InfoReady application portal](https://ucsd.infoready4.com/). Questions can be directed to [gepafellowshipnoms@ucsd.edu,](https://grad.ucsd.edu/financial/fellowships/GEPAFellowshipnoms@UCSD.edu) which will open a Services & Support ticket.

## DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service)

The German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) offers funding opportunities for students and scholars to pursue graduate study or research in Germany. Each year, UC San Diego may nominate **one priority candidate** for either a DAAD Study Scholarship or a DAAD Research Grant.

A UC San Diego nomination provides candidates with preferential consideration in DAAD's highly competitive selection process. While nominees compete in a separate applicant pool with a higher likelihood of receiving funding, final award decisions are made by DAAD’s independent Selection Committee.

#### Funding Opportunities

**Study Scholarships**Support graduate-level study in Germany for students from all academic disciplines (excluding architecture and artistic fields).

Funding may be used for:

-   A full master’s degree program at a state or state-recognized German university.
-   Up to one year of study in Germany as part of a master's or postgraduate degree program being completed at the student's home institution.

**Research Grants (One-Year Grants)**Support doctoral students and recent postdoctoral scholars conducting research in Germany.

Eligible applicants include:

-   Doctoral students enrolled at a university or research institution outside Germany.
-   Postdoctoral scholars within four years of completing their doctorate.

#### Eligibility Requirements

**Study Scholarship Applicants**

-   Must hold an undergraduate degree.
-   Must plan to pursue graduate-level study in Germany.
-   Cannot receive funding for a master's program already started in Germany.
-   If studying abroad as part of a degree program at their home institution, academic credits earned in Germany must be recognized by the home institution.

**Research Grant Applicants**

-   Must be a doctoral student or recent postdoctoral scholar.
-   Cannot be completing their entire doctorate at a German university.

#### Funding Duration

-   **Study Scholarships:** 10–24 months, depending on the length of the academic program.
-   **Research Grants:** Typically support research projects of up to one year.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Curriculum Vitae (CV)**
-   **Letter of Motivation** describing:
    -   Academic and personal reasons for pursuing study or research in Germany
    -   Expected benefits of the experience
    -   How the opportunity will contribute to future academic and professional goals
-   **Reference Letter**

**This fellowship is typically run in September. Please reach out to your home department graduate coordinator for more details.**

## Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings

The Lindau Meetings are unique scientific conferences where Nobel Laureates and the most promising Young Scientists meet to educate, inspire, and connect beyond cultural and political boundaries. The annual conferences are alternately dedicated to the Nobel Prize disciplines of Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, as well as Economics. Every year, the Lindau Meetings attract about 30-40 Nobel Laureates and 600 outstanding Young Scientists from around the globe, thus becoming the world’s largest regular gathering of Nobel Laureates beside the award ceremony in Stockholm itself. Since 1951, more than 35,000 Young Scientists have participated in the Lindau Meetings. Thanks to the Lindau Alumni Network, many of them are still connected to Lindau and to each other, thus creating an exceptional network of scientific excellence.

Please visit the [Lindau Nobel Laureate Meetings website](https://www.lindau-nobel.org/young-scientists/) for more information and selection criteria.

Students/postdocs should receive support and approval by their department. Nominees should:

-   receive strong support of their application by their graduate advisor and/or faculty advisor and/or by an internationally renowned scientist, who knows the applicant well, via the letters of recommendation
-   deliver a comprehensive Curriculum Vitae (CV) together with their application materials
-   commit themselves to being present in Lindau for the full duration of the meeting.

This fellowship is typically run in September. Please reach out to your home department graduate coordinator for more details.

## Charlotte W. Newcombe Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship

The Charlotte W. Newcombe Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship supports doctoral candidates whose dissertation research in the humanities, social sciences, or related fields examines questions of religion, ethics, values, or morals. The fellowship is designed to provide support for a year of focused dissertation writing beginning in September following the selection of Fellows.

While most recipients come from disciplines such as Religious Studies, Philosophy, History, Anthropology, Political Science, Sociology, and Literature, eligibility is based on the dissertation topic rather than the student's academic department. Students from interdisciplinary programs and, in some cases, STEM fields are encouraged to apply if their research centrally addresses questions of religion, ethics, values, or morals.

Students apply directly to the Newcombe Fellowship program and do not require nomination or oversight from GEPA during the application process. Following the national review, Newcombe Fellowship administrators provide GEPA with a list of eligible UC San Diego applicants. GEPA then conducts an internal review through the Fellowship and Award Review Committee to select the UC San Diego nominee.

Students interested in this fellowship are encouraged to visit the official [Charlotte W. Newcombe Doctoral Dissertation Fellowship website](https://newcombefoundation.org/fellowships/) for complete eligibility requirements, application instructions, and deadlines. Applications are submitted directly to the fellowship program. This fellowship typically opens in late summer.

## Judith & Neil Morgan Fellowship

The Judith & Neil Morgan Fellowship recognizes outstanding doctoral students whose research uses **humanistic methods to examine the culture and context of pressing societal issues**. The fellowship is intended as a mark of distinction and is awarded in addition to the financial support already provided by the student's graduate program. The Judith & Neil Morgan Fellowship supports exceptional scholars whose research brings humanistic perspectives to complex societal challenges and demonstrates the potential to make meaningful contributions to their academic disciplines.

Awardees will be awarded a stipend.

**Applicants must:**

-   Be enrolled in one of the following Ph.D. programs:
    -   Anthropology
    -   Communication
    -   History
    -   Linguistics
    -   Literature
    -   Philosophy
    -   Psychology
    -   Sociology
-   Be an outstanding doctoral student or candidate.
-   Be enrolled as a **full-time student** during the 2026–27 academic year.
-   Meet all university fellowship eligibility requirements at the time of award disbursement.

Both **incoming and continuing Ph.D. students** are eligible for nomination.

#### Required Application Materials

Departments must submit the following materials:

-   **Departmental Financial Support Package Letter**
    -   Describes the financial support offered to the student through their graduate program.
-   **Department Chair or Graduate Adviser Letter**
    -   Addresses the student's academic performance and accomplishments.
    -   Evaluates their potential for significant contributions to research and/or teaching.
    -   Explains how the nominee meets the fellowship eligibility criteria and aligns with the fellowship's purpose.
    -   For incoming students, it includes information about undergraduate academic achievement.
-   **Research Statement** (maximum 2 pages, excluding citations)
    -   Describes the student's research project.
    -   Explains how the work uses humanistic methods to illuminate the culture and context of important societal issues.
    -   Discusses the potential impact of the research on the field.
-   **Admission Materials** (for incoming students only)
    -   Statement of purpose
    -   Letters of recommendation
    -   Unofficial undergraduate transcripts
-   **Department Nomination Attestation Form**

## Rita L. Atkinson Graduate Fellowship

The Rita L. Atkinson Graduate Fellowship supports outstanding doctoral students conducting **interdisciplinary research in the sciences and related fields** at UC San Diego. The fellowship is designed to encourage innovative research that integrates perspectives, methods, or expertise from multiple disciplines. The Rita L. Atkinson Graduate Fellowship recognizes doctoral students whose interdisciplinary research bridges multiple fields and has the potential to advance knowledge through innovative collaboration across disciplines.

The fellowship provides:

-   **A stipend** paid over 12 months (four consecutive quarters, including summer)
-   **Tuition and fee coverage** during fall, winter, and spring quarters

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be a continuing Ph.D. student at UC San Diego.
-   Be engaged in interdisciplinary research in the sciences or related fields.
-   Be actively advised by **at least two faculty advisors from different disciplines and departments/programs**.
-   Demonstrate that both advisors play meaningful roles in guiding the interdisciplinary aspects of the research.
-   Remain enrolled through the entire fellowship period and **not plan to graduate before the final quarter of fellowship funding**.

Both domestic and international students are eligible.

**Important Notes**

-   Co-advisors do not need to be formally designated as co-chairs or be members of the dissertation committee.
-   Advisors may be from different institutions, provided they represent different disciplines and are actively involved in the student's research.
-   Students with only one advisor are not eligible.
-   Fellowship recipients may not hold instructional appointments during the fellowship period, except during the summer period between spring and fall terms. Limited research appointments may be approved by exception if they directly support dissertation research.

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants clearly demonstrate:

-   A well-integrated interdisciplinary research project.
-   Meaningful collaboration across distinct academic disciplines.
-   Active mentorship from faculty advisors representing different departments or programs.
-   The significance and innovation of their interdisciplinary work.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Statement of Interest** (1 page maximum)
    -   Describes the interdisciplinary nature of the research.
    -   Explains the project(s) the student plans to pursue during the fellowship period.
-   **Faculty Support Letter**
    -   Co-signed by at least two faculty advisors and endorsed by the department chair or graduate program.
    -   Addresses:
        -   Academic performance and achievements
        -   Publications, presentations, and other scholarly accomplishments
        -   The interdisciplinary nature of the student's research
        -   How the nominee meets the fellowship eligibility criteria
-   **Current Transcript** (unofficial transcripts accepted)
-   **Department Attestation Form**

## Dissertation Year Fellowships

The Dissertation Year Fellowships provide **terminal support** for outstanding doctoral candidates in the final stages of their Ph.D. research. These fellowships support students who demonstrate strong potential for university teaching and research and who are expected to complete all degree requirements during the fellowship year.

There are three Dissertation Year Fellowship opportunities:

-   **UC President’s Dissertation Year Fellowship (PDYF)**
-   **Fletcher Jones Dissertation Year Fellowship (FJF)**
-   **Marye Anne Fox Dissertation Year Fellowship (MAF)**

All nominees are reviewed together by an interdisciplinary committee. Graduate programs may nominate **one student for each fellowship**, and nominations must be determined and approved by the student’s graduate program.

#### Award Benefits

Each Dissertation Year Fellowship includes:

-   **a stipend** for the fellowship year
-   **Resident tuition and mandatory fees** covered by the student’s degree-granting program

Additional benefits:

-   **PDYF:** Includes a **$500 travel allowance** to support presentation of dissertation research at another UC campus, California State University campus, or independent California university.

#### General Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be a doctoral student in a Ph.D. program under the jurisdiction of EVC-Academic Affairs.
-   Have **advanced to candidacy** at the time of nomination.
-   Be making satisfactory progress toward completing the dissertation during the fellowship year.
-   Be projected to complete all Ph.D. requirements by the end of the award year (for example, if the fellowship is awarded for the 2026-27 academic year, the fellow must be projected to complete the fellowship by June 2027)
-   Be eligible for student support throughout the award period.
-   Be a:
    -   U.S. citizen,
    -   permanent resident, or
    -   student eligible for nonresident tuition exemption under AB 540.

A presentation of dissertation research during the fellowship year is required.

**Fellowship-Specific Eligibility**

### UC President’s Dissertation Year Fellowship (PDYF)

The PDYF supports students who:

-   Have experienced educational or economic disadvantage, **or**
-   Conduct research or plan careers focused on issues affecting disadvantaged communities, and
-   Demonstrate strong potential and commitment to a career in university teaching and research.

**Award:**

-   $37,500 stipend
-   $500 research presentation travel allowance
-   Resident tuition and fees covered by the degree-granting program

### Fletcher Jones Dissertation Year Fellowship (FJF)

The Fletcher Jones Fellowship supports students who demonstrate **financial need**, including those who:

-   Depend on student loans, or
-   Have worked more than 50% employment to finance their education.

**Eligible disciplines:**

-   All General Campus disciplines except Business Management
-   Health Sciences disciplines except Medicine

**Award:**

-   $37,500 stipend
-   Resident tuition and fees covered by the degree-granting program

### Marye Anne Fox Dissertation Year Fellowship (MAF)

The Marye Anne Fox Fellowship recognizes students with **highly distinguished academic records**.

**Award:**

-   $37,500 stipend
-   Resident tuition and fees covered by the degree-granting program

**Required Nomination Materials**

All nomination materials must be submitted by the graduate program and include:

-   **Student Autobiographical Sketch**
-   **Dissertation Synopsis** 
    -   Written for a non-technical audience
    -   Describes the significance and relevance of the dissertation research
    -   For PDYF nominees, includes a brief proposal for presenting research at another California university
-   **Academic Completion Timeline**
    -   Includes projected dissertation completion date
-   **Faculty Advisor Letter of Support**
    -   Addresses the student’s qualifications and eligibility for the fellowship
    -   Discusses academic achievements, research potential, and progress toward completion
    -   For PDYF: Includes plans to support the student’s research presentation requirement
    -   For Fletcher Jones: Addresses the student’s financial need
-   **Completed Department Attestation Form**
    -   Signed by the department/program chair

### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong candidates will demonstrate:

-   Clear evidence that they can complete the dissertation within the fellowship year.
-   Significant research accomplishments and scholarly potential.
-   A compelling dissertation project with broader academic or societal impact.
-   Strong commitment to a future career in university teaching and research.
-   Alignment with the specific goals of the fellowship for which they are nominated.

This fellowship is typically run in October. Please reach out to your home department graduate coordinator for more details.

## Dolores Zohrab Liebmann Fellowship

The Will of Dolores Zohrab Liebmann created a perpetual charitable trust designated as “The Dolores Zohrab Liebmann Fund” for the purpose of funding advanced education and graduate study grants, which must be carried out entirely in the United States of America. The Dolores Zohrab Liebmann Fellowships are to be awarded only to candidates who have outstanding undergraduate records, have demonstrated a need for financial assistance, are citizens of the United States of America, are enrolled in accredited colleges and universities in the United States and have received baccalaureate degrees.

Mrs. Liebmann was the daughter of a prominent Armenian intellectual, writer and statesman and was married to one of the owners of a successful American business. She supported students and educational and charitable organizations during her lifetime. Mrs. Liebmann’s primary concern, as expressed in her Will, was to attract and support students with outstanding character and ability who hold promise for achievement and distinction in their chosen fields of study. The trustees welcome applications from students of all national origins who are United States citizens.

Fellowships are available to students who:

-   are enrolled in a graduate program and pursuing a graduate degree at a designated institution of higher learning at the time of application. Individuals applying to graduate programs are not qualified candidates.
-   pursuing a program of study that includes any recognized field of study in the humanities, social sciences, or natural sciences (including law, medicine, engineering, architecture or other formal professional training). The selection committee has a strong preference for supporting scholarly endeavors.
-   have an outstanding undergraduate record, must demonstrate financial need, and must be a US citizen.

Fellowship Award: Stipend $18,000, tuition (no fees) for the academic year

This fellowship is typically run in November. Please reach out to your home department graduate coordinator for more details.

## Graduate Research Mentorship Program (GRMP) Fellowship

The Graduate Research Mentorship Program (GRMP) supports doctoral students in developing advanced research skills through faculty mentorship and dissertation-related research activities. The fellowship is designed to help students make progress toward advancement to candidacy, increase doctoral completion rates, and prepare future faculty and scholars.

Each award includes a stipend, resident tuition, and mandatory fees. Fellowship recipients and their faculty mentors must submit a final report describing progress made toward the goals outlined in the proposed work plan.

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be enrolled in an eligible UC San Diego Ph.D. program under the jurisdiction of EVC-Academic Affairs.
-   Have completed required coursework but **not yet advanced to candidacy**.
-   Be eligible for support throughout the entire fellowship period.
-   Be a U.S. citizen, permanent resident, or qualify for a nonresident tuition exemption under AB 540.
-   Not have previously received a GRMP fellowship.

Preference is given to students in their **second, third, or fourth year** of doctoral study.

Applicants must also meet **at least one** of the following criteria:

-   Have experienced circumstances that created barriers to pursuing graduate education.
-   Conduct research focused on cultural, societal, or educational issues affecting educationally disadvantaged communities.
-   Demonstrate an intention to use their doctoral degree to serve educationally underrepresented populations.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Student CV**
-   **Faculty mentor CV**
-   **Research Proposal**
-   **Work Plan** (1 page maximum) outlining:
    -   Goals and timeline for advancement to candidacy
    -   How the GRMP fellowship will support these objectives
-   **Personal Statement** 
-   **Faculty Mentor Letter of Support**

This fellowship is an excellent opportunity for doctoral students to strengthen their research skills, receive dedicated faculty mentorship, and make meaningful progress toward their academic and professional goals.

This fellowship is typically run in December. Please reach out to your home department graduate coordinator for more details.

## UC President’s Pre-Professoriate Fellowship Program (PPPF)

The UC President’s Pre-Professoriate Fellowship Program (PPPF) is funded by the University of California Office of the President (UCOP) to support doctoral students from California Hispanic Serving Institutions (HSIs) who are interested in pursuing careers in higher education and the professoriate.

The program aims to strengthen pathways to faculty careers by providing financial support, professional development opportunities, and resources that help fellows prepare for academic careers.

Each fellow will receive:

-   **Stipend** for one academic year
-   **Professional development fund** to support activities that prepare and inspire students for careers in the professoriate
-   **Resident tuition and mandatory fees** covered by GEPA during the fellowship period

Professional development funds may be used throughout the remainder of the fellow’s doctoral program and do not need to be spent during the fellowship year.

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be a **U.S. citizen, permanent resident, or AB 540-eligible student** (international students are not eligible).
-   Be enrolled in an **academic Ph.D. program** at UC San Diego.
-   Have **advanced to candidacy** before nomination.
-   Have earned a **bachelor’s or stand-alone master’s degree** from a California Hispanic Serving Institution (HSI), or:
    -   Have transferred from a California Community College designated as an HSI and completed at least **24 semester units (or equivalent)**.
-   Demonstrate exceptional academic and research achievement.
-   Have a record of advancing **equity and inclusion** and a commitment to continuing these efforts throughout their career.
-   Demonstrate potential and interest in pursuing a career in the professoriate.

Preference is given to students who are not expected to graduate before the end of the fellowship year.

**Note:** UC San Diego alumni who earned a bachelor's or master's degree at UC San Diego are eligible, as UC San Diego is recognized as an HSI for this fellowship program.

#### Application Materials

-   **Curriculum Vitae (CV)** (maximum 3 pages)
-   **Student Statement** (maximum 1 page) describing:
    -   Contributions to equity and inclusion
    -   Commitment to advancing these efforts throughout their career
-   **Two Faculty Letters of Support**
    -   One letter must be from the faculty advisor
    -   Letters should address:
        -   Academic performance and research potential
        -   Likelihood of pursuing a faculty or professoriate career
-   **Department Attestation Form**

Strong nominees typically demonstrate:

## UC’s Native American Opportunity Plan (NAOP)

UC’s Native American Opportunity Plan ensures that in-state systemwide Tuition and Student Services Fees are fully covered for California students who are also enrolled in federally recognized Native American, American Indian, and Alaska Native tribes. This plan applies to undergraduate and graduate students.

To qualify for the Native American Opportunity Plan, students must meet **all** of the following eligibility requirements:

-   Be a current or newly admitted University of California graduate student.
-   Be classified as a [California resident for tuition purposes.](https://students.ucsd.edu/finances/fees/residence/residency-requirements-graduate-students.html)
-   Be an enrolled member of a federally recognized Native American, American Indian, and/or Alaska Native tribe and provide acceptable documentation of enrollment.
-   Be enrolled in a state-supported graduate degree program. Certificate programs, [self-supporting graduate programs](https://blink.ucsd.edu/instructors/academic-info/majors/selfsupport-codes.html) (e.g., Executive MBA programs), and extension programs do not qualify.

GEPA will reach out directly to new and continuing students who might be eligible.

## Schmidt Science Fellows Program

The Schmidt Science Fellows Program supports exceptional Ph.D. students and recent graduates who are interested in pursuing **interdisciplinary research** and becoming future leaders in science and society. The program is designed for researchers who want to **pivot into a new field of study during their postdoctoral training**, expanding beyond the discipline of their Ph.D.

UC San Diego may nominate **up to seven applicants** to compete for this prestigious international fellowship.

Fellows receive funding to conduct a postdoctoral research project in a field different from their doctoral research area, while participating in leadership development, mentorship, and a global network of interdisciplinary scientists.

#### Award Benefits

Selected Schmidt Science Fellows receive:

-   **$110,000 annual stipend** for up to two years of postdoctoral research
-   Funding to conduct research at an approved institution anywhere in the world
-   Participation in the **Science Leadership Program**, including leadership training and professional development
-   Personalized mentorship from internationally recognized scientific leaders
-   Membership in a lifelong global community of interdisciplinary researchers
-   Flexibility to pursue ambitious, innovative, and high-risk research projects

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be completing a Ph.D. in:
    -   Astronomy
    -   Biology
    -   Chemistry
    -   Physics
    -   Earth Sciences
    -   Engineering
    -   Mathematics
    -   Computing
-   Be expected to complete all Ph.D. requirements by the end of the academic year of the award 
-   Demonstrate a strong interest in pursuing an **interdisciplinary research pivot** during a postdoctoral appointment.

Typically not eligible:

-   Students in clinical-track MD-Ph.D. or veterinary-Ph.D. programs.
-   Students pursuing Ph.D.s in social sciences, political science, or economics.
-   Applicants proposing a research pivot into social sciences, political science, or economics.

#### What Makes a Competitive Applicant?

The strongest applicants demonstrate:

-   Outstanding academic achievement and research excellence.
-   A genuine commitment to interdisciplinary science.
-   Interest in moving into a substantially different research field during postdoctoral training.
-   Creativity, curiosity, and willingness to take scientific risks.
-   Strong leadership potential and the ability to inspire and collaborate with others.
-   A commitment to using science to address important societal and global challenges.
-   A record of successful collaboration across diverse teams and perspectives.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Letter of Recommendation**
    -   From the primary Ph.D. advisor or principal investigator (PI)
    -   Should address the fellowship selection criteria and the applicant's potential for interdisciplinary leadership and research
-   **Personal Statement** (maximum 2 pages)
    -   Discusses:
        -   Interest in pursuing research outside the applicant’s Ph.D. discipline
        -   Commitment to interdisciplinary science and scientific risk-taking
        -   Collaborative experiences and teamwork
        -   Leadership potential
        -   Goals for creating positive societal impact through science
-   **Curriculum Vitae (CV)** (maximum 2 pages)
-   **Department Attestation Form**

This fellowship is typically run in March. Please reach out to your graduate coordinator for more details.

## Guru Gobind Singh Fellowship

The Guru Gobind Singh Fellowship was established through a gift from **Karam Singh Maughan** to support graduate students from designated regions of **India and Pakistan** pursuing advanced study at the University of California.

Named in honor of **Guru Gobind Singh**, the fellowship supports outstanding students who demonstrate strong academic achievement and a commitment to using their education to benefit their home country after completing their degree.

UC San Diego may nominate **one student** to compete for this University of California-wide fellowship.

#### Award Benefits

-   **$36,000 stipend award** 
-   Nonresident Supplemental Tuition (NRST), if applicable, may be supported by the nominating campus
-   Recognition as the UC San Diego nominee for a prestigious UC systemwide fellowship

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be a graduate student from an eligible region of **India or Pakistan**![Map of Eligible Area](https://grad.ucsd.edu/financial/fellowships/Guru-Gobind-Singh-image.png)
-   Be committed to returning to their country of origin after completing their degree.
-   **Not have previously engaged in graduate study at another U.S. institution** before attending UC San Diego.
-   Be enrolled in a graduate degree program at UC San Diego.

Students at **any stage of graduate study** may be nominated.

Preference is given to students who:

-   Already hold a master's degree.
-   Have advanced to the dissertation stage of their graduate program.
-   Have exhausted or are nearing the end of traditional sources of graduate student funding.

#### Selection Criteria

Applications are evaluated based on:

-   Academic achievement and scholarly excellence.
-   Strength of recommendation letters.
-   Potential contributions to the student's home country following degree completion.
-   Overall quality of nomination materials.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Up to Three Letters of Recommendation**
    -   One letter must be from the student's faculty sponsor.
-   **University Transcripts**
-   **Supporting Documentation** (optional)
    -   Any materials that strengthen the nomination.
-   **Academic Status Information**
    -   Whether the student has advanced to candidacy.
    -   Expected degree completion date.
-   **Statement of Future Plans**
    -   Describes how the student intends to use their graduate education to contribute to their home country after graduation.

This fellowship is typically run in May.

## Google Ph.D. Fellowship Program

The Google Ph.D. Fellowship Program recognizes exceptional doctoral students conducting innovative research in computer science and related fields. The fellowship is designed to support emerging researchers whose work has the potential to make significant contributions to their discipline and society.

Google Fellows are selected based on the quality and impact of their research, academic achievements, and leadership potential. In addition to financial support, fellows are paired with a Google Research Mentor and become part of a global community of leading researchers.

UC San Diego may nominate **up to three students** for the fellowship competition.

#### Award Benefits

Recipients receive:

-   **Up to two years of fellowship funding**
-   Full coverage of:
    -   Tuition and fees
    -   Health insurance
    -   Books and educational expenses
-   A stipend for:
    -   Living expenses
    -   Travel
    -   Research-related equipment
-   Mentorship from a Google researcher

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be enrolled full-time in a Ph.D. program at UC San Diego.
-   Have completed graduate coursework requirements before the fellowship begins in the Fall
-   Remain enrolled full-time in their Ph.D. program throughout the fellowship period.
-   Conduct research in a field aligned with Google's eligible research areas.
-   Demonstrate outstanding academic achievement and research potential.

Not eligible:

-   Google employees and members of their household.
-   Students already receiving a comparable industry-sponsored fellowship.
-   Previous Google Ph.D. Fellowship recipients.

#### Selection Criteria

Applications are evaluated based on:

-   Quality, originality, and impact of the research proposal.
-   Alignment of the proposed research with Google's research priorities and AI Principles.
-   Academic accomplishments and scholarly achievements.
-   Leadership potential and contributions to their academic or broader community.
-   Potential impact of the research on the field and society.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Academic Transcripts**
    -   Current and previous academic records (unofficial transcripts accepted)
-   **Primary Advisor CV**
    -   Maximum 1 page
-   **Student CV**
    -   Include publications, research accomplishments, and links to professional websites if applicable
-   **Research/Dissertation Proposal** (maximum 3 pages, excluding references)
    -   Describes current research and future research directions
    -   Explains significance, innovation, and potential impact
-   **Essay #1** (350 words maximum)
    -   Describes:
        -   Desired impact of the research
        -   Importance of the research area
        -   Personal, educational, and professional experiences that influenced research interests
-   **Essay #2** (350 words maximum)
    -   Describes a leadership experience and its impact
    -   May include leadership through mentoring, project management, community engagement, conflict resolution, caregiving, or other meaningful contributions
-   **Two to Three Letters of Recommendation**
    -   At least one letter must be from the student's thesis advisor

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants typically demonstrate:

-   Innovative and technically rigorous research.
-   Clear alignment between their work and Google's research interests.
-   Evidence of significant research contributions and scholarly impact.
-   Strong leadership, collaboration, and mentorship experiences.
-   A compelling vision for how their research will advance the field and benefit society.

This fellowship is typically run in May.

## Hartwell Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship

The Hartwell Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship supports outstanding early-career researchers pursuing **innovative biomedical research with the potential to benefit children in the United States**. The fellowship is intended to help promising scientists and engineers establish themselves during the initial stages of their postdoctoral research careers.

UC San Diego may nominate **one candidate** for this highly competitive national fellowship.

The Hartwell Foundation prioritizes **cutting-edge, applied biomedical research** that addresses unmet clinical needs or strategic challenges in biomedical science. Preference is given to projects with a clear and compelling potential benefit to children's health.

#### Award Benefits

-   **Two years of postdoctoral fellowship support**
-   Funding to support advanced research training under the supervision of a qualified mentor
-   Recognition as a Hartwell Foundation Fellow

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Hold a **Ph.D. or equivalent doctoral degree**.
-   Be a **U.S. citizen**.
-   Be in the **early stages of their research career development**.
-   Conduct research in a science or engineering field related to **biomedical science**.
-   Work under the supervision of an approved faculty mentor.
-   Commit to completing the full two-year fellowship period.

Additional requirements:

-   Research must have a clear connection to improving health outcomes for children in the United States.
-   Fellowship funds may **not** be used to complete or extend clinical training.
-   The primary mentor cannot be a currently funded Hartwell Investigator.
-   Fellows must begin their postdoctoral appointment no later than **November 1** of the award year.
-   Previous Hartwell Fellows are not eligible to apply again.

Current postdoctoral scholars may apply, provided their proposed training plan reflects the intended two-year fellowship period.

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants typically demonstrate:

-   Outstanding potential as an independent biomedical researcher.
-   Research that addresses an important unmet need in pediatric health or biomedical science.
-   Innovative and translational approaches with potential real-world impact.
-   A well-defined training plan that will significantly advance their research career.
-   Strong mentorship and a clear pathway toward future scientific leadership.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Mentor Letter of Support** (maximum 2 pages)
    -   Describes:
        -   Applicant qualifications and potential
        -   Eligibility for the fellowship
        -   Current appointment status
        -   Quality of the proposed mentorship and training environment
-   **Project Statement** (maximum 2 pages)
    -   Must be formatted in:
        -   Times New Roman, 12-point font
        -   One-inch margins
    -   Should include:
        -   Proposed fellowship start and end dates
        -   Summary of the proposed research training
        -   Relevant prior training experiences
        -   Career goals
        -   Description of how the two-year fellowship period will support professional development
-   **Curriculum Vitae (CV) or NIH-Style Biosketch** (maximum 5 pages)
    -   Must clearly indicate:
        -   Ph.D. award date
        -   Postdoctoral start date (or anticipated start date)

The Hartwell Foundation Postdoctoral Fellowship is an exceptional opportunity for emerging biomedical researchers whose work has the potential to improve children's health through innovative scientific discovery and translational research.

This fellowship is typically run in May. Please reach out to your graduate coordinator for further details.

## ARCS Scholar Fellowship

The ARCS Scholar Fellowship supports outstanding doctoral students in science, engineering, mathematics, and health-related fields who demonstrate exceptional promise for advancing research and innovation. Funded by the ARCS Foundation, the fellowship recognizes students with the potential to make significant contributions to scientific discovery and the intellectual and material welfare of society.

The fellowship is intended to supplement, not replace, existing support provided by the student's department, program, advisor, or other funding sources.

#### Award Benefits

-   **Stipend fellowship award**
-   Renewable for up to **three years**, contingent upon:
    -   Continued academic excellence
    -   Full-time enrollment
    -   Available funding

**Note:** The ARCS Fellowship does **not** include tuition or fee payments.

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be a **U.S. citizen** (naturalized citizens are eligible; permanent residents are not eligible).
-   Have a **minimum GPA of 3.5**.
-   Be enrolled full-time during the entire academic year.
-   Be entering their **second, third, or fourth year** of doctoral study during the award year
-   Not in their first year or final year of study.
-   Ideally have at least three years remaining in their doctoral program.

\*Clinical Psychology JDP students whose primary faculty advisor is affiliated with SDSU must apply through SDSU; students whose primary faculty advisor is affiliated with UC San Diego must apply through UC San Diego. Please note that the calls for nominations and submission deadlines vary between institutions.

Applicants must be enrolled in one of the eligible doctoral programs, including fields such as:

-   Astronomy and Astrophysics
-   Biological Sciences and Biomedical Sciences
-   Bioengineering and Bioinformatics
-   Biostatistics
-   Chemistry and Biochemistry
-   Chemical Engineering
-   Computer Science and Engineering
-   Data Science
-   Electrical and Computer Engineering
-   Materials Science
-   Mathematics
-   Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering
-   NanoEngineering
-   Neurosciences
-   Physics
-   Public Health (Epidemiology, Global Health, Health Behavior)
-   Scripps Institution of Oceanography
-   Structural Engineering
-   Clinical Psychology (eligibility depends on advisor affiliation)

#### Selection Criteria

Selection is based primarily on:

-   Excellence in research and scholarship.
-   Exceptional promise for future scientific achievement.
-   Potential to make significant contributions to science, technology, and society.
-   Academic accomplishments, leadership, service, and professional engagement.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Faculty Recommendation Letter**
    -   Written by a UC San Diego faculty member
    -   Co-signed by the graduate program chair
-   **Expanded Curriculum Vitae (CV) or Resume**
    -   Includes:
        -   Academic achievements
        -   Short abstracts of publications, presentations, and speaking engagements
        -   Honors, awards, leadership activities, service, and extracurricular involvement
-   **Unofficial UC San Diego Transcript**
-   **Department Attestation Form**

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants typically demonstrate:

-   Outstanding academic performance and research accomplishments.
-   Significant potential for future scientific contributions.
-   Active engagement in leadership, service, mentoring, or professional activities.
-   A sustained record of excellence that reflects both scholarly achievement and future promise as a researcher and innovator.

This fellowship is typically run in June.

## Apple Scholars in AI/ML PhD Fellowship

The Apple Scholars in AI/ML PhD Fellowship recognizes outstanding Ph.D. students conducting cutting-edge research in **artificial intelligence (AI)** and **machine learning (ML)**. The program supports emerging leaders in computer science and engineering who have demonstrated exceptional research accomplishments and show strong potential to advance the field through innovation, collaboration, and leadership.

In addition to financial support, Apple Scholars receive mentorship from Apple researchers and become part of a community of leading AI and ML researchers.

#### Award Benefits

Recipients receive support for **two academic years**, including:

-   **Full tuition and fees** (including enrollment fees, health insurance, and books)
-   **Up to $40,000 per year** for living expenses and related educational costs
-   **Stipend** for research-related travel and professional development
-   **Mentorship from an Apple researcher**

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Be enrolled full-time in a Ph.D. program at the time of nomination.
-   Expect to remain enrolled through the end of the **academic year** of the award year
-   Be entering the **final two to three years** of their Ph.D. program 
-   Conduct research relevant to AI, machine learning, or related fields.
-   Not hold another industry-sponsored full fellowship during the Apple Fellowship period

Not eligible:

-   Students in the first year of their Ph.D. program during the nomination cycle

**Selection Criteria**

Applications are evaluated based on:

-   Strength, originality, and technical quality of the research proposal.
-   Impact of the applicant's research contributions to date.
-   Potential to advance the field of AI and machine learning.
-   Leadership, collaboration, and engagement within the research community.
-   Alignment of the proposed research with Apple's areas of interest.
-   Unique perspectives, experiences, and contributions the nominee brings to the field.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Curriculum Vitae (CV) and Publication List**
    -   Includes research experience, publications, awards, and professional accomplishments.
-   **Research Abstract** (maximum 200 words)
    -   Concise summary of the research project and its significance.
-   **Research Statement** (maximum 4 pages, including citations)
    -   Describes:
        -   Previous research accomplishments
        -   Research goals for the next two years
        -   Research hypothesis or key questions
        -   Expected contributions and impact
    -   Should be written in a clear and readable format.
-   **Two Letters of Recommendation**
    -   One letter must be from the student's current advisor.
    -   Maximum one page per letter.
-   **Link to Most Recent Published Work** (optional)
-   **Estimated Tuition and Fees**
    -   Includes tuition, enrollment fees, health insurance, books, and other required educational expenses for the academic year.
-   **Department Attestation Form**

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants typically demonstrate:

-   Outstanding research accomplishments in AI, machine learning, or related fields.
-   A clear and innovative research vision with potential for significant impact.
-   Evidence of leadership, collaboration, and engagement within the research community.
-   A strong publication record or other meaningful scholarly contributions.
-   Research that aligns with emerging challenges and opportunities in artificial intelligence and machine learning.

This fellowship is typically run in Spring.

## Warren Alpert Distinguished Scholar Award

The Warren Alpert Distinguished Scholar Award supports outstanding postdoctoral researchers in **neuroscience** as they transition to independent academic careers. The program is designed to help exceptional early-career scientists advance to faculty positions and establish research programs that can compete successfully for independent funding.

UC San Diego may nominate **one postdoctoral scholar** for this prestigious national award.

#### Award Benefits

The award provides:

-   **$200,000 per year for two years** (awarded to the sponsoring institution)
-   Funding may be used for:
    -   Salary support
    -   Research and laboratory expenses
    -   Other project-related costs
-   Under certain circumstances, funding may be transferred to support the scholar's new faculty appointment
-   Up to **15% indirect costs** may be included within the award budget

#### Eligibility Requirements

Applicants must:

-   Hold an **M.D., Ph.D., or equivalent doctoral degree**.
-   Be employed in a postdoctoral research position at a U.S. medical school, research institute, academic institution, or hospital.
-   Have completed **at least 3 years but no more than 6 years** of postdoctoral training by **July 1, 2026**.
-   Conduct research focused on **neuroscience**.
-   Commit a minimum of **75% effort** to the proposed research project.
-   Be preparing for a transition to an independent academic research career.

Each nominee must propose a unique neuroscience research project.

#### Selection Criteria

Applications are evaluated based on:

-   Excellence and impact of prior research accomplishments.
-   Scientific creativity and innovation.
-   Significance of the proposed neuroscience research.
-   Potential to become an independent research leader.
-   Strength and feasibility of the proposed research plan.
-   Overall promise for a successful academic career.

#### Required Application Materials

Applicants must submit:

-   **Application Form**
    -   Includes:
        -   Project title
        -   Project summary (maximum 250 words)
-   **Career and Background Statement** (maximum 350 words)
    -   Describes:
        -   Academic and research background
        -   Qualifications for carrying out the proposed project
        -   Career goals and future plans
-   **Research Proposal** (maximum 4 pages)
    -   Includes:
        -   Introduction and background
        -   Research methods and approach
        -   Expected outcomes
        -   Significance and impact of the research
-   **Bibliography**
-   **NIH Biosketch** (maximum 5 pages)
-   **Three Letters of Recommendation**
-   **Proposed Budget and Budget Justification**
    -   Prepared using the **NIH G300 budget format**

#### What Makes a Competitive Application?

Strong applicants typically demonstrate:

-   A distinguished record of postdoctoral research accomplishments.
-   Innovative neuroscience research with the potential for significant scientific impact.
-   Clear readiness to transition to an independent faculty position.
-   A compelling research vision and a well-developed plan for establishing an independent laboratory.
-   Strong support from mentors and institutional leadership.

This fellowship is typically run in early Fall.
