# MGT 405 — Managerial Economics

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA26

## What this course covers

This course introduces the use of microeconomics in managerial decision-making,
examining how market structure, competitive and regulatory constraints, and the
structure of information affect a firm's optimal strategy. Topics include supply and
demand analysis, price discrimination, contractual relationships, and asymmetric
information, with an emphasis on marginal analysis. The course uses lectures, case
discussions, in-class work sessions, and homework to apply economic principles to
business problems.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- applying marginal analysis to profit maximization
- using price elasticity to guide pricing decisions
- designing pricing strategies such as versioning, bundling, and non-linear pricing
- deriving optimal supply decisions for competitive firms
- analyzing the effects of regulations on market behavior
- developing strategic responses to market entry threats
- designing incentive schemes to address conflicts of interest

## Topics covered

supply and demand analysis, marginal analysis, price elasticity and pricing decisions, price discrimination, versioning, bundling, and non-linear pricing, production inputs and costs, competitive markets and market structure, regulation and market welfare, market entry and strategic threats, asymmetric information and incentive design.

DESCRIPTION
This course is an introduction to the use of microeconomics in managerial decision-making. We examine
how the optimal strategy of a firm is impacted by the structure of the market, the competitive and
regulatory constraints, and the structure of information. Topics include supply and demand analysis, price
discrimination techniques, contractual relationships, and asymmetric information. Throughout we highlight
the importance of marginal analysis in decisions.
All the sub-fields of management education (marketing, finance, organizational behavior, operations,
information systems, accounting, and strategy) deal in some way with how businesses and other
organizations work to allocate scarce resources among competing needs. Because of this, economics
can serve as a unifying framework. I will show you connections between economics and many of the
other courses you will take as part of your MBA.
OBJECTIVES
The basic objective of this course is to familiarize you with the approach, language, and techniques of
managerial economics. After this course, you will understand economic principles sufficiently to apply
them to real world issues and make real world decisions. You should expect to leave the course with a
firm grip on how economists think about business problems, and the ability to apply tools from the course
to a variety of problems.
To sum up, the objective of this course is to teach students to apply economic principles to solving
business problems.
At the close of Managerial Economics you will be able to:
• Learn marginal analysis and how it can be applied for profit maximization
• Understand consumer demand and how to use price elasticity to guide pricing decisions
• Use versioning, bundling and non-linear pricing as strategies to increase profits
• Understand and compare different types of inputs to production and production costs.
• Derive optimal supply recommendations for firms in competitive markets
• Recognize the effects of regulations on market behavior and welfare
• Apply short-term and long-term strategic thinking for pricing and capacity decisions
• Be cognizant of threats to market entry and how to address them
• Design effective incentive schemes considering conflicts of interest

Group Homewwork Assignments (gHW)
You will be asked to prepare these assignments with your study group and hand in one written solution for
each group.
a. There will be 3 group cases.
b. Guidelines for the case write-ups will be provided on canvas.
c. In addition to submitting the group homework, we will have some teams present their proposed case
analyses to the class, and some teams lead the discussion of the proposed solutions.
d. The schedule of presentations will be discussed during Week 1.
Individual Homewwork Assignments (iHW)
These are problem sets, which are to be done individually or in groups, but you must hand in your own
individual assignment. They won’t be graded for correctness. Full points can be attained if you sincerely
attempt to answer each question, even if you make mistakes. Solutions will then be posted so that you
can check your work. The idea is that these will help you get practice with the material.
Reading Questions
There will be reading assignments for each session. The list of questions that you are asked to prepare
for each session will be posted on Canvas, together with the specific required and suggested readings.
It is essential that you prepare the readings before class. During class we will discuss your answers to the
questions asked about the readings and you will be expected to discuss with your fellow classmates.
