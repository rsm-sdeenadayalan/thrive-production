# MGT 495 — Topics in Finance: International Finance

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SP26, SU26

## What this course covers

This course introduces machine learning and AI methodologies for financial applications,
focusing on alternative and unstructured data sources. Students learn to apply
econometric and ML tools to extract patterns that inform portfolio choices, systematic
strategies, and trading signals. The course emphasizes hands-on programming, industry
guest speakers, and a group project mimicking quant research pods.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- utilizing capital budgeting techniques in an international setting
- assessing transaction, translation, and competitive currency risks
- designing hedging strategies with forwards, options, and swaps
- managing political risk in cross-border transactions
- explaining interactions between fiscal policy, monetary policy, trade, and currency fluctuations
- analyzing international finance cases
- analyzing alternative data
- applying machine learning to financial data
- building trading signals
- designing quantitative research projects

## Topics covered

exchange rates, international capital budgeting, political risk, currency risk, derivatives, hedging strategies, trade and monetary policy, international portfolio theory, cross-border valuation, cost of capital, alternative data, unstructured data.

## Tools used

excel, canvas, zoom, python.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on class participation (10%), two group assignments (60%), and one group case submission (30%).

## Workload

Heavy. Grading is based on a group project (50%), a project presentation (25%), and class participation (25%).

AI USAGE
The use of AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, etc. is encouraged. AI has the potential to
streamline and augment your work, but you should use AI with the following caveats:
1. AI can only augment your work, not replace it. For instance, AI can improve the
quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of
complex ideas, and test your arguments for logical inconsistencies. AI can also
stimulate new ideas or refine your original ideas. However, you cannot use
Generative AI tools to create content that you simply put your name on. This
constitutes an academic integrity violation.
2. If you use Generative AI for an assignment, be transparent about how it was
used in a footnote or citation.
3. Large language models have issues with hallucinations and inaccuracies (e.g.,
generating fake citations, failing logical arguments, misrepresenting sources,
etc.). You are fully responsible for the content you submit and will be graded
accordingly

DESCRIPTION
This course introduces students to cutting-edge Machine Learning and AI methodologies in
financial applications. Beginning with an overview of alternative data, such as text data,
students will explore techniques for analyzing these data and will be exposed to the latest
methodologies in the field. The course emphasizes how such data and methods can improve
financial decision-making, particularly in shaping portfolio choices, systematic strategies, and
trading signals.

OBJECTIVES
The course aims to provide students with an overview of the latest advances in Machine
Learning and AI tools used for analyzing financial markets and making financial decisions.
Additionally, it equips students with a toolkit to analyze these datasets and stay up to date with
the latest research as it becomes available. The ultimate goal of the course is to give students
insights on how alternative data and AI tools can be used by industry players — such as asset
managers, investors, trading firms — to improve their decision-making process. As such, the
course will emphasize practical financial applications through hands-on programming and data
analysis. Guest speakers from industry will share practical perspectives on how research
developments are being applied in real-world financial settings. Finally, students will have the
opportunity to apply the techniques covered during the course of their group project.

STRUCTURE AND TOPICS
We begin by reviewing new data sources and learning how they are mapped into the statistical
domain. Next, we learn to apply the latest econometric and machine learning tools to extract
statistical information and patterns that serve as key inputs for financial decision-making. The
emphasis is on the underlying financial problems and how these are shaped by the latest
advancements in AI. Finally, we investigate issues that may arise from an incorrect use of the
methodologies we learn during the course.

SCHEDULE
1.​ Introduction to alternative and unstructured data sources;
2.​ Structure and modeling of alternative data;
3.​ AI methodologies to analyze alternative data;
4.​ Review of the latest research in finance;
5.​ Financial applications.

GRADING AND EVALUATION
●​

Group Project (50%) : students are required to design, execute, and craft a research idea

which is rooted around the topics of the course. The project must be done in group, and
the purpose is to mimic how quant research pods work in quantitative investment firms.
●​ Project Presentation (25%): students will present (pitch) their research idea to the
classroom. The object should be to convince the audience of the quality of their idea.
●​ Class Participation (25%): actively engage and following classes.
