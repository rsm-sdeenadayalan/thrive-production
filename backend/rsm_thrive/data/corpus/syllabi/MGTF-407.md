# MGTF 407 — Valuation in Corporate Finance

Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course focuses on valuing assets, from an entire firm to a single business project,
and understanding the drivers of that value. It covers the main valuation approaches in
use today, including DCF, WACC/APV, relative valuation, and real options, with extensive
practice via case studies and lectures.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- calculating valuation-appropriate cash flows
- calculating appropriate discount rates
- choosing the appropriate valuation model for a firm or project
- analyzing how growth and capital structure affect valuation
- assessing uncertainty in estimated value
- communicating valuation analysis clearly and succinctly

## Topics covered

dcf basics, cash flow estimation, discount rates, wacc and apv, capital structure, terminal value, relative valuation, real options valuation, valuation drivers.

## Prerequisites

Master of Finance Core courses or MGT 408 (Finance Core)

## How it is graded

Course grade is based on case reports (20%), case presentations (30%), a real-world valuation case report (5%), participation (5%), and a final exam (40%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes six case reports, two case presentations, a real-world valuation case report, participation, and a final exam.

DESCRIPTION
Estimating the financial value of a project or asset is an essential part of the investment decision process. This
course focuses on valuing assets - from an entire firm to a single business project – and understanding the
drivers of that value.
We will cover the main valuation approaches in use today, and provide extensive practice via case studies, as
well as discussing the underlying conceptual and practical issues in lectures. The course has the secondary goal
of deepening your knowledge of other important areas of corporate finance, such as capital structure, and will
equip you with skills and knowledge that every finance practitioner should have.
Prerequisites: Master of Finance Core courses or MGT 408 (Finance Core)

Calculate valuation-appropriate cash flows
Calculate an appropriate discount rate for the cash flows, considering risk characteristics and firm/project

approaches, multiples, and real option valuation)
Understand the drivers and the uncertainty of the estimated value

Case reports (x6): This should have a maximum length of 3 pages of text with an additional 3 pages of exhibits
(for a maximum of 6 pages). The case report should use reasonable fonts and margins. Senior management is
the intended audience. Finally, it is not necessary to summarize the case situation in the report. Each team may
choose to “skip” a case report for 1 case, although team members must still read the case and participate
actively in class discussion. Teams that provide 6 case reports will have their lowest case grade dropped – but I
strongly recommend that you do all of the cases.
Real-world valuation case report (x1): for this task you will value a firm without a case to help you. Prepare a
report in the same style as the case reports. The firm will be announced early in the quarter. You cannot skip this
report. This is group/team work.
Case presentation (x2): these require your team to prepare approximately 10 slides on the case for a 15 minute
presentation (not counting question time). Do not summarize the case facts: I may ask one or two random
students in groups that are not presenting to summarize the case situation. Each group will present twice in the
semester (subject to class size considerations), once in class and once out of class (to one of the TAs).
Note that the evaluation of this is necessarily subjective, and both I and the TAs will be evaluating both
content and presentation effectiveness.
Class and Group Participation: You should prepare for class, and constructively participate in class discussions
and in working through assignments in your groups. I will “cold call” in class, and your responses will affect your
class participation grade. Also, peer evaluations conducted at the end of the quarter will significantly
determine your participation grade.
