# MGT 493R — New Venture Finance

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA26

## What this course covers

This course helps managers make better investment and financing decisions in
entrepreneurial settings, covering the full lifecycle of a new venture from deal
sourcing and evaluation to term sheets, fund performance, and exits. It uses a hybrid
model combining asynchronous online lectures and readings with synchronous, interactive
case discussions and exercises.

- evaluating startup investment opportunities

venture capital overview, deal sourcing and evaluation, venture capital securities, term sheets, startup valuation, deal negotiation, alternative funding sources, gp-lp relationships, fund performance measurement, exits.

DESCRIPTION
This course is designed to help managers make better investment and financing decisions in entrepreneurial settings.
Aimed at students interested in working as either entrepreneurs or investors, the class will explore topics from both
perspectives. The curriculum focuses on the intersection of entrepreneurship and finance, with a primary emphasis on the
venture capital process. We will cover the full lifecycle of a new venture, from sourcing and evaluating deals to structuring
term sheets, measuring fund performance, and planning for a successful exit. The course uses a hybrid instructional
model, combining asynchronous online lectures and readings with synchronous, interactive case discussions and
exercises.

OBJECTIVES
At the close of this course, you will be able to:
●

Analyze investment opportunities by evaluating the four key areas of a startup: the founding team, market opportunity,
product, and early traction.

●

Calculate startup valuations using the VC Method, working backward from a projected exit value while accounting for target
returns and future dilution.

●

Structure and negotiate venture financing deals by interpreting the key economic and control terms in term sheets, such as
convertible preferred stock, anti-dilution clauses, and protective provisions.

●

Evaluate the structure, economics, and performance of venture capital funds from the perspective of both a General Partner
(GP) and a Limited Partner (LP).

●

Compare and contrast various funding sources for new ventures, including venture capital, angel investing, crowdfunding,
and venture debt.

●

Develop an optimal exit strategy for a startup by evaluating the trade-offs between the two primary exit avenues: an Initial
Public Offering (IPO) and an acquisition.
