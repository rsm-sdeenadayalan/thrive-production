# MGTP 435 — Accounting for Mergers and Acquisitions

Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

Introduces the full lifecycle of mergers and acquisitions, from strategy and valuation
to financing, structuring, due diligence, and accounting under ASC 805. Students learn
the acquisition method for business combinations, including goodwill, contingent
consideration, and tax implications, using CPA exam materials and professional roadmaps.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing m&a transactions
- applying the acquisition method to business combinations
- valuing acquired assets and liabilities
- structuring deals across legal, accounting, and tax dimensions
- evaluating tax consequences of acquisitions
- conducting due diligence
- negotiating definitive documents
- preparing for cpa exam far and bar sections

## Topics covered

mergers and acquisitions strategy, business combinations accounting (asc 805), acquisition method, valuation approaches, equity method of accounting, financing structures, transaction structuring, tax aspects of m&a, due diligence, definitive agreements.

## Tools used

mcgraw hill connect, wiley cpa exam materials, deloitte business combinations roadmap, deloitte income tax roadmap, powerpoint.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Final grade is 20% participation, 20% discussion questions, 20% quizzes, 20% group project, and 20% final exam.

COURSE DESCRIPTION: MGTP 435: Accounting for Mergers and Acquisitions
This class introduces students to the world of mergers and acquisitions and their reporting. M&A specialists are at the center of balancing the
demands of executing corporate strategy within the constraints of finance.
Career opportunities in M&A can be found in one of several areas summarized as follows:
•
•
•

Professional service firms (e.g., legal, accounting, tax advisory, diligence, and structuring)
Industry (e.g., corporate development teams)
Investment banks and private equity firms

Senior corporate leadership and development officers work closely across the business enterprise to analyze potential transactions, negotiate
deals and create a plan to deliver the synergies hoped for in every transaction. As noted above, these leaders must interact with third-party
advisors, including but not limited to legal, finance, accounting, tax, and a broad array of business and investment advisors.
WHAT WILL WE BE STUDYING
This class is for the serious student who wants to focus on entering a field where you might be involved in mergers and acquisitions. We will
focus on the entire lifecycle of mergers and acquisitions transactions, from strategy to valuation and financing, to deal due diligence and
definitive document negotiation, to structuring, and ultimately to the accounting for mergers and acquisition transactions.
To evaluate a deal, you must have at least a basic understanding of the reporting of business combinations, one of the most complex areas of
financial reporting. However, to account for a complex transaction, you much understand why and how entities engage in mergers and

acquisitions. You will be required to bring your knowledge from accounting, economics, finance, business law, and even taxes to understand
better key risks and legal consequences related to structuring a business combination. Moreover, we will have fun along the way!!
Our focus will be on Accounting Standards Codification (ASC 805) Business Combinations. We will also deep dive into the strategy, valuation,
financing, structuring and deal execution aspects of M&A transactions. You will be introduced to the key steps involved in the reporting of a

Although the above diagram may appear to be simple, there are numerous complexities involved in each of the steps. For example,
understanding entity valuations, tax implications, using different forms of consideration (e.g., debt, equity, share-based payments), treatment of
assets acquired including IPR&D, reserves, contingencies (e.g., earnouts), and the impact of indemnifications provided by the parties to the
transaction make the accounting for these transactions extremely difficult.
COURSE OUTCOMES
Accounting for business combinations is tested in the Financial Accounting Reporting (FAR) and (BAR) section of the Certified Public Accounting
examination.
Therefore, I will test your knowledge and understanding by using materials taken from or substantially inspired by the CPA examination.
Our two primary textbooks will be:
(1) To develop a holistic understanding of the M&A process: The Art of M&A, A Merger, Acquisition and Buy-out Guide, 6th Edition.
(2) To develop an understanding of the basic accounting for business combinations: Advanced Accounting, 15th Edition.
