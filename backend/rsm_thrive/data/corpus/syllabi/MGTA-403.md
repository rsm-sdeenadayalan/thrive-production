# MGTA 403 — AI-Assisted Math & Programming for Business Analytics

**2 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered SU26

## What this course covers

An intensive summer gateway course that builds math, statistics, and programming
foundations for the MSBA in a Generative AI era. Students implement core concepts (mean,
variance, A/B testing, clustering) from scratch in Python without external libraries,
practice test-driven development, and learn to collaborate with AI tools on business
problems.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- writing Python from first principles
- implementing statistical concepts in code
- test-driven development
- guiding GenAI tools for coding
- explaining code and math orally

## Topics covered

Python programming fundamentals, probability and statistics, test-driven development, A/B testing, clustering and segmentation, Generative AI collaboration, agentic engineering.

## Tools used

Python, GitHub, Claude Code, DataCamp.

## Prerequisites

Assigned DataCamp coursework, self-paced online math & stats bootcamp, recommended OpenIntro Statistics review (Ch. 1-5).

## How it is graded

Pass/fail (S/U) based on written and oral final exam; individual assignments and participation count significantly.

## Workload

Heavy. Compressed two-week format: daily sessions, skills test, programming cases, probability problem sets, written and oral final exam.

DESCRIPTION
This course is intended as a gateway to mastering analytics and analytical thinking in a rapidly developing
Generative AI era. Our goal is to help you build the confidence, mindset, and skills needed to tackle complex
business problems using data, mathematics, programming, and Generative AI.
Unlike traditional math or coding classes, this course doesn't focus on memorizing formulas or syntax. Instead, we
use Python, without any external libraries, to bring core math and statistics concepts to life. You'll learn how
analytics works under the hood, and why that matters for business decision-making.
OBJECTIVES
In this course ...
• You'll code to learn: Every concept, from mean and variance to A/B testing and clustering, is
something you'll implement yourself.
• You'll learn to think like an Agentic Engineer: We'll teach you to break down problems, write test
cases, and build business solutions using a test-driven development (TDD) mindset.
• You'll collaborate with AI: Rather than using AI for cognitive offloading, you'll learn how to guide AI
effectively as a thought partner.
• You'll solve real problems: Every concept is tied to a business context: Marketing analytics, customer
segmentation, inventory decisions, and more.
By the end of this course, you won't just be better at coding or statistics; you'll be better at thinking clearly, solving
problems creatively, and working with AI to build solutions that matter in the business world. Let's start building!

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) policy
You will use Generative AI tools in this class. Learning to leverage Generative AI in your work is critical. These
tools have considerable potential to streamline and augment our work and you should use them with the following
caveats and conditions:
• GenAI tools should enhance your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models can improve
the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex ideas or terms,
generate code, etc. These tools can help you come up with new ideas, overcome writer’s block, or refine
your original ideas. However, you should not use GenAI tools to generate content that you simply put
your name on. This constitutes an academic integrity violation, much like taking another individual’s work
and misrepresenting it as your own.
• We expect all students to use GenAI for class preparation, help with assignments, etc. However, we
require that you fully understand and can explain, in-person or on video, all details of every line of code
and every mathematical calculation that you submit as your own work.
• Large language models have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy (e.g., generating fake
Python packages, failing logical arguments, misrepresenting sources, etc.). You are fully responsible for
all content you submit in your assignments and will be graded accordingly.
