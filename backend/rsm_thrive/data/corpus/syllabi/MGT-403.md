# MGT 403 — Business Analytics for Managers

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA25, FA26

## What this course covers

This course provides a foundation in business analytics for students of all backgrounds,
covering statistics and uncertainty, decision analysis, and optimization. Students
process data, build mathematical models to improve decision making, and use statistics
to gain business insights. The course uses the Radiant tool, a no-code data processing
and visualization tool within the R environment.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- exploring and visualizing data
- building decision models under uncertainty
- conducting hypothesis tests
- interpreting regression relationships
- using simulation to predict complex outcomes
- formulating and solving optimization models
- communicating analytical findings in managerial memos
- making evidence-based business decisions
- reading and interpreting business analytics reports
- producing data visualizations using radiant

## Topics covered

decision analysis, sensitivity analysis, probability distributions, conditional probability, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, regression analysis, simulation, linear optimization, data visualization, probability.

## Tools used

radiant, r, canvas.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

The syllabus lists weights of 40% for assignments, 25% for the final exam, 20% for the midterm project and presentation, 15% for individual homework assignments, and 15% for group case assignments.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes seven weekly assignments, six group case memos, a take-home midterm project with presentation, a final exam, and class participation.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
In today’s increasingly digital economy, the availability of data is widespread and business
success can depend on smart, capable, and clever use of this data. Business analytics now
drives ticket pricing, retail assortments, movie recommendations, digital marketing
campaigns, healthcare scheduling, crime fighting, social media investments, capacity
decisions and more. Today’s companies generate an immense volume of transactional data,
capturing trillions of bytes of information about their customers, suppliers, and operations.
Millions of networked sensors are also embedded in the physical world in devices such as
computers, mobile phones, smart energy meters, automobiles and industrial machines.
Further, in a digitized world, consumers communicate, browse, buy, share, and search,
creating extensive trails of valuable data.
Hence, analytics — the use of data with statistical and quantitative models to make better,
data-driven business decisions — has become important to gain competitive advantages.
With all of these different sources of data ranging from online sensors, email campaigns, to
physical sensors, business analytics is pervasive in any size business.
This importance of analytics means that all managers should be comfortable discussing,
assessing, and effectively conducting their own analyses. There is a significant shortage
(and significant demand!) of business analysts and project managers who are adept at
effectively utilizing analytics to create substantial value to an organization by solving
impactful business problems.
This course introduces students to the foundations of business analytics. It teaches
managers how to be comfortable exploring their data, how to create and use models to
make better decisions in complex settings, and how to use statistics to better understand
their business setting. Prior analytical experience is not a prerequisite for this course, we

LEARNING OBJECTIVES
There are three main Units we cover in this course. Each Unit teaches you tools to answer
key data- driven and quantitative questions. Each of the three units center around a central
theme of addressing and studying uncertainty. Data and business analytics allows us to
better understand and deal with uncertainty.
Unit 1: Randomness and Uncertainty
•
Decisions when facing uncertain and probabilistic (“random”) future outcomes.
•
Test the important of our assumptions in our models (“sensitivity analysis”)
•
Modeling randomness to understand what risks and potential outcomes you face.
•
Understanding why data gives us confidence and interpreting such confidence.
•
Making evidence-based business decisions (“hypothesis testing”).
•
Is the evidence sufficient to convince us? Or is it just noise? [Key business question.]
Unit 2: Prediction and modeling relationships
Unit 1 primarily focuses on studying individual random variables. Unit 2 expands our tools
to model relationships between multiple variables.
•
Use data to create predictions for the future (“regression”).
•
Use regression to interpret the relationships between data.
o
Understand the limitations of regression and how to address those
limitations.
o
Understand the limitations of causality interpretation.
•
Use simulation to predict complex future outcomes (such as long-tail insurance
risks).
Unit 3: Optimization
In optimization, we transition from modeling random outcomes to optimizing decisions,
such as how many resources to allocate to different sectors or investments. Each use of
resources has tradeoffs, which we learn to use tools to quantitatively model and consider.
•
Simulating possible futures, and using this simulation to make the optimal decision.
•
Formalizing the resource tradeoffs you face in a business into an optimization
model.
•
Using optimization model to understand the best use of many scarce resources,
when faced with many tradeoffs.
All of these are KEY ways businesses and managers must think about their problems. This
course will give you ample experience in these fundamental ways of quantitative thinking.

RADIANT TOOL
The central tool we use in this course to perform analysis is Radiant.
The modern use of statistics and analytics heavily relies on statistical software to conduct
analyses. “R” is one of the most famous and is heavily used throughout all companies.
However, using R requires being comfortable with coding first.
To best teach analytical thinking, we use Radiant to give you the power of exploring your
data, visualizing it, and seeing analysis without needing to code. Radiant is built on-top of R,
meaning it is free and it can be used at any business that can use R — which again, almost
all modern analytics teams support the use of R.
Lastly and most importantly, Radiant is a valuable resume piece. This course will not only
give you comfort with “data visualization”, “business analytics”, “regression analysis”, and
“linear optimization” — you will also have experience with “R statistical analysis (via the
Radiant interface)”.

AI + CHATGPT POLICY
ChatGPT and large-language AI models have become popular in the past couple years. They
can answer simple statistical questions. Modern statistical analysts and programmers can

If you find it helpful, you are welcome to try to use ChatGPT to offer advice or additional
support on your homework, cases, and midterm projects. However, you must ultimately do

DESCRIPTION
This course provides a foundation for business analytics, and is intended for students of all backgrounds.
Students will gain experience processing data, constructing mathematical models to improve decision
making, and using statistics to gain business insights. Data analytical fluency has become an essential
skill for any manager. As such, we will work extremely hard to ensure students have all the support and
resources they need to succeed in this course. This course will also serve as a bridge to higher level
analytics courses within The Rady School of Management.
The course will be comprised of three units: Statistic & Uncertainty, Decision Analysis, and Optimization.
The first unit will focus on data visualization and probability, spending a significant amount of time on
regression analysis. The second unit will carry forward the notion of uncertainty presented in the first unit,
and focus on modeling techniques for using data to make better decisions under uncertainty. We will
emphasize interpretating model results and will spend two weeks working on simulation approaches. The
final unit will introduce optimization models, with the objective of providing an understanding of the
capabilities of optimization and recognizing opportunities to deploy such models.
To allow students to focus on the key concepts of the course, the course is built around the Radiant Tool.
Radiant is a data processing and visualization tool that does not require coding, while operating within the
R environment (which is free to use).
