# MGTF 495 — Special Topics: Fixed Income

Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course follows the life of an actual loan as it migrates from investment grade to
junk, undergoes restructuring, and is transformed through bankruptcy, using the
narrative to illuminate fixed income categories, capital structure, and valuation.
Students analyze and value corporate, sovereign, and municipal debt, calculate duration,
convexity, and credit spreads, and build fixed income portfolios using Bloomberg
terminal capabilities. The course includes guest speakers, case studies, and a hands-on
Bloomberg lab.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing alternative data
- applying machine learning to financial data
- building trading signals
- designing quantitative research projects
- presenting research ideas
- using ai for portfolio decisions
- evaluating financial research
- evaluating blockchain and ai use cases in finance
- distinguishing fads from foundational projects
- analyzing product and go-to-market considerations

## Topics covered

alternative data, unstructured data, data modeling, ai methodologies, machine learning, econometrics, financial research, financial applications, portfolio choices, systematic strategies, trading signals, blockchain.

## Tools used

python, bloomberg terminal, canvas.

DESCRIPTION
This course introduces students to cutting-edge data sources and AI methodologies in financial
applications. Beginning with an overview of alternative data, such as price charts, students will
explore techniques for analyzing these data and will be exposed to the latest methodologies in
the field. The course emphasizes how such data and methods can improve financial
decision-making, particularly in shaping portfolio choices, systematic strategies, and trading
signals.

OBJECTIVES
The course aims to provide students with an overview of the latest advances in alternative data
sources and the AI tools used for analyzing financial markets and making financial decisions.
Additionally, it equips students with a toolkit to analyze these datasets and stay up to date with
the latest research as it becomes available. The ultimate goal of the course is to give students
insights on how alternative data and AI tools can be used by industry players — such as asset
managers, investors, trading firms — to improve their decision-making process. As such, the
course will emphasize practical financial applications through hands-on programming and data
analysis. Guest speakers from industry will share practical perspectives on how research
developments are being applied in real-world financial settings. Finally, students will have the
opportunity to apply the techniques covered during the course of their group project.

STRUCTURE AND TOPICS
We begin by reviewing new data sources and learning how they are mapped into the statistical
domain. Next, we learn to apply the latest econometric and machine learning tools to extract
statistical information and patterns that serve as key inputs for financial decision-making. The
emphasis is on the underlying financial problems and how these are shaped by the latest
advancements in AI. Finally, we investigate issues that may arise from an incorrect use of the
methodologies we learn during the course.

SCHEDULE
1.​ Introduction to alternative and unstructured data sources;
2.​ Structure and modeling of alternative data;
3.​ AI methodologies to analyze alternative data;
4.​ Review of the latest research in finance;
5.​ Financial applications.

DESCRIPTION

become a ubiquitous technology across consumer and enterprise use-cases within the next few years.

expectations remain high.

class provides a core knowledge and understanding to go do that.

DESCRIPTION:
As a narrative to take us through the world of fixed income we are going to follow the life of an actual
loan made to a publicly traded corporation. This loan originated as an unsecured, investment grade bond.
When it started out the yield was low and the prospect for full repayment was excellent. Due to
unforeseen circumstances, the bond migrated to junk. Junk bonds are quite different from investment
grade bonds – and we will learn how. Conditions for the issuer worsened and the loan transformed again.
This time it was exchanged for secured, 2nd lien bonds (also known as an out-of-court restructuring).
Eventually, with much kicking and screaming, this loan was transformed by the bankruptcy court into the
present form - a diminished package of investment grade bonds, warrants and equity. The original loan
lives on in this new form.
Following the life, death and resurrection of this actual loan is a novel way to shed light on fixed income
categories, capital structure elements and the dark underside of capitalism.
