# MGT 477R — Consumer Behavior

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SP26, SP27

## What this course covers

This course provides frameworks, methodology, and knowledge to help students gain
customer insight and connect that insight to marketing strategy and implementation. It
covers qualitative methods, consumer culture, lifestyle, needs, customer journeys, and
innovation adoption, with a focus on designing and interpreting marketing research. The
course blends in-person sessions with online asynchronous materials, case discussions,
lectures, and group projects.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing consumer behavior
- designing marketing research
- interpreting research results
- applying behavioral insights to marketing strategy
- developing customer insight frameworks
- segmenting audiences
- crafting brand positioning

## Topics covered

customer insight, qualitative research, consumer culture, lifestyle and lifestage, consumer needs and behavior, customer journey, innovation adoption, marketing research, behavioral economics, brand loyalty.

## Tools used

canvas, generative ai.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on class participation (20% online, 10% in-person), individual assignments (15%, 2%, 8%), and group assignments (15% each, totaling 45%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes class participation (30%), three individual assignments (25%), and three group assignments (45%), with multiple case readings and group projects.

DESCRIPTION
Contemporary approaches to business emphasize the importance of adopting a
marketing focus (a.k.a. market demand focus) in which customer insight becomes a
leading driver of business strategy. The primary goal of this course is to provide the
framework, methodology and knowledge to help you effectively gain customer insight.
We train you to think about customer behavior in a disciplined, strategic manner, and
connect insight to marketing implementation. In addition, while we will not cover the
mathematical mechanics of marketing research, we focus on the high-level
understanding of marketing research to help you design and interpret research. You are
very likely to be either the designers or clients of marketing research. Being able to ask
the right questions and interpret the results in an insightful manner will greatly help the
usefulness and impact of marketing research.
This course will include 3 weeks of in-person sessions, plus online asynchronous
materials the rest of the time. Materials in-person and online will include case
discussions, lectures, individual and group activities to practice what we have learned.
Group projects will require coordination among teammates during the asynchronous
weeks. Every session requires student input, so it is important that you prepare for class
and participate in class activities as instructed.
In-person attendance is required for the in-person sessions. Online attendance is
required for the asynchronous material.

Generative AI use is allowed.
o For assignments that do not specify how AI can be used, you may use
Generative AI in any way you see fit, while keeping in mind that AI may not
give the best answers, and accepting AI answers without critical thinking
and editing may lead to worse work than not using AI. You will need to use
your judgment based on your learnings in the course to incorporate AI into
your work. To help everyone understand the role of AI in marketing
functions, I encourage you to disclose all usage of AI in your work (it will
not affect your grade either way, but it will be helpful learning for the class
in terms of what AI can do).
o Further, for some assignments, you will be explicitly asked to come up with
two sets of answers: (a) Answers that AI generates for you, and (b) using
non-AI methods to come up with answers; and you will be asked to
compare the difference between the two sets of answers.
