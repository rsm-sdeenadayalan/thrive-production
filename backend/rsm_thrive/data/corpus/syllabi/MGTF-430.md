# MGTF 430 — Asset Management

Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

A rigorous exploration of quantitative tools in asset management, bridging theory with
real-world investment practice to generate superior risk-adjusted returns, with a
primary focus on equities. The course involves considerable programming in Python and
covers signal generation, portfolio construction, performance measurement, and
contemporary topics such as AI in investing.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- implementing quantitative investment research in python
- generating equity investment signals
- evaluating investment performance
- presenting investment ideas and results
- navigating practical portfolio implementation issues
- using a comprehensive quantitative research framework

## Topics covered

quantitative asset management, equity investing, signal generation, alpha research, portfolio construction, performance measurement, performance attribution, ai in investing, active etfs, international investing.

## Tools used

python, jupyter notebook, anaconda, vs code, google sheets, llm tools.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Class attendance is 10%, homework 25%, midterm 25%, and final project 40%.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes five homework assignments, a closed-book midterm, and a final project with report, code, and presentation.

DESCRIPTION
This course will provide a rigorous exploration of the use of quantitative tools in asset management,
bridging theory with real world investment practice in the quest for generating superior risk-adjusted
returns. The primary focus will be equities. The course will have a considerable programming requirement
and will utilize Python.

ASSIGNMENTS
There will be 5 assignments. Collaboration with classmates is allowed. However, each student must

CLASS ATTENDANCE
In-person class attendance is mandatory. Participating in class, such as asking questions and speaking
up in class discussions are highly encouraged, however it is not mandatory for receiving a full ‘class
attendance’ grade. After the first class, there will be a simple question to answer at the end (with all
electronic devices put away) that will be easy to answer for those paying attention to the class.
Answering the question correctly will be needed for full credit for class attendance.
MIDTERM EXAM
There will be a midterm held during the 5th week. It will be closed-book, closed-notes, and in-person.
Computer usage will not be allowed for the exam.
FINAL PROJECT
Groups will be determined during the first two weeks of the course. Project subject proposals will be
due on the 5th week of the course. Students are expected to propose, test, and evaluate an original
quantitatively-based investment signal. Groups will need to submit their project report and python code
by the due date, and will present their project to the class the following week.

CHAT-GPT AND RELATED TOOLS
Students may use large language model (LLM) tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Perplexity,
etc.) to support their learning, homework assignments, and project work.
However, all submitted work must reflect the student’s own understanding, analysis, and effort. Reliance
on LLM-generated content in bulk or without critical engagement is not permitted. Moreover, certain
assignments may restrict or prohibit the use of LLMs, which will be clearly noted in the assignment
instructions when applicable.
Students are responsible for verifying the accuracy and appropriateness of any AI-generated material and
must be able to explain and justify all aspects of their work. This policy aligns with professional
expectations in the workplace, where AI tools typically may be used to assist with tasks, but individual
accountability, accuracy, and judgment remain essential.
