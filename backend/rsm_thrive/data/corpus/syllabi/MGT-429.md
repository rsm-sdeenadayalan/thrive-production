# MGT 429 — Topics in Corporate Governance – Leadership in a Crisis

Rady MBA · offered SU25 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course examines how organizations respond to highly visible crises, focusing on
leadership, transparency, and communication. Students work in teams to develop crisis
management plans, participate in simulated press conferences, and receive feedback on
their communication styles. Real-world cases such as Toyota, Wells Fargo, and Covid-19
illustrate effective and ineffective crisis leadership.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- developing crisis management plans
- delivering effective press conference presentations
- identifying stakeholders in a crisis
- balancing organizational and public interests
- providing leadership during crises
- preparing organizations for future crises
- improving public speaking under stress
- giving and receiving constructive feedback

## Topics covered

crisis management, leadership, stakeholder identification, crisis communication, media relations, organizational transparency, crisis preparedness, corporate governance, public interest, communication styles.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on individual scripts (30%), oral presentations (30%), a final crisis management plan (15%), and class participation (25%).

## Workload

Heavy. Multiple team presentations, individual scripts, and a final organizational crisis management plan.

DESCRIPTION
Class Size limited to 35.
A defining moment in the history of an organization is how it responds to a highly visible crisis that causes
real harm to individuals or communities. In today’s environment of 24/7 communication and global
transparency, the need to be prepared and to act quickly and effectively is essential. Organizations must
balance their own interests with the public interest. Crises involving Toyota, General Motors, United
Airlines, Wells Fargo, the Catholic Church, Michigan State University, and the Boy Scouts of America
provide vivid examples of the damage caused by the lack of effective leadership during a crisis.The
Covid-19 crisis provides a clear and compelling picture of the damage caused by the lack of transparent
leadership and the positive benefits of being honest and open.
This course will provide you with the opportunity to develop your own crisis management plan as part of a
team, to define leadership roles, and to make two videotaped presentations that will be evaluated by your
colleagues and me. This course will provide a vibrant, highly interactive learning environment designed to
help you experience what it’s like to successfully navigate through a complex and challenging simulated
crisis which has the potential to cause considerable loss of public image and trust. Ernest Rady will be a
visiting speaker during the third class.
A collateral benefit will be to identify your most effective communication styles. Students will expand their
existing repertoire of communications skills by watching peers demonstrate alternative styles. Student
teams will make brief presentations in class in a simulated press conference, assuring that they will
receive helpful feedback from classmates and from me on how to improve their content, style, and
credibility during a crisis presentation. By the conclusion of this course, you should have a clear idea of
your personal strengths and weaknesses and be able to build on your strengths. Students will also see
the value of working with a communications coach and in preparing far in advance. This class will provide
a safe environment for students who are seeking to improve their public speaking skills in stressful
situations, and deepen your confidence to assume leadership roles going forward.
OBJECTIVES
In this course, you will:
Learn how to identify the stakeholders involved in a crisis
Learn how to balance an organization’s interest with the public interest

Learn how to provide effective leadership and understand leadership roles in a crisis

Learn how to communicate effectively in a simulated press conference
Learn how to help your organization be better prepared to handle a future crisis
Develop enhanced confidence to assume leadership roles and seek leadership opportunities.
