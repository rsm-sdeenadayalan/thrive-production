# MGT 414R — Rady Action Project

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA26

## What this course covers

Students work in teams on a real consulting project sponsored by a business client,
applying management techniques to scope, analyze, and solve a business problem. The
course emphasizes client communication, team dynamics, and professional deliverables,
culminating in a final presentation and executive summary.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- clarifying client problems through interviews and questioning
- structuring business problems using frameworks like fishbone diagrams
- developing and evaluating solution options
- managing client expectations and communication
- delivering professional oral presentations
- writing concise executive summaries
- collaborating effectively in project teams
- applying consulting best practices

## Topics covered

management consulting, project scoping, client management, problem structuring, business analysis, solution development, business communication, team dynamics, presentation skills, executive summary writing.

## Tools used

canvas, zoom, powerpoint, chatgpt.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Graded on team deliverables (project charter, work plans, client status reports, executive summary), midterm and final presentations, discussion posts, case study, and professionalism/participation.

## Workload

Heavy. Estimated 11-12 hours per week across research, problem solving, status reports, work plans, presentations, and final deliverables.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course provides students with external consulting experience that utilizes and builds upon the
knowledge and skills acquired through work experience and in this course. The course enriches the Rady
experiential learning component and provides an interdisciplinary approach to project execution and
presentation. Projects are sponsored by and guided by clients from the business community.
OBJECTIVES
• Gain a practical understanding of management consulting via hands-on experience working with
a corporate client.
• Gain an in-depth understanding of advanced management techniques, including project and
client management, communication, and team dynamics.
• Perfect use of business communications skills, oral and written, throughout the quarter,
culminating in a final oral presentation and written executive summary.
• Learn best practices for aligning client expectations with realistic project goals and objectives
through effective communication and consensus building.
• Create and deliver a well-thought-out and professionally executed final deliverable to the client

PROJECTS
A Rady Action Project is sponsored by a company or organization. A typical project includes the following
activities and can encompass a business problem that an MBA student can solve.
1. Engaging with clients to clarify the problem – interviewing, questioning.
a. Context
b. Background
c. Symptoms
2. Structure the problem – fishbone diagram, etc.
a. Determine Cause
b. Revise if needed
3. Analyze research –think of all the possibilities
a. Develop solutions – how does it relate to the root cause
4. Select a solution
a. Feasibility
b. Best Fit
5. Ongoing communication with the client throughout the course, sharing your insights and
solutions, and revising as needed
Teams will consist of 3-6 students who receive project guidance from a project leader provided by the
company and mentoring from faculty. In addition, teams receive business communications coaching from
industry and academic professionals. Students must bring their business skills and professionalism to the
team to achieve project objectives. You represent the Rady brand alongside our sponsors, who are also
employers interested in hiring UCSD students.

PROFESSIONALISM
Professionalism is defined as participating actively in every aspect of your project and operating as an
effective team member and student. The professionalism grade is drawn from peer and faculty grading.
PEER GRADING (I)
At Weeks 5 and 10 of the quarter, each student will complete and submit a “peer evaluation” form on
Canvas. Students will be asked to provide feedback on the contributions of their teammates. Submitting
this form is considered part of your class participation grade.
FACULTY GRADING
Faculty will provide feedback about each team member’s contribution to the project.
The criteria are:
• Showing up for meetings on time and being prepared for discussion
• Demonstrating understanding of the project and your contribution to the research and outcome
• Demonstrating active leadership and participation in activities
• Communication and Professional Dress while engaging with the client
• Effective Listening and incorporating feedback given during your project development

AI STRATEGY FOR THE COURSE
The overarching philosophy is use AI as a junior analyst.
Enhance, Don’t Replace – treat AI as an input to group collaboration. It serves to augment, not
substitute, student critical thinking and faculty guidance.
Final Accountability- Final responsibility for all deliverables rests with the student. Think of AI as a
junior analyst working for you- you are the lead partner.
Ethical Guardrails -Transparency is paramount. Use of AI must be documented and cited in final reports.
RULES OF ENGAGEMENT (HOW SHOULD AI BE USED?)
Hallucination Check: Always verify outputs for errors or outdated information. AI is a reasoning engine,
not a fact- checker
Academic Integrity: Plagiarism rules apply. Copy-paste without critical synthesis is prohibited.
Proper Citation: Mandatory footnotes and bibliography entries for all AI-assisted work components.
Responsible Use: Ensure sponsor data is never fed into public models without explicit security
confirmation.
