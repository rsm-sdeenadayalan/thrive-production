# MGTA 460 — Business Analytics Project Management

**2 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered SP26

## What this course covers

Focuses on leading analytics projects: designing and planning work, coordinating
dispersed teams, managing senior-management expectations, and managing risk. Covers
stage-gate processes, critical path and PERT scheduling, resource allocation, real
options, modularity, and agile development, anchored by a team case building a Python
simulation of uncertain project durations.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- building project network representations
- Monte Carlo simulation of project durations in Python
- writing design documents
- managing analytics project risk

## Topics covered

project planning, stage-gate processes, critical path method, PERT and uncertainty, schedule crashing and resource allocation, risk management and real options, agile development.

## Tools used

COURSE DESCRIPTION:
Analytics projects, though distinct from general projects, also share a large number of
common challenges and pitfalls. Whether working for a startup designing and building the
next-generation analytics tools to generate business insights, or for an established firm
managing a small team executing an analytics project, competent project leadership
requires understanding how to design, plan, and allocate work; how to coordinate and
manage dispersed teams working on tightly interconnected pieces; how to manage senior
management expectations; and how motivate and maintain focus of your project team.
Good intentions cannot substitute for misdirected resource utilization and poor leadership,
as is demonstrated by the fact that fully one-third of all projects fail and another third end
up well below expectations.
While the general topic of project management is vast, this course has a narrower focus and
will aim to provide relevant technical and management skills along three dimensions: (i)
principles of designing and planning for analytics projects, (ii) coordinating and
integrating work and managing its execution, and (iii) managing risk and learning to
obtain desired outcomes through planned experimentation and exploration.

Dragonfly Case & Class Project
You are expected to work in teams of 3-4 people to complete this team assignment.
PART A (Due prior to the 3rd session)
Assume for PART A that the there is no uncertainty in the activity times given in the case
(i.e., use the most likely estimate), and also assume for PART A that the interdependence
among activities A4 and A9 does not exist (i.e., assume that while A9 depends on A4, A4
does not depend on A9). Each team will submit a single pdf file.
1) How could Bob Lake formally represent the evolution of the project, showing
interdependence and parallelism among activities? What is the completion time of
the project?
2) Should any activities be shortened to ensure timely completion of the proposal, and
if so, which ones?
Design Document (Due prior to the 3rd session)
Along with submitting PART A of dragonfly case, you will also be required to submit a onepage design document for what you will deliver in Part B. This design document should clarify
the goals, functional description, “interfaces,” and milestones/checkpoints. See below for
details on expectation from this design document. Each team will submit a single pdf file.

The design document, given the limited assignment scope, can be fairly short (even 1 or
2 pages). But at the minimum, it should address the following elements
1) Goals
Who will use it? Why will they use it? Provides a high-level description of its
purpose.
2) Description of functionality
What does the tool/application do? Enumerate and clarify the use cases.
3) Description of design and Interface.
What functionality “chunks” will you have? How does the user interface with the
tool? Do they type in values? Do they specify a csv file? Do they drag and drop to
create the network?
4) Checkpoints
What are the intermediate deliverables and when. Who is responsible?

PART B (Due one week from last class)
For part B, you will need to create a tool that can be used to analyze uncertain activity
durations in a project. To make things concrete, consider the Dragonfly case with
uncertainty (and possible feedback loops). As a manager you may be interested to know
the distribution of completion time (or costs), with or without crashing.
The deliverable for this part is a single python jupyter notebook that explicitly answers the
following specific Dragonfly questions. In addition, the notebook should be flexible enough
that I can upload a new csv data file with project information, and re-run the notebook to
get new expected project duration and the histograms for the project duration for the
newly uploaded project.
3) Now explicitly consider the uncertainty in activity durations, but assume that there
is no interdependence between A4 and A9. Create a histogram to help understand
the distribution of the project completion times. What is the expected completion
time of the project?
4) How does the histogram change because of the interdependence between A4 and
A9.
5) What is the expected cost of the proposal. Also, create a histogram to help
understand the distribution of the cost.
