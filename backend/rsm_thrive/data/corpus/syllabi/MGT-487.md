# MGT 487 — Valuation

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA26, SP27

## What this course covers

Valuation is the science and art of assigning a fair market value to an asset. The
course develops in-depth knowledge of the determinants of firm value and why short-term
stock price is likely to deviate from long-term value, covering discounted cash flow
valuation, equity risk models, relative valuation, and real options. It uses case
studies and spreadsheet models and ties valuation to the state of the macroeconomy.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- preparing pro forma financial statements
- managing growth
- calculating cash flow to equity, cash flow to the firm, and free cash flow
- evaluating risk characteristics and developing appropriate discount rates
- assessing capital structure decisions
- choosing the right valuation model for a particular firm
- appraising special situations such as start-ups and distressed firms
- building discounted cash flow and relative valuation models

## Topics covered

discounted cash flow valuation, equity risk models, relative valuation, real options, financial statement forecasting, cost of capital, capital structure, valuation of start-ups and young firms, valuation of distressed firms, pro forma financial statements.

DESCRIPTION
Valuation is the science and art of assigning a fair market value to an asset. Valuation skills are
indispensable in the financial toolset of entrepreneurs, managers, and investors. The simple
strategy of "buy low sell high" depends on a dependable estimate of value that serves as the
benchmark. Without the knowledge to establish value and assess risk, financial managers will
make suboptimal decisions.
In this course we will develop an in-depth knowledge of the determinants of firm value and the
reasons short-term stock price is likely to deviate from long-term value. We will discuss
discounted cash flow valuation, equity risk models, relative valuation, and real options. We will
build on the knowledge you already have about some of these topics but deal with the issues in
much greater depth and detail. We will spend a substantial part of the course on case studies
and spreadsheet models and tie valuation to the state of the macroeconomy.
Prerequisites: Core MBA courses: MGT 403, 404, 405, 406, 407, 408, 409, 410, 413, familiarity
with Microsoft Excel.

ASSIGNMENTS
Reading Assignments
Independent work.
The course integrates lectures, case study analysis, and financial modeling.
The Powerpoint slides and handouts contain the concepts and calculations, that you
need to know well for the quizzes and the team project. The spreadsheet models
illustrate the techniques we use in Valuation.
The case studies provide practical application of the material and useful guides for applying the
concepts we discuss. Please read the assigned cases carefully before each class. If you
are not familiar with the decision-making situation chances are, you will not enjoy and
understand the concepts that guide the reasoning.
The chapters in the book serve as background material for the lectures and case studies. I
would like you to consult them to become familiar with the terminology and additional insights to
the theory and techniques.
Team Projects
Collaborative work.
The course includes a team project. Teams may have up to five members. Please send me the
names of team members by the second session on October 8, 2025 via email.
The team project will involve creating a case study on valuing a publicly held company.
This project involves substantial work. The final presentation will describe the firm and its
activities, analyze past financial performance, estimate pro forma financial projections, include a
discounted cash flow and relative valuation and discuss whether the current stock price reflects
intrinsic value correctly.
Team presentations take place at the last session on December 3, 2025.
I recommend that teams choose a single-segment company: a firm whose operations are
limited to a single industry. If a team needs help choosing I am ready to recommend
companies. Teams will present their work and submit a detailed report of their analysis in the
