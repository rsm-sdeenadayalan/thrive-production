# MGT 431R — Power and Politics

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered WI26, WI27

## What this course covers

This course examines organizational power and politics, teaching students to understand
power dynamics, develop influence skills, and build a personal path to power. It uses
readings, case discussions, in-class exercises, reflection papers, and a final strategic
plan, with a hybrid format of video lectures, Canvas discussions, and in-person
sessions.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing power dynamics in organizations
- assessing sources of power and influence
- applying persuasion principles
- conducting informational interviews
- developing a personal strategic plan for power
- reflecting on ethical dilemmas
- building political intelligence

## Topics covered

power dynamics, sources of power, principles of influence, persuasion, organizational politics, ethical dilemmas of power, network building, career strategy.

## Tools used

canvas.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Course grade is 37% participation (34% class discussion, 3% surveys), 18% reflection papers, and 45% final paper.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes weekly discussion participation, short reflection papers, and a 12-page final paper with an informational interview.

DESCRIPTION
In any organization, it is not enough to have a good idea or a brilliant plan; you need the
cooperation and support of others to get things done. Organizations are fundamentally political
entities, and power and influence are key mechanisms by which things are accomplished. This
course will encourage you to think about and develop your own personal path to power—which
can, of course, include a path away from power. Together we will work to build your political
intelligence. Power is a topic that can make people uncomfortable, but it is a critical part of
organizational life, and this course will provide a safe space to work through this discomfort.
INSTRUCTIONAL METHODS
The core of this course will be 1) discussion of the materials and 2) exercises designed to help
you gain and practice skills. Any lecturing I do will be geared towards further illuminating
concepts and issues and guiding discussion. As this is a hybrid course, most weeks you will
watch my lectures on video and have discussion on Canvas, but other weeks, class will be in
person. During these in-person weeks, you will also take part in exercises.
Throughout this course, we will use multiple methodologies:
• Analysis and discussion of articles and cases. You will be expected to share your
relevant management and leadership experiences.
• In-class exercises to illustrate and practice course concepts.
• Assignments to practice and apply course concepts. You will be learning concrete skills
throughout this class. You will regularly be asked to practice these skills outside the
classroom and then discuss your experience. You will also write reflection papers to help
you synthesize the various topics covered in class and apply them to your own career, in
terms of both previous experiences and anticipated issues in the future.
OBJECTIVES
The overall course objective is for you to gain knowledge, skills, and strategies related to power
that will help you become highly effective and impactful in your career. By the end of this
course, you will:

Class Participation (37%)
Your course participation grade will be based on 1) your timely completion of course-related
activities outside of class (i.e., surveys; 3% of grade), and 2) your contributions to class
discussion (34% of grade). During asynchronous weeks, class discussion will occur on Canvas
discussion boards. Full participation for one of these weeks will involve both responding to the
questions posted and engaging with your classmates’ posts (e.g., answering questions they
raise, offering your perspective, providing relevant information). The quality of your posts is
more important than the quantity.
During in-person weeks, class discussion will occur in person. It is essential that you come to
each in-person class prepared, meaning you have completed all assignments (i.e., readings,
surveys, reflection papers) for that week. Coming to class unprepared will interfere with your
ability to participate in discussions and learn from the course in general. Similar to
asynchronous weeks, your goal during in-class discussions should be to contribute high-quality
comments, not a high quantity of comments. This means you must listen carefully to the

Reflection Papers (18%)
A reflection paper is designed to encourage you to relate the class material to your own career.
In writing these papers, do not repeat what we have all read or discussed during class. Rather,
use the available space (no more than 2 pages, double-spaced, 1-inch margins) to reflect on
how the material applies to you, following the prompt provided. Please post each paper on
Canvas by the due date.
The grading scheme is designed so that almost all reflection papers will receive a 100, if they
are submitted on time and respond to the prompt provided. Such a grade indicates you are
putting the expected thought and effort into these papers. For this reason, these papers will
often not be given specific feedback. I am always available if you want to talk about your
reflection paper content or grade.
Final Paper (45%)
The final paper is designed to help you strategize for the next few years of your career (and
beyond). It will give you an opportunity to apply the concepts and lessons of this course to your
present job, or to the job you plan to secure after graduation.
Your paper should address the following five questions:
1. What are the power dynamics inherent in your current job/future job?
2. What sources of power and influence will you need to be effective?
3. Do your current sources of power and influence fit the requirements of the job? Explain.
4. What are the ethical dilemmas for which you will need to be vigilant?
5. What is your plan for developing the sources of power, influence skills, and social
network necessary to be effective
a. currently/before taking the job,
b. during your first year after graduating, and
c. a few years down the road?
One way to get a sense of a political environment is to talk to people who have been there. As
part of this assignment, you are required to conduct one informational interview with someone
who is qualified to speak about the political environment that surrounds the position you have or
the position you seek. About one page of your paper should be dedicated to (briefly) discussing
this informational interview: Think about the interview you conducted for your final paper. Why
did you choose this individual? What are some primary insights you gained from your
conversation with them? You do not need to name this person (and they are likely to be more

COURSE POLICIES
Attendance Policy
This course follows a hybrid structure, with only three in-person sessions. Our time together in
person is limited, and I want us to make the most of it. You must tell me if you expect to miss
one of our in-person sessions (even part of one) and why. If there is a good reason you cannot
attend class, please contact me in advance and I can give you an assignment to complete to
earn participation credit for that missed class. Unexplained absences will hurt your grade, and
multiple absences (explained or not) will hinder your ability to learn from this class.
It is not possible for you to attend in-person sessions over Zoom. These sessions will involve
exercises that cannot be completed remotely.
Generative AI Policy
This policy applies to use of various new generative AI tools that can generate responses of
various types (e.g., text, images) to prompts by users (e.g., ChatGPT, BingChat, Bard,
Midjourney, etc.). These new tools can streamline and augment our work but can also dull our
critical thinking skills. You are permitted to use these tools for this class under these
circumstances:
• You may use these tools to refine your initial work, but not to generate an entire paper you
then claim as your own. This course is focused on your personal experience and growth,
and you will gain little from the course if you leave all the writing (and thus the thinking) to
generative AI.
• If you use generative AI for an assignment, be transparent how you used it. I am not fussed
about how you make this acknowledgment (e.g., footnote, separate section at end of paper),
just that you do it.
