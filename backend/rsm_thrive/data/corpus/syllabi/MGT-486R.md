# MGT 486R — Fundamentals of Real Estate Analysis

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SP26, SP27

## What this course covers

This course applies corporate finance and investment principles to real estate markets,
covering private and public markets, debt and equity, and commercial real estate assets.
It emphasizes a value-based framework for real estate decisions, including valuation,
mortgage underwriting, development, and leasing, while also addressing historical trends
such as segregation and redlining. Instruction combines asynchronous lectures, in-person
sessions, quizzes, and team presentations.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- valuing commercial real estate assets
- building pro forma statements
- analyzing leverage and mortgage effects on returns
- evaluating real estate investment risk and return
- measuring returns and using price indices
- assessing demand and supply drivers of real estate value
- understanding historical public policy impacts on urban patterns

## Topics covered

real estate market analysis, commercial real estate asset classes, real estate valuation, pro forma analysis, mortgage and leverage, real estate investment trusts, urban economics, housing affordability and inequality, taxation of real estate, historical segregation and redlining.

DESCRIPTION
The course will draw on the principals from corporate finance and investments and apply them to the
context of real estate markets in their various shapes and forms (private vs. public markets; debt vs.
equity). However, we may at times make reference to the complementary fields of real estate law, public
and urban economics, design and architecture, and the economics of inequality.
OBJECTIVES
While the study of real estate can be undertaken from many different perspectives, we will choose the
value perspective as our major theme. What is the value of a particular asset? This question is central to
all real estate decision making: acquiring a property, mortgage underwriting, real estate development,
lease vs. buy decisions, to name a few. Additionally, the course will focus on commercial real estate
(CRE) assets. Office, Multi-family Residential, Retail and Industrial are the four main types of commercial
real estate. The CRE landscape also encompasses assets such as hotels, leisure facilities, data centers,
cell-phone towers, among others. Finally, we will address historical trends in real estate, including the
legacy of residential segregation, redlining, and reverse-redlining.
Fundamentals of Real Estate Analysis thus has the following objectives:
To develop students’ awareness of a fundamental value-based framework—both its strengths and
weaknesses—from which to analyze a key set of real estate decisions.
To heighten students’ awareness of macroeconomic and local demand and supply factors which drive
value.
To increase students literacy of the historical drivers of urban patterns and the role of public institutions in
driving or correcting historical patterns.
To introduce students to financial modelling techniques in real estate investment analysis. To understand
the impact of critical assumptions (model inputs) on valuation and risk metrics (model outputs).
Consequently, to make students aware of the strengths and weaknesses of these widely used
techniques, and help them develop an intuition for, and a critical eye towards, these models.
To develop an understanding of risk and its relationship to return in the context of real estate investments.
Additionally, to understand how risk interacts with the financing and capital structure of real estate
investments.

The use of Artificial Intelligence (“AI”) tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, and similar
systems is permitted in this course. These tools may be used to support learning, including improving
writing quality, clarifying complex ideas, identifying potential sources, and refining arguments.

content as one’s own constitutes an academic integrity violation, comparable to misrepresenting another
person’s work. Students are expected to demonstrate their own understanding of course material.
Students may use AI tools for homework assignments. However, reliance on AI as a substitute for
mastering core concepts is strongly discouraged. Most variation in course grades is determined by inperson examinations, which assess students’ independent understanding of fundamental concepts.
Students who do not develop this understanding will perform poorly on the exam regardless of AI use
during the semester.
