# MGT 471 — Digital Transformation (of est. businesses)

Rady MBA · offered FA25 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

Digital Transformation is an advanced graduate course on all critical aspects of
transforming a traditional 'analog' incumbent into a digital organization. Students
learn to identify weaknesses of incumbents, propose business model innovations to
address those weaknesses, and plan a transition toward digitization for established
companies facing disruption by startups and tech companies. The course is case-based,
using Harvard cases in every class, with interactive presentations and hands-on group
activities.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- identifying weaknesses of incumbent businesses
- proposing business model innovations
- planning digital transformation transitions
- analyzing digital disruption cases
- evaluating digital transformation strategies
- applying strategic frameworks to established companies
- conducting a group analysis of a company's digital transformation

## Topics covered

digital disruption, business model innovation, value creation, digital transformation, digital touchpoints, digital factory, maintaining competitive dominance, subscription business models, technology transitions, incumbent strategy.

## Tools used

canvas, chatgpt, large language models.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on class participation (45%), assignments (10%), and a final group project (45%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes class participation (45%), assignments (10%), and a final group project (45%), plus weekly required case readings and assignment questions.

Policy on AI, gen AI and LLMs
I will assume all students will have access to and use generative AI and LLMs (chatGPT and its clones
chatPDF, LLama, Bard, etc.) as their own personal tutor. Anything incremental is their own work and
will contribute to their grades. In practice I intend to use Chat GPT to feed my Harvard cases and ask it
for summaries and to answer assignment questions about the case/company. I will tell that to students.
And I may start class discussions with whatever the genAI tool spits out. That will be the baseline
understanding. Any student that delivers in class or in homework at exactly that level will get a
maximum grade of a C- or D. In other words, they just learned basic genAI prompts and to memorize
the output. For work handed in, I ask students the courtesy to disclose gen AI as a source like any
other source on the internet or otherwise.

IN SUM, I explicitly allow any use of large language models like ChatGPT or others. Be advised
that their accuracy is often poor, so please be careful, as you remain solely responsible for the
accuracy, content and conciseness of your submitted answers. I view LLMs as good sources of ideas
and pointers, but poor sources of accurate information and VERY poor substitutes for human
judgment.
