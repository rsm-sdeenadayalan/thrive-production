# MGT 412 — New Venture Design

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA26

## What this course covers

This course teaches students to transform user needs, strategic intent, and
technological innovation into viable new products, services, and ventures. It introduces
design thinking and a hybrid top-down/bottom-up approach, using case studies, readings,
practical exercises, peer feedback, and a final team project to develop and pitch a
compelling business concept.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing factors that focus a company's innovation efforts
- conducting user and market research
- synthesizing research to uncover user insights
- constructing compelling positioning for new offerings
- prototyping, testing, and refining concepts
- crafting and presenting a compelling business case
- evaluating and critiquing the work of others
- collaborating effectively in teams

## Topics covered

design thinking, user research, market research, customer needs analysis, opportunity identification, product positioning, prototyping, concept testing, business case development, new venture creation.

## Tools used

generative ai, chatgpt, custom ai agents, npd ideation assistant.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

The course grade is based on teamwork (10%), class participation (20%), practical exercises and submissions (30%), and the team project presentation (40%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes teamwork, class participation, practical exercises and submissions, and a final team project presentation and report.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course is the foundational elective to begin shaping your MBA for a career based upon Innovation,
Design, or Entrepreneurship. Along with MGT 452: New Product Development, it forms the core of the
Certificate in Design & Innovation. For those looking to fully prepare their startup to launch post-MBA, it is a
prerequisite for MGT 415: New Venture Development.
The course will provide experience in transforming an understanding of user need, a company’s strategic
intent, and the scientific or technological innovation to which the company has access, into viable new lines
of business or ventures that bring desirable and profitable new offerings to the world. The course will provide
an introduction to the user-based, strategic and operational aspects of sparking an innovation and
developing it into a product or service that people will love, as a competitive and sustainable business.
Traditional business planning has generally approached this challenge in a top-down manner, in the sense of
being driven by the company’s motivations, capabilities, or vision of the future. The newer practice of Design
Thinking frames the process as one driven from below. Vital insights as to people’s needs and wants are
gathered through primary research, and the company takes its direction from the people it serves.
In actual practice, successful companies pursue a hybrid top-down and bottom-up approach, though often
without framing this as an explicit process, or even realizing that they are doing so. We will learn the tools
that help do this, explore their utility and boundaries, and place them in the context of an overarching
framework.
Course materials are a mix of case studies, readings, multimedia, and practical exercises. Online
assignments are meant to provide the flexibility for students and teams to adapt workflow to their own
schedules, with due dates to ensure that materials are available as needed for peer feedback, instructor
feedback, and whole-class discussion.
The peer feedback and whole-class discussion assignments are a vital part of the learning process, because,
as managers after graduation, you will need to evaluate and critique the work of others on your team and

across the organization, as well as contributing your own work. Active participation is vital, as much of this
can only be learned by doing.
Additionally, teams will meet individually with the instructor, for focused coaching and critique as they
develop their concepts and final presentations.
There is a final project, in which student teams will develop a concept that forms the basis of a new line of
business, and the accompanying explanation of why this is a compelling offering from the customer’s
perspective and a compelling opportunity from the business’ perspective. We will help you initiate, develop,
and refine your concept using the practical exercises throughout the course, and you will present your final
concept pitch to the entire class in the third in-person session.
For those students pursuing a startup idea, the team you form and concept you explore this quarter
does not need to continue into the Winter New Venture Development course - many students discover
better opportunities and form stronger teams as a result of their experience in this course, and you are
absolutely free to pursue those. Others find value in learning the essential tools using a “practice” idea – one
free from the preconceptions and assumptions they may have accumulated.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy and Approach
Generative AI is a disruptive force in both business and education. While many choose to focus on the
negative aspects of disruption, leaders of Design and Innovation teams recognize that the disruption of
established systems and competitive dynamics also creates opportunities for those who are able to harness
the new tools and master the new landscapes created as a result. This is the essence of the concept
“Disruptive Innovation” at the heart of Clayton Christensen’s seminal work The Innovator’s Dilemma.
We also know from the history of technological advances in design and manufacturing that new tools tend to
improve both quality and speed of work for the thoughtful user, and shift the thoughtful adopter’s focus to
the higher-value creative and intellectual tasks. Thoughtless (and non-) adopters become obsolete.
The assignments in this course do not lend themselves to simply “having ChatGPT do it;” I know this from
experiments we have run in previous quarters of this course. There are, however, elements that we can learn
to accomplish better or faster by treating generative AI as an assistant to our work, which further helps us
develop our ability to critique and guide innovation work, critical managerial skills. There are also examples of
good process that a custom agent can model for us to help us learn to use good process. You will have an
opportunity to experience this firsthand through exercises during our in-person sessions and through custom
agents I have created for your use in this course, such as the NPD Ideation Assistant.
To help us advance our ability to use AI as an effective tool and to identify ineffective uses of AI, I allow its
use in this course under the following conditions: Cite and Critique.
Cite: Identify which AI tool you used, and for what task(s). Save and forward the prompt and output.
Critique: Give your assessment of how helpful the tool was to you and your team. Identify how it could have
been more helpful. I will synthesize these learnings for the class as a whole.

This applies both to any tools you discover and to the custom tools and agents I supply for your use.
