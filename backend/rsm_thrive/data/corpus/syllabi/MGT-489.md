# MGT 489 — Skills of the future

Rady MBA · offered SU25, SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

A case-based MBA course that treats brands as co-creations of consumers and marketers,
emphasizing the strategic management of brand meaning to maximize value and reduce risk.
It covers brand equity, architecture, positioning, extensions, storytelling, licensing,
crisis management, and luxury branding. Students apply frameworks through case
discussions, experiential assignments, and a comprehensive brand audit.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- seeing the big picture
- making intentional career decisions
- navigating change and complexity
- building trust and influence
- telling compelling stories
- leading in ambiguous situations
- generating creative ideas
- applying social intelligence
- analyzing brand equity
- crafting brand strategy

## Topics covered

big picture thinking, strategic thinking, decision-making, communication, storytelling, career management, adaptivity, creativity, social intelligence, leadership, brand equity, brand architecture.

## Tools used

chatgpt, ai tools, canvas, discord.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on participation/discussion boards (40 points), assignments (40 points), and a final exam (20 points), with a group brand audit and presentation also required.

## Workload

Moderate. Weekly participation/discussion posts, ten experiential assignments, a comprehensive group brand audit and presentation, and a final exam.

DESCRIPTION
Imagine this: you’re about to meet your new colleagues, and someone whispers that every one of them is
more intelligent than Albert Einstein. Intimidating, right?
That’s the reality we’re stepping into. Workplaces will be filled with advanced AIs — brilliant, tireless,
and beyond anything we’ve known. Frightening? Maybe.
But here’s the good news: humans still hold five unique advantages. We can see the bigger picture. We
connect through social intelligence and storytelling. We adapt. We make decisions in complex, messy
systems. And we still spark ideas no machine can replicate.
This course is designed not to alarm, but to equip you. You’ll learn to sharpen these skills with practical
techniques you can apply immediately. By the end, you won’t just understand these advantages — you’ll
embody them.
So when you walk into that future workplace, you’ll feel ready — not overshadowed, but confident,
prepared to thrive alongside AI.

OBJECTIVES
This course strengthens the human skills that matter most in an AI-driven world. You will learn to:
• Think more clearly and strategically by seeing the big picture.
• Manage your career and choices with intention by strengthening your decision-making skills.
• Navigate change and complexity with confidence by staying adaptive and creative.
• Build trust and influence by using social intelligence and storytelling.
• Develop the leadership capacity to integrate thinking, deciding, creating, adapting, and
influencing – when problems are ambiguous and stakes are high.

CHATGPT AND OTHER AI TOOLS
This course encourages thoughtful and transparent use of artificial intelligence tools as part of your
learning process. You are invited to treat AI as a thinking partner – a resource for exploration,
refinement, and feedback – not as a substitute for your own analysis or creativity.
Guidelines for using AI (from the assignment document)
Allowed: Editing, formatting, summarizing notes, checking grammar, and refining phrasing or structure.
Allowed with disclosure: Using AI to generate ideas, outlines, or phrasing. If you use AI to generate
any part of your analysis or phrasing, you must include a short AI Usage Note at the end of your report,
as well as briefly state what prompts or tasks you used AI for. Also, explain how you verified, modified,
or improved the AI’s output, and provide at least one example of that.
Recommended: Use AI as a thinking partner, not a writer – to question assumptions, test reasoning,
explore alternatives, or clarify structure.
Purpose of this policy
The goal is to help you develop judgment about when and how to use AI responsibly – building your
capacity to combine human insight with technological support. This aligns with the course’s broader
focus.

DESCRIPTION
A brand is often a company’s most important asset and as such has received immense attention in recent years.
Effective brand-building and band management drives superior financial results, consumer loyalty and competitive
advantage. This is a course with a decided point-of-view on the nature of brands and the skills and tasks this
requires of managers. We will consider brands as co-creations of consumers and marketers. If brand
management is a collaborative process of meaning management, the job of the brand manager then becomes
one of navigating the meaning making processes, attaining meanings that resonate therein, and to managing
these meanings deftly through time so as to maximize brand value, capture opportunities, and diminish risk. Some
of a firm’s most valuable assets are the brands that it has invested in and developed over time. Although
manufacturing processes can often be duplicated, strongly held beliefs and attitudes established in consumers’
minds cannot.
This is a case-based course. Cases, as microcosms of brand management, deliver the power of real-world,
applied settings and reveal concepts and frameworks that can inform management thoughts and actions. Our
collective experience and expertise outweighs and overshadows the knowledge any one faculty member can
impart. Case discussions, and the full participation they require, is an integral component of this course.

Class Participation (C)
Class participation is earned by “attending” class by contributing your thoughts and questions to the various
discussions in class. In practice, great ideas that are not articulated (or are not articulated in a compelling way)
are ignored. Thus, participation is important to your continued professional development. Further, much of the
value of an MBA comes from the interchange with classmates who come from various industries and strategic
contexts. The benefits that each of you receive from this class will be directly enhanced by the mutual sharing of
insights. Thus, throughout the term, I will track the degree to which you contribute to discussion. Please know
that quality of contribution (meaningful insights that help us progress in our discussion of the topic) far outweighs
quantity.
In online weeks, discussion takes place on our Discord channel. In those weeks, I will post a series of questions
related to the readings and case study that I would like you to explore. Each student is responsible for a) posting a
detailed response to one of the questions or b) posting a critical and thoughtful reply to one of your fellow
students’ responses.

Experiential Assignments (C/G)
You will be asked to recognize, apply, and explore the branding strategies discussed in the readings and the
cases. To solidify the knowledge, you will complete group discussion assignments that will ask you to do deeper
analysis or take managerial stances and think deeper about the readings. These are not meant to explicitly test
whether you have read that week’s readings, but to answer the questions fully, understanding the weekly readings
is necessary. Therefore, when completing these assignments you should use your notes and class materials. You
should not use the wider internet unless specifically noted. We want you to learn and be able to apply this
knowledge; LLMs already know this stuff. Hopefully, these quizzes will keep you “on task” by solidifying the
knowledge and keep you learning and engaged throughout the quarter.

Brand Audit and Presentation (G)
The final group project for this course is a comprehensive brand audit, where each team will analyze a brand of
their choice (pending instructor approval) using frameworks and tools introduced throughout the quarter. The goal
is to evaluate the brand’s current positioning, identity, equity, and communication strategy, and to develop
strategic recommendations for strengthening or evolving the brand. Over the course of the term, student groups
will gather and synthesize data from a variety of sources, including the brand’s own materials, customer feedback,
competitor analysis, and market trends. The final deliverable will be a well-organized report that presents the

Yorkston – Brand Management
brand’s strengths and weaknesses, diagnoses key branding challenges, and proposes actionable insights
supported by evidence. In addition to the written report, each group will deliver a 10–12 minute presentation
summarizing their audit findings and recommendations, tailored for a professional audience. Creativity, analytical
rigor, and visual polish will all be key components of successful projects. This assignment will allow students to
apply branding theory to a real-world scenario and practice delivering strategic recommendations in a compelling

AI POLICY FOR MARKETING MANAGEMENT COURSE (written with the help of ChatGPT)
This policy outlines the appropriate use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools in the Marketing Management course,
ensuring that the technology enhances learning while preserving the integrity of individual thought and effort. The
policy applies to all enrolled students in the Executive MBA Marketing Management course.

Permitted Use of AI:

o​ Understanding and Research: Students are permitted to use AI tools to deepen their understanding of
course materials and assist with research related to course topics. AI can be utilized to summarize
readings, provide explanations of complex concepts, and generate ideas for project brainstorming.

o​ Explicitly Allowed Assignments: AI may be used in completing assignments only when explicitly
stated in the assignment instructions. These will be designed to specifically leverage AI capabilities in
a way that enhances the learning objectives of the task.

Prohibited Use of AI:

o​ General Assignments and Examinations: The use of generative AI is strictly prohibited in the
completion of regular assignments and during examinations. Assignments are crafted to assess
individual understanding and the ability to apply marketing concepts independently.
o​ Discussion Contributions: Participation in course discussions, whether online or in-person, must be
free of AI assistance. The value of these discussions lies in the personal insights, original thought,
and interaction among peers, which AI usage could undermine.

Rationale:

o​ The educational value of assignments and discussions in this course stems from personal intellectual
engagement and the development of individual analytical skills. AI, while beneficial in certain
contexts, can shortcut these learning processes and reduce the depth of personal understanding and
critical thinking.

Enforcement:

o​ Students are expected to adhere to this policy as part of their commitment to academic integrity.
Violations of the AI usage policy will be subject to review under the standard academic misconduct
procedures and could result in disciplinary action.

Support and Clarifications:

o​ Students are encouraged to seek clarification from the instructor regarding the use of AI tools in
specific instances. The instructor is available to provide guidance on how AI can be appropriately
integrated into learning activities without compromising educational outcomes.
