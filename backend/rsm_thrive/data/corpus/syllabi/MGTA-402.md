# MGTA 402 — Data-Driven Communications

**2 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered FA25, FA26

## What this course covers

A highly participatory lab course on presenting data through clear, compelling
storytelling. Students use actor-training techniques to strengthen delivery, vocal
range, and audience connection, building weekly presentations that culminate in a final
storytelling-with-data presentation.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- delivering engaging presentations
- structuring a narrative around data
- designing effective slides
- giving and receiving peer feedback

## Topics covered

storytelling with data, presentation structure, slide design, data visualization for audiences, public speaking and vocal delivery, storyboarding.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

40% engagement/participation/peer feedback, 60% individual and group assignments; no exams.

## Workload

Moderate. Weekly readings, presentation prep, peer feedback and reflections, plus a final project presentation over five sessions.

## Offerings

- **FA25** — Walsh
- **FA26**

DESCRIPTION
Communication skills in the 21st century are of utmost importance. A strong presentation requires skill in
sharing your ideas with authenticity, clarity and passion. This course will focus on preparing presentations
using clear, effective stories to support your data and to persuade your listeners to action.
Whether you are presenting to a large crowd or to an individual client, we will focus on communication
skills needed to inspire your audience. Using some of the techniques that actors use in training, you will
have the opportunity to develop your speaking skills, and to improve your ability to effectively connect with
your audience.
This is a lab class that is highly participatory. Each week you will work on exercises to enhance your
performance skills designed to help you give clear and compelling presentations. You will work individually
and in groups. Each week you will have readings on relative topics, prepare presentations, provide written
feedback to your peers and write self-reflections. You will have a final project presentation to prepare on a
subject of your choice, which we will begin to work on class 2.
