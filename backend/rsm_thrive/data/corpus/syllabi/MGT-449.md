# MGT 449 — Topics in Operations & Technology: Global Supply Chain Management - New Approaches

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SU25, SP26, SP27

## What this course covers

This course takes an end-to-end systems perspective on global supply chains, focusing on
how supply chain decisions affect a company's financial statements, cash flow, working
capital, and shareholder value. It covers operational finance, traditional and newer
supply chain financing methods, fintech and blockchain-enabled trade finance, and the
costs, benefits, and risks involved. Teaching combines lectures, data-driven case
studies, in-class discussion, and team research projects, delivered over Zoom.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- identifying genai opportunities in business processes
- designing practical genai solutions
- implementing genai prototypes
- evaluating ai adoption decisions
- communicating ai capabilities and limitations
- integrating genai into workflows
- analyzing supply chain risk and resilience
- evaluating supply chain trade-offs and decisions
- matching demand and supply
- articulating supply chain issues to executives and policymakers

## Topics covered

generative ai, business process analysis, prompt engineering, retrieval augmented generation, ai-human interaction, tool use and mcp, multi-agent systems, ai-gateways, data generation and analysis, end-to-end supply chain, supply chain evolution, customer experience lifecycle.

## Tools used

claude code, n8n, openclaw, vs code, chatgpt, github copilot, zoom, canvas, grok.

## Prerequisites

This is a Master’s Level Program.

## How it is graded

The course is graded on a team case assignment (25%), a team research assignment deck/presentation (35%), class attendance/participation (10%), and an individual final exam/deliverable (30%), with final grades also considering ranking among students.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes a full-quarter group project (50%), individual assignments (20%), and class participation (30%), with weekly sessions and project milestones.

2 Course Objectives
Generative AI (GenAI) is developing at an incredible speed. Companies are eager to adopt
these new technologies but many struggle with implementation. In this course you will learn
how to leverage GenAI in real business settings.
• Business Impact: GenAI is already transforming core business functions - from enhancing
and automating business processes to supporting and enhancing data analysis. You’ll
learn which tools work best for different business problems and how to implement them
effectively.
• Practical Decision-Making: You’ll learn how to combine GenAI with existing business
processes to improve decision-making. This includes knowing when to use AI and,
importantly, when not to use it.
• Workflow Improvement: GenAI can streamline operations across departments. You’ll
learn to identify where these tools add real value and how to implement them in ways
that actually work.
• Career Development: Understanding GenAI isn’t optional anymore - it’s becoming a core
business skill. This course will give you hands-on experience with tools that companies
are actively using and hiring for.

3

• Broad Applications: The skills you’ll learn apply across business functions - from marketing and finance to operations and strategy. You’ll work on real business processes to
understand how these tools can solve problems. This is a practical course. You’ll spend
most of your time working with GenAI tools on real business problems. By the end,
you’ll be able to:
• Identify where GenAI can add value to business processes
• Design and implement practical GenAI solutions
• Make better-informed decisions about AI adoption
• Effectively communicate AI capabilities and limitations to stakeholders

11 Project
The group project will span the full quarter and is divided into multiple milestones to develop
an AI-coworker. The AI-coworker should (partially) automate and enhance a business process.
This process can be in any business area or can even combine multiple business functions (e.g.,
marketing, analytics, finance, operations, supply chain, consulting, etc. etc.).

11.1 Key Components
1. Business Process Analysis: Identify and document a real-world business process related
to your career interests, analyzing inefficiencies and opportunities for improvement.
2. GenAI Integration Strategy: Develop a comprehensive plan for integrating generative AI to enhance the selected business process, clearly delineating human and AI
responsibilities.
3. Functional Prototype Development: Create a working prototype that demonstrates
core functionality and key GenAI capabilities.
4. User Experience Design: Design a user-friendly interface with explicit mechanisms
for human oversight and feedback.

with integrated GenAI.
Note: No programming experience is required. With state-of-the-art GenAI tools
plus instructor and TA support you can build (almost) anything you can envision.

COURSE DESCRIPTION:
Supply/Value Chains are among the most discussed topics of today at the business, societal and
policy levels. In the highly competitive, volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA)
international environment that we operate in, companies do not compete. Supply chains and
industries compete. And countries compete through their supply chains. It is imperative,
therefore, that managers, policy makers and students understand global supply chains, their
operations, the key decisions and trade-offs and the events that impact them.
The course addresses the new issues and environment of the Global Supply Chain, the impacts
of technology and people, and the new imperatives and practices for companies and
governments. It is a critical course for executives, managers, policy-makers and students in all
the major functions of business and policy across all industrial sectors.
The supply chain is the most significant and critical component of business and national
security, and accounts for the bulk of the material, people, cash and value-added in an
organization. Recent events – trade wars and tariffs, the pandemic, wars, major international
crises and extreme weather events have highlighted the critical role that the supply chains play
in business, global trade, the world economy, as well as in national security and societal wellbeing.
The supply chain involves the flow of materials, cash, information and processes from the
source of raw materials to end customers... and back. Its objectives are multiple and complex
and include maximizing shareholder value (and stakeholder interests) while minimizing total

doing all this while minimizing risk, maximizing resilience and ensuring supply chain
sustainability. This “holistic”, multi-faceted and “End-to-End” view of the supply/value chain
incorporates Critical Thinking and is very different from the traditional functional approach. It is

The course will be taught using a combination of several learning approaches – classroom

Course Description
Supply Chains are among the most important and discussed topics at today’s business, board
and national policy levels In our complex and technology-intensive environment, companies do
not compete. Supply chains and industries compete. And countries compete through their supply
chains. This is particularly evident in today’s environment of tariffs, trade wars and restrictions.
Supply Chain Finance and the Financing of operations are fundamental aspects of a company’s
successful operations and strategic options.
The supply chain typically controls most of a company’s costs and cash. Free Cash Flow from
operations, Cash-to-Cash Cycles, financial performance, operating margins and supply
assurance are major drivers and indicators of shareholder value and sustainable competitive
success. A complex and global supply chain cannot be managed successfully without a firm
understanding of this topic. The course takes an End-to-End (E2E) “systems perspective” of
today’s global supply chain and will address the critical areas of operational finance (the issues
related to the financials and analysis of a company’s operations, cash flow and cost
management), and financing the supply chain & operations (including traditional and newer
methods, financing estimation, fintech, costs, benefits and risks). Every CEO, COO and CFO must
understand this aspect of the global supply chain. This course is a critical component of a
business education and career and is directed at managers and students in all the major
business functions.

Learning Objectives
The objective of Supply Chain Finance is to provide insights into
• the impacts that the supply chain has on the financials of a company.
• analyzing and optimizing working capital, cash and operational costs, and
• the traditional and more advanced instruments that are used to help finance the
operations of the company.
while explicitly considering End-to-End supply chain transactions, flow and risk management.
The course will provide executives and policy makers, with an integrated business
understanding of the operations of a company, the financial levers, the supply chain impacts on
the financial statements and shareholder value, and the methods and instruments used in
financing the company’s supply chain and operations.

Dr. Gopal has over 40 years of experience in global supply chain & operations strategy, execution,
and technology in a career that has encompassed industry executive management, consulting
and executive education. Dr. Gopal has held executive positions at several leading companies,
including Partner & Director of Global Supply Chain & Operations Services at Ernst & Young
Consulting, VP in World-Wide Operations at Dell Computer, as well as executive VP positions at
Unisys, Overland Storage and SAIC. His consulting clients have included prominent global and
regional companies across a range of industries, and he has also assisted in several start-up
companies.
Dr. Gopal has served as a Member of the Defense Business Board (DBB) for several years. The
DBB provides the Secretary and Deputy Secretary of Defense, as well as other senior leaders,
trusted independent and objective advice which reflects an outside private sector perspective
on proven and effective best business practices for consideration and potential application to
the Department.
Chris is also a Supply Chain Advisor to the Global Fund based in Geneva, a public-private
organization designed to accelerate the end of AIDS, tuberculosis, malaria and COVID as
epidemics. He has also served on a White House sub-committee on Manufacturing
Technologies
Chris currently consults with companies, serves as the Strategic Advisor at several small
companies, teaches at the University of California San Diego and at the University of Southern
California, where he also serves on the Advisory Board of the Global Supply Chain Management
Center
Dr. Gopal has spoken and served as an invited panelist at major industry forums, including the
Milken Institute Global Forum, IATA World Conferences, the OMI-OECD Forum, the Harvard
Business Review Forum, the USC “Learning from the Pandemic to Improve Healthcare”
Symposiums in 2021-22, the Energy Institute, Texas on “Sustainable Supply Chains”, the EU
Conference on Monopoly and Security held recently in Brussels, the Panel on “The Next World
System: Can US Trade Policy Make Americans More Secure, Democratic, and Prosperous? ” at
the National Press Club in Washington, and recently testified before the Congressional USChina Committee on Supply Chain security in Washington.
Dr. Gopal is the co-author of four books on Supply Chain Management. His latest book,
“Breakthrough Supply Chains: How companies and nations can thrive in an Uncertain World”
was published June 2023 and has been endorsed by the Council for Supply Chain Management
Professionals and been accepted into the Pentagon Library.
Chris holds a PhD from the University of Southern California, and an MBA from the Cranfield
School of Management, UK.
