# MGTF 421 — Corporate Finance

Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course covers the foundations of corporate finance with an emphasis on
applications, including investment decision rules, valuation, risk and return, capital
structure, security issuance, and the use of derivatives in corporate finance. It
combines lectures, case discussions, and textbook readings to build a practical toolkit
for solving corporate finance problems.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- valuing firms and financial assets
- making capital budgeting decisions
- estimating cost of capital
- choosing capital structure and security issuance
- using derivatives in corporate finance
- analyzing mergers and acquisitions
- applying real options analysis

## Topics covered

capital budgeting, investment decision rules, firm valuation, mergers and acquisitions, risk and return, cost of capital, capital structure, payout policy, derivative securities, real options.

DESCRIPTION
This course covers the foundations of corporate finance with an emphasis on applications. We will
discuss many of the major financial decisions made by managers both within the firm and in their
interactions with investors. Topics include criteria for making investment decisions, valuation of financial
assets and liabilities, relationships between risk and return and their consequences for the firm, capital
structure choice and security issuance, and the effective use of derivatives pricing methods and concepts
in corporate finance contexts.
OBJECTIVES
The goal of this course is to provide a conceptual framework for understanding and tackling the most
important corporate finance problems, and how to apply these concepts in practice. Specifically, the
course is meant to: (1) to familiarize students with core corporate finance concepts, (2) to develop a
toolkit that allows one to solve important corporate finance problems in practice (including, but not limited
to, capital budgeting, valuation, optimal capital structure and security issuance, and uses of derivatives
securities and concepts within the firm), and (3) to provide preparation for a variety of roles in corporate
finance and capital markets in which knowledge of valuation and financing policy are important.

ASSIGNMENTS
Case assignments (Group work)
Written case analyses, which respond to specific questions or prompts in the posted case assignments, are to be
prepared by groups of 3-6 students (groups with fewer students than this are allowed but are not ideal; groups with
more students are not allowed). Each group will submit one write-up as a pdf document, using a drop box on the
Canvas course site. The analyses are due at the beginning of class on the day that the case will be discussed.
Your case write-ups should reflect the independent judgement and analysis of your group. Making use of case solutions or
case materials of any kind from the Internet, from other universities, from previous versions of this course, or from any
other sources is strictly prohibited.
The use of generative AI / large language model (LLM) tools such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Bing Chat, etc. is
allowed when preparing your case write-ups, subject to the following conditions:
• You should not use AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on. This constitutes an
academic integrity violation, just like plagiarizing another student’s work, copying from
pre-existing case solutions, or using unauthorized materials during an exam. These tools should support your own work, not
replace it!
• If you use an AI tool, be transparent about how it was used by including a relevant footnote or citation,
just like if you were citing an academic paper or textbook that you used as a reference.
• You are still fully responsible for the content in all deliverables. If you submit a poor-quality write-up (e.g.,
one with AI “hallucinations”, logical inconsistencies, responses that are not well-supported by your analysis, etc.)
you will receive a correspondingly poor score.
Exams (Individual work)
Exams will be based on material covered in the class sessions. There will be no makeup exams except for university
sanctioned reasons (including, but not limited to: time conflict with another course, medical emergency, family
emergency, religious observance, etc.). No accommodations can be implemented retroactively.
If you miss the midterm, then the grade weighting % from the midterm will be added to the final exam, and therefore the
final will count for 60% of the course grade. If you take the midterm and your score (in %) on the final is higher than your
score (in %) on the midterm, your score on the final will be substituted for the midterm score when computing course
grades. If you are penalized on either exam for an academic integrity violation, this “score substitution” policy does not
apply!
Both the midterm and final exam are in-person “paper and pencil” exams. You are allowed a calculator(s) of your choice
(any financial, scientific, and/or graphing calculators are acceptable, as long as they cannot connect to the internet, nor have
