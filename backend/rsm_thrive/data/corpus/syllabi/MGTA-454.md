# MGTA 454 — Business Analytics Capstone

**4 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered SP26

## What this course covers

The MSBA capstone: teams solve a real business analytics problem for an external sponsor
company, covering the full project cycle from scoping and a client-approved charter
through data preparation, analysis, and final recommendations. Emphasis on project
management, stakeholder communication, and professional delivery.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- managing project scope
- working with real clients
- end-to-end analysis execution
- team collaboration
- presenting to technical and nontechnical audiences

## Topics covered

project scoping and charters, client and stakeholder management, data preparation, exploratory and predictive analysis, prescriptive recommendations, professional presentations.

## Tools used

EduSourced, PowerPoint.

## Prerequisites

Taken in the final quarter; applies techniques from the whole program (requires MGTA 451, 452, 453).

## How it is graded

25% professionalism, 25% teamwork (peer surveys), 15% output quality, 15% communication, 20% individual understanding.

## Workload

Heavy. Explicitly ~12 hours/week: weekly client and advisor meetings, biweekly peer surveys, mid-term and final presentations with individual exams.

## Offerings

- **SP26**

DESCRIPTION

In today’s digital age, leveraging data-driven insights is essential for achieving a competitive edge.
Companies that harness the power of data continuously develop new methods, algorithms, and tools to
address complex business challenges.
This capstone course offers you the opportunity to apply the strategies, techniques, and tools learned
throughout the MSBA program to solve real-world business and analytics problems. Working in teams on
a project sponsored by an external organization, you will engage in every step of the data analytics
process—from defining the problem and preparing data to analysis and final presentation. This course is
designed to prepare you for a successful career in analytics by providing practical, hands-on experience
in teamwork, project management, and professional communication.
It is essential that every team member contributes their data analysis skills and business acumen to
achieve the project objectives.
OBJECTIVES
By the end of this course, you will be able to:
● Experience the full cycle of designing and implementing analytical solutions for practical business
problems.
○ Negotiating the scope of the project
○ Prioritizing certain parts of the project in case the scope of work is greater than the
time/resources available
○ Working with peers from different cultures and different skill-sets
○ Replicating a real-life situation where skills needed for a project often change very
often and at short notice - learning on the job, where the technical skill-set needed for
the project is not something you have acquired so far, but you acquire those skills
while executing the project
● Engage with real clients to address authentic business challenges.
○ Asking for help and support from relevant person if you are running behind schedule
○ Communicating the progress (or lack of it) clearly to all stakeholders
○ Managing stakeholder expectations
○ Communicating your intermediate and final output in a manner which is
understandable to all stakeholders and persuades them to agree to your
recommendations
PROJECTS
Your capstone project, sponsored by an external company or organization, will include the following
activities:
1. Create a Project Charter and get it approved by the client
2. Collaborate with clients to clearly define a business problem and design an analytics solution.
3. Review relevant technical literature and consult with subject matter experts.
4. Organize, cleanse, and preprocess the available data.
5. Perform exploratory data analysis to uncover insights.
6. Update the Project Health Tracker with the latest status every week
7. Conduct predictive data analysis to model future trends.
8. Prepare a comprehensive data product, which may include visualizations, code/scripting, and

even app development in certain cases.
9. Communicate your key findings and insights through presentations.

GROUP PRESENTATIONS
● Mid-Term Presentation:
o A concise 15-minute presentation with a maximum of 12 slides in the main deck, outlining key
aspects of your capstone project; you can have additional slides in the appendix to answer any
questions from the audience
o Suggested content includes:
■ A description of the business problem and any constraints.
■ The business goal and available data.
■ Expected deliverables and metrics for success.
■ Your proposed solution path and an overview of data preparation, exploratory analysis, and
predictive or explanatory analysis.
■ Initial findings, recommendations, and the potential commercial value of your results.
o 30 minutes - Feedback & faculty coaching to address any gaps or blind spots
● Final Presentation:
o A 15-minute presentation with a maximum of 12 slides in the main deck, showcasing your final
results and recommendations; you can have additional slides in the appendix to answer any
questions from the audience
o Your presentation should include:
■ Clear explanation of your predictive model, error metrics, and evaluation methods.
■ Detailed discussion of your prescriptive recommendations; how they create business
value.
■ An interactive Q&A session where you refine your project based on audience feedback.
o Q&A for 30 minutes (includes team & Individual)
Each presentation should be clear, concise, and designed to engage a nontechnical audience.
