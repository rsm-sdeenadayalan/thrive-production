# MGTA 458 — Experiments for Business Analytics (Experiments in Firms)

**4 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered WI26

## What this course covers

Covers how businesses and policymakers use randomized experiments in pricing, marketing,
employee incentives, and consumer behavior, incorporating behavioral economics into
experimental interventions. Groups develop a business question, design a randomized
experiment, run a pilot study, and present results.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- designing randomized experiments
- applying behavioral economics to interventions
- running pilot studies
- presenting experimental results

## Topics covered

randomized experiment design, behavioral economics, pilot studies, pricing experiments, marketing experiments, employee incentives, consumer behavior.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

15% participation, 10% individual assignments, 35% midterm, 40% group final project.

## Workload

Moderate. Midterm (35%), two small individual assignments, milestone-based group pilot project with presentation.

DESCRIPTION
Businesses and policymakers are increasingly using experimentation in a wide range of areas
including product pricing, marketing strategies, employee incentives and shaping consumer
behavior. These experiments can be particularly effective if the interventions tested incorporate
insights from behavioral economics. However, many companies currently lack the knowledge
necessary to take advantage of experimentation. An understanding of how to design and run a
good experiment will position students at the cutting edge of decision-making in business.
The goals of the course are for students to understand: (1) the role of experiments in business
and policy (2) how insights from behavioral economics can be incorporated into experimental
interventions and (3) how to design and run an experiment in an organization.
We will begin by discussing the role of experiments in organizations and examining examples.
Students will work individually or in teams to develop a question and design a randomized
experiment to test their question. Students will run pilot studies to inform their designs. At the end
of the course, students will present their pilots.

Participation: Participation consists of class attendance, speaking in class, engaging in smallgroup discussion, and participating in pilots/feedback with other groups.

AI POLICY
While generative AI can be a very useful tool, in this class we will not be using AI for assignments
or exams (unless your pilot study involves the impact of AI in the pilot study). We would like you
to practice coming up with your own ideas and experimental designs because we believe that this
process teaches critical thinking skills that are essential in business.
