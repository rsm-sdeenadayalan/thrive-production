# MGT 455R — Customer Analytics and (Generative) AI

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SP26, WI27

## What this course covers

This hands-on course uses real-world data to equip students with the tools and
conceptual understanding needed to leverage customer analytics for immediate business
value. It covers the evolving data landscape, the role of generative AI in analytics,
and how to move from traditional segmentation to individual-level customer connections
at scale. Instruction blends in-person sessions, online videos and readings, individual
and group assignments, and exercises.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- interpreting regression output
- building logistic regression models
- scoring customers for targeting
- implementing test and rollout campaigns
- evaluating model performance with lift and gains charts
- calculating roi for targeting campaigns
- applying uplift modeling
- calculating customer lifetime value
- using generative ai for data analysis

## Topics covered

customer analytics, generative ai, regression analysis, logistic regression, customer targeting, test and rollout campaigns, model evaluation, uplift modeling, personalization, churn analysis, customer lifetime value, causal inference.

OBJECTIVES
Many firms possess extensive data on customer behavior, yet few have the expertise to translate this
information into effective actions. Recent developments have made these capabilities even more
valuable: The deprecation of tracking data, Generative AI, and the technical and economic feasibility of
moving beyond traditional segmentation to individual-level customer connections at scale.
Internal Data as Strategic Asset
Privacy regulations such as GDPR, iOS restrictions, ad-blockers, and the advent of GenAI browsers are
introducing huge changes to the data landscape. Organizations can no longer rely on external tracking to
understand customer behavior. This makes internal data and the analysis of such to not merely useful but
critical to support data-driven decision making. Firms that fail to fully leverage their own customer data
operate at a structural disadvantage.
Generative AI, Personalization, and Individual-Level Analytics
Generative AI is democratizing the ability to conduct both basic and advanced analytics. Taking full
advantage of these tools, however, requires deeper understanding of how data are generated, how to
evaluate and apply output from machine learning models, and how to formulate questions that yield
appropriate and valid conclusions. Practically, generative AI enables organizations to act on analytical
insights at scale and lower cost—customer analytics can now inform personalized content generation,
service interactions, and product recommendations at the individual level. Yet the quality of AI-generated
personalization depends predominantly on the quality of the underlying customer-level analysis.
Traditional marketing segmentation groups customers into broad categories for standardized products,
services, and messaging. Customer analytics now makes individual-level analysis economically viable in
both B2C and B2B contexts. Understanding individual customer preferences, lifetime value trajectories,
and behavioral patterns allows organizations to deploy AI tools effectively. Without this analytical
foundation, personalization efforts produce noise rather than value.

COURSE POLICIES
Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) policy
The use of GenAI tools is encouraged in this class. These tools can help to streamline and
augment your work. Use them with the following caveats and conditions:
• GenAI-tools should enhance your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models can
improve the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex
ideas or terms, generate code, etc. etc. You should not use GenAI tools to generate content that
you simply put your name on. This constitutes an academic integrity violation, much like taking
another individual’s work and misrepresenting it as your own.
• We expect that you understand and can answer detailed questions about the work you submit as
your own.
• Cold-calling will be used extensively in class and you may be asked to explain the work you
submitted.
• If you use GenAI for an assignment, be transparent about how it was used in a footnote or
citation (e.g., a markdown file with the chat log).
• Large language Models (LLMs) have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy. You
are responsible for all submitted content and will be graded accordingly.
