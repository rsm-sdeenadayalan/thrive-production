# MGT 453 — Supply Chain Management

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SU26

## What this course covers

Supply chain management involves the flows of materials and information among all firms
that contribute value to a product, from raw materials to end customers. The course
integrates marketing, logistics, and operations management perspectives, and covers how
data analytics and digital transformation can make supply chains more flexible,
resilient, and transparent. Students use conceptual frameworks to analyze supply chain
issues including distribution, forecasting, inventory, and risk management.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- identifying elements of a strong brand
- crafting brand strategy
- analyzing brand management scenarios
- integrating social media into branding
- managing brand meaning over time
- evaluating brand extension opportunities
- analyzing supply chain performance
- forecasting demand
- designing distribution networks
- managing inventory

## Topics covered

brand equity, brand architecture, brand components, brand extensions, brand life cycle, brand storytelling, brand licensing, brand crisis management, luxury branding, brand revitalization, supply chain strategy, distribution network design.

discord, canvas.

DESCRIPTION
A brand is often a company’s most important asset and as such has received immense attention in recent years.
Effective brand-building and brand management drives superior financial results, consumer loyalty and competitive
advantage. This is a course with a decided point-of-view on the nature of brands and the skills and tasks this requires
of managers. We will consider brands as co-creations of consumers and marketers. If brand management is a
collaborative process of meaning management, the job of the brand manager then becomes one of navigating the
meaning making processes, attaining meanings that resonate therein, and to managing these meanings deftly
through time so as to maximize brand value, capture opportunities, and diminish risk. Some of a firm’s most valuable
assets are the brands that it has invested in and developed over time. Although manufacturing processes can often
be duplicated, strongly held beliefs and attitudes established in consumers’ minds cannot.
This is a case-based course. Cases, as microcosms of brand management, deliver the power of real-world, applied
settings and reveal concepts and frameworks that can inform management thoughts and actions. Our collective
experience and expertise outweighs and overshadows the knowledge any one faculty member can impart. Case
discussions, and the full participation they require, is an integral component of this course.

DESCRIPTION
Supply Chain Management involves the flows of materials and information among all of the firms that
contribute value to a product, from the source of raw materials to end customers. We will integrate issues
from marketing (channels of distribution), logistics, and operations management to develop a broad
understanding of a supply chain. Naturally, this course draws upon concepts originally introduced in your
pre-term courses on operations management, statistics, marketing, and strategy. While many operational
processes that we will consider in this course will appear familiar, most of you will find yourselves viewing
them from a different perspective especially through different conceptual frameworks for analyzing supply
chain issues.
Data analytics and digital transformation of supply chains became even more important due to digitation
movement of the supply chains. Digital transformation also enabled information visibility and transparency
across the supply chains. Information visibility helps to speed up the movement of materials including
inventory, which saves capital investment and generates free cash flows. This course also covers how
data analytics can help supply chains to be more flexible and resilient, and how to evaluate and mitigate
risk exposures through analytics.
OBJECTIVES
At the close of this course, you will be able to:

•

supply chain through data analytics
Grasp various strategic and tactical supply chain issues such as distribution and fulfillment
strategy, demand forecasting, information sharing strategy, outsourcing, procurement (including
e-markets), virtual integration, and risk management.
Explore supply chain business opportunities from the digitization of supply chains and the
emergence of platforms.

Case Write-ups: G – Students may work together in groups and turn in one report per each case for the
