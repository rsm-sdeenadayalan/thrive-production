# MGTA 495 — Special Topics: Healthcare Analytics

**2 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered SP26

## What this course covers

A rigorous introduction to healthcare decision modeling centered on cost-effectiveness
analysis for evaluating competing health interventions. Covers deterministic and
probabilistic sensitivity analysis and Markov cohort and microsimulation models of
disease progression, applied to a project on type 2 diabetes medications.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- building healthcare decision models
- cost-effectiveness evaluation under uncertainty
- Markov and microsimulation modeling

## Topics covered

cost-effectiveness analysis, ICER, sensitivity analysis, Markov cohort models, microsimulation, valuing health outcomes.

DESCRIPTION
This course provides a rigorous and practical introduction to healthcare decision modeling and analytics.
We will start with cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA), which is the cornerstone of evidence-based resource
allocation in healthcare. Students will learn how to evaluate competing health interventions by quantifying
their costs and health outcomes using formal decision analysis methods. Building on this foundation,
students will learn how to address uncertainty in decision models through deterministic and probabilistic
sensitivity analyses. We will then focus on dynamic modeling frameworks widely used in health
economics and policy analysis. Students will develop Markov cohort models and microsimulation (patientlevel) models to represent chronic disease progression, competing risks, and patient heterogeneity, and
analyze model outputs and visualize results. Through a combination of lectures, hands-on exercises, and
real-world healthcare examples, students will gain the analytical and computational skills to build,
analyze, and interpret decision models that inform health policy decisions.

COURSE POLICIES
Course Format. The course will be offered in person and will consist of 5 weekly sessions. Sessions will
include interactive lectures and hands-on modeling exercises. Students are encouraged to bring their
laptops to follow along with lecture notes and coding exercises. All lectures will be recorded.
Attendance Policy. In-person attendance is mandatory for all students. If you must miss a class due to a
valid reason (e.g., illness or family emergency), you must email the instructor with appropriate
documentation (proof of valid reason) and watch the lecture recording in full within 7 days of the missed
class to receive the attendance credit. Missing more than 2 classes will result in a failing grade (“F”) for
the course – no exceptions.
Expectations and Late Submissions. Students will be expected to participate in class discussions and
complete two homework assignments, and a course project. All assignments must be turned in via
Canvas by the specified deadline. Late submission will incur a penalty of 10% of the total points for each
24-hour period (or part thereof) past the deadline, and submissions are not accepted after solutions are
posted on Canvas (usually 2-3 days after the due date). Students are encouraged to submit their
assignments early if they anticipate delays closer to the due date.

class. These tools have great potential to enhance your efficiency and improve the quality of your work.

complex concepts. However, you should understand and explain every step of your analysis in your
submission. You should never use AI tools to generate content that you simply present as your own, as
