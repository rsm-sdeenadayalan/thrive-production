# MGTF 403 — Advanced Financial Risk Management

Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course covers modern financial techniques for evaluating and managing various
sources of risk using financial strategies and instruments. It addresses measures such
as Value-at-Risk and Cash-Flow at Risk, methods for changing exposure through
derivatives, and strategic risk management. The class relies on lectures, case studies,
book readings, and financial press handouts, with a significant quantitative component.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- measuring value-at-risk
- hedging financial exposures with derivatives
- managing currency and commodity risk
- assessing credit and operational risk
- implementing risk management techniques
- evaluating strategic risk management cases

## Topics covered

value-at-risk, cash-flow-at-risk, derivatives, dynamic hedging, exchange rate risk, commodity price risk, interest rate risk, credit risk, operational risk, strategic risk management.

excel, python, canvas.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
Risk management is about implementing modern financial techniques to evaluate and manage various
sources of risk using financial strategies and instruments. This class will use class lectures, case
studies, book readings, and additional “last minute” handouts from the financial press to illustrate the
basic principles of modern risk management.
The Advanced Risk Management course is designed primarily for Masters of Finance students. We will
first discuss if, when, and how risk management contributes to firm value. We will analyze various
measures of risk--such as Value-at-Risk (VAR) and Cash-Flow at Risk (CAR)--and how financial and
non-financial companies use these measures in project selection, performance evaluation, and
managerial compensation. Then, we will turn to methods of changing a firm’s exposure to financial risk
using derivatives. More specifically, we will cover dynamic hedging, the management of exchange rate
risk, commodity price risk, interest rate risk, credit risk, weather risk, and operational risk. We will also
analyze how strategic risk management can be applied to extract embedded options. A section of the
class will cover Basel II and other current banking regulations. Finally, we will discuss cases where
financial risk management is vital for the success of a business strategy.
All covered topics will have a significant quantitative component; students will have to work with
financial data and will be required to implement the methods discussed in class. The focus of the
course is on implementing risk management techniques rather than on derivative pricing. We will rely
heavily on case studies to solidify and apply our knowledge to problems encountered in the business
world.
Risk management is a very rapidly growing field. If you are interested in risk management, you might
want to join the Global Association of Risk Professionals (GARP). GARP offers an official financial risk
management (FRM) accreditation (similarly to CPA for accountants and CFA for financial analysts.) The
class will use some of the GARP recommended books (see below).
I will assume that you are familiar with basic statistical concepts (means, variances, covariances and
linear regression, etc.) and Excel.

ASSIGNMENTS AND GRADING
There will be bi-weekly case studies. You have to work in assigned groups. The case studies will be
based on class discussions. Please write the answers to the case studies in a memo format of no more
than two pages. The limit of two pages is for the typed part only. Please, attach all supporting materials
(graphs, tables, pictures, etc.) at the end of the document. You should refer to the supporting materials
in the main body of the memo.
We will discuss answers to the case studies at the beginning of the following week’s class. You should
be ready to take active participation in these discussions.

There will be no midterm exams. There will be a final exam. It will cover class lectures, case studies,
readings assigned in the textbook, additional readings, and materials distributed during class. The final
exam will be comprehensive and should be sole authored. No collaboration of any kind is allowed.

The grading policy is as follows:
1. Case Studies/Written Exercises (35%) — work in pre-assigned groups. One written
assignment per group to be turned in on time.
2. Weekly Quizzes (15%) — We will assign weekly quizzes after each lecture on Canvas.
They will help you keep up with the material.
3. Class Participation (10%) — Weekly attendance and class participation, contributing to a
productive learning environment.
4. Final Examination (40%) —The final examination will be administered during Finals
week.
To prevent one bad performance from affecting your grade, I will drop the lowest case study
exercise and weekly quiz grade.
