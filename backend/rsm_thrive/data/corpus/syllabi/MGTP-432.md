# MGTP 432 — Advanced Auditing & Forensic Accounting

**4 units** · Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered FA26

## What this course covers

A graduate-level, applied treatment of the financial statement assurance function,
emphasizing audit judgment, evidence evaluation, data analytics, and forensic
accounting. Students conduct a staged audit engagement across the quarter, investigate a
real fraud enforcement case, and complete CPA-format checks aligned with the AUD exam.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- evaluating client acceptance and engagement risk
- designing risk-based audit plans
- testing internal controls and evaluating deficiencies
- executing substantive procedures using audit data analytics
- detecting and investigating fraud indicators
- forming and communicating audit conclusions
- drafting appropriate audit reports
- applying professional skepticism and judgment

## Topics covered

professional standards, client acceptance and engagement risk, audit planning and materiality, audit evidence and sampling, internal controls testing, audit data analytics, forensic accounting and fraud investigation, audit reporting.

## Tools used

canvas, connect, becker cpa review, ey helix gl analyzer, techwear, zoom, course response system.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Graded on weekly prep bundles (10%), participation (10%), audit simulation (30%), individual data analytics deliverable (10%), mock AUD check (5%), fraud autopsy project (10%), and midterm exam (25%).

## Workload

Moderate. Weekly prep bundles, five audit simulation installments, an individual data analytics deliverable, a fraud autopsy project, a midterm, and a mock CPA check; estimated 6 out-of-class hours per week.

DESCRIPTION
You have already learned how an audit works. In this course, you perform one — and you learn what
happens when audits fail. MGTP 432 provides a graduate-level, applied treatment of the financial
statement assurance function, with emphasis on audit judgment, evidence evaluation, data analytics,
and forensic accounting.
The course is organized around three connected experiences. First, your team runs a staged audit
engagement across the entire quarter — from client acceptance through risk assessment, controls
testing, substantive procedures, and the audit report — building a complete engagement file the way a
professional team would. Second, a forensic accounting arc examines how frauds are committed,
detected, and investigated, culminating in a team investigation of a real enforcement case. Third, the
course is deliberately aligned with the CPA Exam (AUD): assigned Becker CPA Review modules, in-class
CPA Prep questions, and a Week 10 Mock AUD Comprehensive Check connect each week’s work to the
exam you will sit for — while the midterm exam tests the deeper analysis and evaluation skills that both
the AUD section and professional practice demand.
A deliberate design choice: there is no finals-week exam. The first half of the course — the conceptual
core of risk, planning, and controls — is examined on the midterm. The second half is examined the way
professional practice examines you: through the engagement file your team builds, your individual data
analytics work, your fraud investigation, and a comprehensive CPA-format check in Week 10.
Because this course introduces substantial professional terminology and judgment frameworks,
consistent weekly preparation is essential. The reading and preparation layer (SmartBook and Becker) is
deliberately structured with one deadline per week so that class time can be devoted to application,
discussion, and decision-making.

OBJECTIVES
Building on your prior auditing coursework, this course emphasizes applying professional standards and
exercising the judgment required in professional practice. At the close of this course, you will be able to:
• Evaluate client acceptance and engagement risk, and support the decision in a professional memo
• Design and defend a risk-based audit plan, including materiality judgments and responses to
assessed risks of material misstatement
• Test internal controls, evaluate deficiencies (deficiency, significant deficiency, material weakness),
and determine the impact on the audit approach
• Execute substantive procedures on transaction-level data using audit data analytics tools, and
evaluate identified misstatements
• Detect, investigate, and communicate indicators of fraud using forensic accounting techniques
• Form and communicate audit conclusions, including selecting and drafting the appropriate audit
report
• Exercise professional skepticism and judgment at the analysis and evaluation levels tested on the
CPA Exam (AUD)

ASSIGNMENTS
Each assignment below is designated as: I — independent, individual work only; G — group work with
one team submission; or C — collaboration on concepts allowed, but each student submits their own
work.

Weekly Prep Bundles (I) — 10%
Each week’s foundation layer is packaged as a single Prep Bundle with one deadline: Monday at 11:59
pm before class. A bundle contains (a) the SmartBook assignment for the week’s chapter(s) and (b) the
assigned Becker AUD module(s) mapped to the same topics, tracked through the Becker Navigator
dashboard. Two bundles differ slightly: Bundle 5 (midterm week) contains no new reading — it is a
Becker cumulative review set supporting your exam preparation — and Bundle 8 also includes a onepage preparation memo on the Wirecard case, which readies you for the Week 9 discussion. Bundles are
graded on completion. There are eight bundles; your lowest bundle score is dropped. Completing the
bundle before class is what allows class time to focus on application rather than first exposure — come
prepared with questions on anything you found difficult.

In-Class Participation and Professionalism (I) — 10%
Each class includes CPA Prep questions drawn from the Becker AUD question bank and aligned with that
week’s material, answered in class through the course response system. Points can only be earned in
class during your scheduled class time, and there is a strict no-make-up policy for missed in-class
questions. The professionalism component reflects consistent attendance and punctuality, preparation,
active engagement in case discussions and simulation work sessions, and professional conduct toward
classmates.

Audit Simulation (G) — 30%
Working in assigned teams of four to five, you will conduct a staged audit engagement of a simulated
client across the quarter, submitting five installments that together form a complete engagement file:
(1) Client Acceptance Memo and engagement letter (4%); (2) Risk Assessment and Planning Memo (6%);

(3) Controls Testing and Deficiency Evaluation (6%); (4) Substantive Testing and Misstatement
Evaluation (8%); and (5) Audit Report and Completion Checklist (6%).
• All memos are limited to two pages plus exhibits. Page limits are enforced; concise professional
writing is part of the skill being assessed.
• Installments 2 and 4 include a brief team oral defense, scheduled during class work sessions.
• Each team receives its own data parameters; deliverables are graded on the quality of working
papers, reasoning, and professional communication.
• Engagement roles (partner, manager, senior, staff, reviewer) rotate each installment so that every
member leads at least once.
• Team-member contributions are assessed through the Rady peer-evaluation tool after installments
2, 4, and 5, and factor into individual grades.

Individual Data Analytics Deliverable (I) — 10%
An individual journal-entry-testing analysis using the EY Helix GL Analyzer / TechWear data, opened at
the Week 7 lab and due Week 8. This deliverable carries meaningful individual weight by design: it
ensures every student — not just a team delegate — can plan, execute, and communicate audit data
analytics. You may discuss approaches with classmates, but all analysis and writing must be your own.

Fraud Autopsy Project (G) — 10%
Launched Week 7 and due Week 10, teams investigate a real fraud selected from a curated list anchored
in PCAOB and SEC enforcement documents. Your team will produce an eight-minute recorded
presentation and a one-page executive summary addressing: what happened; what the auditors did and
should have done; which course concepts were violated; and what today’s analytics would have
detected. Presentations are peer-reviewed in class during Week 10 using an engagement-quality-review
rubric, and the class votes an EQR Award for the strongest investigation.

Midterm Exam (I) — 25%
The midterm is a standalone, full class session during Week 6 (11:30 am–2:20 pm) — there is no lecture
that day. It covers Weeks 1–5 (the conceptual core: the profession and its standards, acceptance,
planning, materiality, evidence, audit risk, fraud risk, and the use and testing of internal controls) and
mirrors the structure of the CPA AUD exam. Part 1 consists of scenario-based multiple-choice questions;
Part 2 consists of task-based simulations — document review, deficiency evaluation, and short memo
tasks built on the kinds of situations discussed in class and in the audit simulation. The exam is written
by the instructor, not drawn from a publisher test bank, and tests analysis, evaluation, and judgment:
expect “what is the MOST appropriate response” questions, not definition recall. An exam blueprint
(topic weights, question formats, and timing for each part) will be posted at least two weeks in advance,
and the weekly in-class CPA Prep questions use the same formats all quarter, so nothing about the

exam’s style will be a surprise. There is a strict no-make-up policy. The midterm is graded anonymously
(see Course Policies).
There is no final exam. The second half of the course is assessed through the applied work it produces —
Simulation installments #3–#5, the Individual Data Analytics Deliverable, the Fraud Autopsy Project, and
the Mock AUD Comprehensive Check.

Mock AUD Comprehensive Check (I) — 5%
In class during Week 10, you will complete a CPA-format assessment spanning the full quarter, built
from AUD-exam-style questions. It is deliberately low-stakes — graded on completion and good-faith
performance — and is immediately followed by a live debrief of the most-missed questions. Its purpose
is diagnostic: it shows you, four weeks before many of you begin dedicated AUD study, exactly where
you stand.

CHATGPT AND OTHER AI TOOLS
AI tools can be useful for enhancing clarity, structure, and grammar in your writing. In this course, you
may use such tools only to support and refine your own original work — not to generate complete
responses, analyses, or arguments on your behalf. Any use of AI-generated content must be
meaningfully revised and integrated into your own reasoning.
Each assignment states whether AI use is permitted. Note that this course also treats AI as an object of
study: in Week 9 you will critique an AI-drafted audit memo, because evaluating machine-generated
analysis with professional skepticism is now part of the auditor’s job.
Because MGTP 432 is designed to build the professional judgment needed for auditing practice and CPA
exam success, it is essential that you demonstrate your own learning and critical thinking. Submitting AIgenerated work — particularly for simulation installments, the Fraud Autopsy, or exams — undermines
your professional development and may be treated as an academic integrity violation under university
policy. Auditing, like the CPA profession itself, is built on independence, integrity, and accountability.
Your work in this course should reflect those same values.

COURSE POLICIES
Attendance
Regular attendance is essential for success in this course. Because this course emphasizes case
discussion, simulation work sessions, and applied exercises, your presence contributes significantly to
both your learning and your team’s. If you are not in attendance, you cannot earn participation points
for that session, and participation points cannot be made up for unexcused absences. If you must miss
class due to illness, emergency, or university-sanctioned events, please notify me in advance when
possible.

Late and Make-Up Work
Assignments must be submitted by the posted due date and time to receive full credit. Exceptions may
be considered only for documented, university-approved absences (UC San Diego-sanctioned events,
religious observances, medical emergencies). For known absences, request approval and provide

documentation at least one week in advance of the due date. The lowest Prep Bundle score is dropped
automatically, which covers routine disruptions; please reserve requests for genuine emergencies.

Anonymous Grading
Consistent with Rady School policy, grading in this course uses Canvas’s anonymous grading feature
wherever the assignment format permits, including the midterm exam and written individual
deliverables. Team deliverables and oral defenses cannot be anonymized by nature; those are graded
against published rubrics, and subjective elements are calibrated by the instructor.

Peer Evaluations
Team-based work in this course uses the Rady peer-evaluation tool. Evaluations are confidential, are
completed at three points in the quarter, and inform individual adjustments to team grades.
Professional, specific feedback is expected — writing a useful peer evaluation is itself a professional skill.
