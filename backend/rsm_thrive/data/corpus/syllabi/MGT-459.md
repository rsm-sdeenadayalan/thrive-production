# MGT 459 — Topics in Innovation - Bringing a Product to the U.S. Market

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SP26, WI26, SP27

## What this course covers

This course focuses on contextual growth and entrepreneurship, exploring options across
the risk/reward spectrum such as acquisition entrepreneurship, side-hustles, scalable
entrepreneurship, corporate entrepreneurship, and angel/venture investing. Students
develop a Personal Entrepreneurial Plan and/or business pitch deck, engage in a multi-
week venture simulation, and learn through real-life case studies and guest speakers
from the life-sciences and technology industries.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- developing a personal entrepreneurial plan
- creating a business pitch deck
- analyzing venture opportunities
- evaluating business models
- making strategic growth decisions
- participating in venture simulations
- providing constructive peer feedback
- synthesizing course material in an exam
- collaborating in cross-cultural teams
- analyzing business cases

## Topics covered

acquisition entrepreneurship, side-hustles, scalable entrepreneurship, corporate entrepreneurship, angel and venture investing, venture financing, business model design, growth strategy, intrapreneurship and spinouts, venture simulation, innovation, entrepreneurship.

## Tools used

canvas, lockdown browser, venture simulation, google drive, powerpoint, spreadsheets.

### MGT 459 Topics in Innovation - Venture Innovation & Growth Strategy (Krishnan) WI26

MGT 459: Venture Innovation, Growth Strategy, and Simulation
PROFESSOR VISH KRISHNAN; vk@ucsd.edu
TA: Siqi Wang; siw119@ucsd.edu
COURSE OBJECTIVES
Our class focuses on the topic of Contextual Growth and Entrepreneurship, which makes
students reflect on their current context and tailor their growth and entrepreneurship path,
thereby accelerating their career trajectory. The course exposes students to a variety of options
on the risk/reward spectrum, including Acquisition Entrepreneurship, Side-hustles, Scalable
Entrepreneurship, Corporate Entrepreneurship, and Angel/Venture Investing. Students develop a
Personal Entrepreneurial Plan (PEP) and/or a business pitch deck, as well as engage in a multiweek simulation. The simulation, originally developed at Stanford University, helps us
experience the dynamics of a venture lifecycle and challenges us to raise money and strategically
invest in growth while building wealth for the founders and stakeholders.
Through a combination of real-life venture case studies and a venture simulation, we learn and
immerse ourselves on the topics of venture innovation, business model development, venture
financing, and growth strategy. In addition, we will learn first-hand from distinguished
alumni/entrepreneurs and venture capitalists in the life-sciences and technology industries their
journey of juggling a new venture and developing/executing a business model while working
full-time and learning the economics of the industry to build a thriving new venture. The
examples include companies that went from an Idea to an IPO to others that are still well into
their journey towards major milestones. Our goal is to develop the creative, strategic and critical
thinking skills characteristic of an innovative, opportunity-driven, and entrepreneurial manager.
We consider different types of startup/enterprises in the life sciences and information technology
industries as well as lifestyle/service businesses. We also consider other approaches such as
buying your way into entrepreneurship. The course may help students get a firmer grasp of the
nuances and trade-offs, find their innovation sweetspot, and develop their professional growth
plan in an established or start-up setting. We challenge students to build on their personal
leadership plan to chart out their entrepreneurial/growth trajectory for the medium time horizon.

Class Engagement/Participation (20%):
Your class participation (contribution to class discussions) will count for 20% of the class grade.
You are expected to be an active participant throughout the entire class and to contribute to the
quality of the discussion. Please note that the frequency (i.e., the quantity) of your interventions
in class is not a key criterion for effective class contribution.
Some criteria used to evaluate class contribution are as follows:
1. Is the participant deeply engaged in class?
2. Is the participant a good listener? Are the points made relevant to the discussion? Are
they linked to the comments of others? Do the comments show evidence of analysis of
the case?
3. Is there willingness to test new ideas, or are all comments "safe"? (For example,
repetition of case facts without analysis and conclusions).
4. Do comments clarify or build upon the important aspects of earlier comments and lead to
a clearer understanding of the case?
Note: You are expected to attend EVERY class; you are responsible for the material covered in
class whether or not you attend. However, I realize that despite your best efforts you might have
be forced to miss a class due to unforeseen emergencies. If you must miss a class, you may
provide a one-page written evaluation of the material or case to be covered (contact us for details
of specific assignments). This assignment should be e-mailed in advance of class for planned
absences and by the BEGINNING of the following class for emergencies.
Case Analysis Quiz (20%):
Few minutes of class sessions will be allocated to an in-class Canvas quiz based on a case
assigned for that class. These are to be taken without any external/AI guidance and would be
mostly administered using a lockdown browser. There will be four quizzes based on case studies.
Professional Growth Sweetspot Reflection: 20%
One of the objectives of this class is to figure out how venture creation and analysis fits in your
career journey. As a first step, you will be asked to create a first draft of your Growth trajectory
and sweetspots. Then you will work in pairs to offer feedback to a classmate and update your
own Statement based on the feedback received. You will receive credit for your first draft
feedback provided to your partner on their first draft, and an updated second draft submitted to
the Canvas dropbox.

OVERVIEW AND OBJECTIVES
The Rady School of Management at UC San Diego established a center connecting students and
entrepreneurs across borders in education, collaboration, and technology translation into new
market opportunities. The U.S.-Israel Center on Innovation and Economic Sustainability (USIC)
empowers entrepreneurs in San Diego and Israel to jointly examine and address factors in the
success of startup companies, innovation, and sustainability.
Fostering the collaborative spirit of the USIC, the San Diego Immersion course brings together up
to fifteen graduate students from leading Israeli universities and up to fifteen RSOM graduate
students to build cross-cultural relationships, meet local entrepreneurs and thought leaders, and
apply their skills to a 48-hour business case hackathon, culminating in presentations to an expert
panel.
This class does not offer tangible learning goals such as frameworks, models, etc. Yet, students
will have multiple opportunities to learn by doing and observing. As loosely translated from
Newton’s third law of motion: you only get out of it what you put into it.
Students applying to this program must sign a statement acknowledging they have read and
understood all requirements, course structure, etc., and commit to participate in all activities.
