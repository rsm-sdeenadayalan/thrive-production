# MGT 433 — Managing Healthcare and Life Sciences

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered WI26, WI27

## What this course covers

This seminar-style course explores the business of healthcare and life sciences from a
U.S. perspective, with comparisons to other countries. Students examine the roles,
motivations, and interdependencies of patients, providers, payers, government, and
industry, and then focus on innovation and disruption in the field. Teaching relies on
lectures, case studies, and open discussion.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing healthcare business models
- evaluating stakeholder incentives
- assessing regulatory impact on strategy
- identifying innovation opportunities
- conducting financial analysis of healthcare ventures
- presenting business proposals
- interviewing healthcare executives
- writing reflective analyses

## Topics covered

healthcare system and stakeholders, providers and payers, pharmaceuticals and biotechnology, medical devices and healthcare it, private equity in healthcare, innovation and disruption, government regulation, global healthcare models.

## Tools used

canvas, study.net, brytewave, linkedin, youtube.

None stated in the syllabus.

Grading is based on class participation (20%), a healthcare executive interview (10%), an innovation team project (25%), reflection memos (20%), and a midterm exam (25%).

DESCRIPTION
Health touches all of us and most of us know someone who is suffering from a potentially serious health issue.
Healthcare is often described as a fundamental right. Yet, the realities of limited resources, a complex system and
difficult ethics make healthcare one of the most challenging problems of our time. Every fifth dollar in the U.S. is
now spent on healthcare with results that are lagging other developed nations.
This presents unique opportunities for innovation and disruption that could yield enormous benefits for patients
globally. The prospect of novel business models, innovative new therapies and alternative regulatory approaches is
promising and at the same time introduces new risks.
Our course will explore the business of healthcare and life sciences from a U.S. perspective and will draw
comparisons and highlight alternate approaches from around the world.
You will gain an understanding of the roles, motivations, and interdependencies of key stakeholders such as
patients, providers, payers, government, and industry. We will examine how these stakeholders seek to address
challenges and gain a competitive advantage – both domestic and international.
During the second half of the course, our focus will shift to innovation and disruption in healthcare. You will have a
chance to critically evaluate current trends and new approaches and initiatives by various players.
Upon completion of the course, you will have gained an understanding of the business side of healthcare and life
sciences and career opportunities in this exciting field.

ASSIGNMENTS:
Reading Assignments:
Please read the assigned readings prior to the respective class sessions. This will allow you to come prepared with
your own thoughts, examples and questions and allow you to participate fully in class discussions.
All readings can be found on Canvas under “Study.net Materials”. You can download the entire reader or select
individual articles.

Class Participation and Professionalism:
Being present and active participation are key in this class. Much of your learning will happen during class
discussions through the exchange of ideas. This can mean asking questions to enhance your (and others’)
understanding of the material as well as contributing with examples and thoughts that demonstrate your
understanding of what we discuss. The expectation is that you come prepared with comments and/or questions
that are relevant to the class. You may also consider bringing news articles and stories that pertain to the topic of
the class session.
Professionalism is expected. That means treating others with courtesy, respect, and listening to their points of
view as well as working collaboratively in teams. It also means being present during class and refraining from the
use of laptops, tablets and phones unless required. Lastly, it is expected that you arrive on time and remain until
the end of each class meeting.
Healthcare Business Executive Interview:
There will be one “smaller” team project that you will do in teams of two. You will reach out to a professional who
works in healthcare or life sciences in a business capacity at a senior level (director or above) and interview them.
You could use LinkedIn to reach out to Rady or UCSD alumni. The goal is to understand a) what they see as key
trends, business challenges and opportunities in their area of healthcare and/or life sciences, b) how they got to
where they are and what career advice they may have for you. You will submit a 1.5-2 page (single-spaced) written
summary of what you learned from the interview. This should not be a transcript of your conversation but a
synopsis of what you discussed and the key insights that you took away from the conversation.
This assignment is due by February 5.
Healthcare Innovation Team Project:
There will be one “big” team project including a final team presentation in lieu of a final exam. The project allows
you to apply what you have learned about healthcare stakeholders, incentives, government regulation, and the
challenges in healthcare. The assignment is for your team to develop an innovation idea in healthcare or life
sciences and demonstrate how it is advantageous from a business perspective. As a first step you may want to
brainstorm problems and choose one to focus on as a team. This may involve conducting research on the size of
the problem, which stakeholders it impacts, what their motivations are and whether solutions are available. From
there, you will propose one or more solutions as your innovation idea. This should include a projection of demand
and a financial analysis of the opportunity and consider reimbursement. Whenever possible, data should be used

Mid-Term Exam:
The mid-term exam will consist of multiple-choice and short-answer/essay questions to assess your knowledge of
the concepts around healthcare stakeholders in Module I (weeks 1-5). The exam will cover any class material up to
that point including readings, lectures and discussions.
Reflection Memos:
You will write reflection memos on the four articles highlighted in red on the course schedule. These reflections
should be ~ 1 ½ pages in length and not exceed 2 pages in length (single-spaced). These submissions are due on
the day the respective article will be discussed in class.
In the reflection memo, please discuss your thoughts on the article e.g. what surprised you? How does it relate to
what we have discussed in class? Do you have personal experiences that you can relate to the article? What
thoughts do you have that expand on the article e.g. based on current events or other articles? What do you think
will happen next and why? The key point to remember is that the paper is not a test. The goal is not to summarize
the article or to answer an exam question, but rather it is to explore your own thoughts and ideas about a topic.

COURSE POLICIES
The majority of content and learning will be covered during class sessions in the form of lectures and especially
discussions. Therefore, you will take away more from the course if you attend the class meetings.
During the class, you may want to share healthcare examples that are personal or relate to people you know.
Please use your judgement in what you are comfortable sharing and respect others’ privacy (e.g. by keeping their
identity confidential).
UCSD email and Canvas will be used as the primary means of communication and material delivery within this
course. Be sure to have consistent access and regularly check both.
While AI tools will likely not be as helpful in this course, the use of AI tools is allowed. However, AI tools must be
noted as a source when used for assignments. It should be noted that the accuracy of large language models is
often poor and that they do not serve as a replacement for human understanding. It is the responsibility of each
student to ensure their use of content generated by AI tools does not constitute academic misconduct, such as
plagiarism (intentional or unintentional) or cheating.
