# MGT 404 — Accounting

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA25, FA26, WI26

## What this course covers

This course examines fundamental accounting concepts and principles, with the goal of
helping students become informed users of financial statements rather than training them
as accountants. Classes include interactive lectures, discussions, and short cases
involving corporate financial statements to illustrate accounting concepts and explore
companies' disclosure strategies.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- reading financial statements
- analyzing real-world financial statements
- asking management questions about financial statements
- understanding international differences in financial statements
- preparing financial statements
- recording business transactions
- analyzing how transactions flow into financial statements
- using accounting information for business decisions
- understanding the financial reporting process

## Topics covered

accounting principles, financial statements, balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement, financial statement analysis, corporate disclosure, international accounting differences, financial accounting framework, double-entry bookkeeping, accounting cycle, accounts receivable.

## Tools used

mcgraw-hill connect, canvas, mcgraw hill connect.

## Prerequisites

None.

## How it is graded

Grading is based on graded online homework (15%), class participation (10%), the mid-quarter exam (25%), and the final exam (50%).

Moderate. Graded work consists of weekly Connect quizzes, a midterm, a final exam, and in-class participation, with optional homework and self-study problems.

DESCRIPTION
In this course, we will examine fundamental accounting concepts and principles. The course is intended to
provide you with the basic skills necessary to understand and analyze financial statements as well as to
prepare you for more advanced financial statement analysis courses. The objective is not to train you as
an accountant but rather to help you become an informed user of financial statements. Classes will include
interactive lectures and discussions about financial accounting principles and practices. Short cases
involving corporate financial statements will also be used to illustrate accounting concepts and to explore
the disclosure strategies of companies.

Course Description
This course focuses on developing participants’ ability to understand accounting information to make business decisions.
Specifically, the class provides an introduction to financial statements and the financial reporting process. Participants
learn skills necessary to become informed users of financial statements. By the end of the course, students should be (i)
comfortable reading and preparing financial statements, and (ii) familiar with common business transactions and how
these flow into financial statements.
Classes consist of lectures along with case discussions intended to illustrate key concepts and provide opportunities to
practice recording business transactions and constructing financial statements. Students are expected to complete reading
assignments, quizzes, homework assignments, and cases in preparation for each class. This course prepares students for
more advanced financial statement analysis courses.
