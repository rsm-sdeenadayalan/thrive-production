# MGT 414 — Rady Action Project

Rady MBA · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course provides external consulting experience through hands-on work with a
corporate client. It uses an interdisciplinary approach to project execution and
presentation, with lectures, weekly faculty and client meetings, and a final oral
presentation and written executive summary. Projects are sponsored and guided by clients
from the business community.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- engaging clients to clarify business problems
- structuring problems using frameworks
- conducting research and analysis
- developing and selecting feasible solutions
- managing client expectations and communication
- delivering professional oral presentations
- writing executive summaries
- collaborating effectively in teams

## Topics covered

management consulting, client management, project management, business communication, team dynamics, problem structuring, research and analysis, solution development, client presentations, executive summary writing.

## Tools used

canvas.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Graded on team contract, project charter, weekly client status reports and work plans, case analysis, presentation slides, executive summary, work cited, and peer reviews.

## Workload

Heavy. Estimated 11-12 hours per week from research, problem solving, weekly status reports and work plans, individual assignments, team presentation slides, final presentation practice, and final written deliverables.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course provides you with external consulting experience which utilizes and builds upon the knowledge and
skills acquired through work experience and in this course. The course enriches the Rady
experiential learning component and provides an interdisciplinary approach to project execution and
presentation. Projects are sponsored and guided by clients from the business community.
OBJECTIVES
• Gain a practical understanding of management consulting via hands-on experience working with
a corporate client.
• Gain an in-depth understanding of advanced management techniques, including project and
client management, communication, and team dynamics.
• Perfect use of business communications skills, oral and written, throughout the quarter,
culminating in a final oral presentation and written executive summary.
• Learn best practices for aligning client expectations with realistic project goals and objectives
through effective communication and consensus building.
• Create and deliver a well thought out and professionally executed final deliverable to the client.

PROJECTS
A Rady Action Project is sponsored by a company or organization. A typical project includes the following
activities and can encompass a business problem that an MBA can solve.
1)
Engaging clients to clarify the problem – interviewing, questioning.
b. Context
c. Background
d. Symptom
2)
Structure the problem – fishbone diagram, etc.
a) Determine Cause
b) Revise if needed
3)
Analyze research –think of all the possibilities
a) Develop solutions – how does it relate to root cause
4)
Select a solution
a) Feasibility
b) Best Fit
5)
Ongoing communication with the client throughout, sharing your insights and solutions, and revising
as needed
Teams will consist of 3-6 students who receive project guidance from a project leader provided by the company
and mentoring from your faculty. In addition, teams receive business communications coaching from industry and
academic professionals. Students must bring their business skills and professionalism to the team to achieve
project objectives. You are representing the Rady brand with our sponsors who are also employers, interested in
hiring UCSD students.

Written Assignments & Discussions
1. Class Attendance (I): You will receive 2 pts each week for attendance.
2. Class Discussions (G): You will also receive 4 pts for participating in each of our in-class discussion and one
online discussion (Week 1, 3, 4, 6).
3. Team Contract (G): Outlines the roles and procedures the team will follow throughout the project.
4. Project Charter (G) – Outlines the current client situation (business opportunity or challenge), project goals,
scope of work, milestones and associated timelines, deliverables, risk assessment, and the basic terms and
conditions under which the work will be performed.
5. Work Plan(G) – The detailed weekly breakdown of the scope of work milestones. WP is for internal use only
and not shared with the client. You may input your project into ChatGPT and ask it to break down your
tasks and milestones for your work plan. Although I will need names and details on the project plan.
6. Client Status Reports (G) – The client status report is due to the client 24 hours in advance. This document
allows the client to stay up to date on the progress of the project. You will also submit the client status report
weekly into canvas.
7. Case Assignment (I)- Review the case Skills for Tomorrow: A Management Team in Crisis and answer the
questions; submit two-pages to Canvas.
8. Executive Summary (G) - Approximately 1–2-page summary of project purpose, methodology, analysis,
findings, actionable recommendations, and next steps, as applicable. The key is to be concise.
9. Works Cited (G) – Reference listings, providing all sources utilized during the quarter. This document will be
graded based on the criteria for proper APA citation.
10. Final Presentations (G & I):
Internal Final Presentation to Faculty –Final opportunity to refine presentation before giving final client
presentation. Each team has a scheduled time
Final Client Presentation – Final presentation to the client. This is the culmination of the project work.
Your final internal and client presentation will be graded on your ability to communicate your results clearly
and effectively translate them to your audience, your pace and transitions, and the relevance and quality of
your analysis and explanation of research, as well as its connection to business goals.
11. PROFESSIONALISM
Professionalism is defined as participating actively in every aspect of your project and operating as an effective
team member and student. The professionalism grade is drawn from peer and faculty grading.
PEER GRADING (I)
At weeks 5 and 10 of the quarter, each student will complete and submit a “peer evaluation” form on Canvas.
Students will be asked to provide feedback on the contributions of their teammates. Submitting this form is
considered part of your class participation grade.
FACULTY GRADING
Faculty will provide feedback about each team member’s contribution to the project.
