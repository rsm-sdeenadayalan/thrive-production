# MGT 406 — Leadership, Ethics, and Team Management

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA25, SU25, FA26, SU26

## What this course covers

Students learn and develop key leadership skills through case analysis, articles, in-
class exercises, discussion, and written assignments. The course covers self-awareness,
personality, emotional intelligence, ethics, team dynamics, conflict negotiation, and
power and influence, with an emphasis on applying research-based knowledge to real
managerial challenges.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- applying leadership research to managerial challenges
- assessing personal leadership strengths and weaknesses
- setting and pursuing leadership development goals
- analyzing team dynamics using course frameworks
- negotiating effectively
- exercising ethical leadership
- managing conflict
- giving and receiving feedback
- applying research-based knowledge to leadership challenges
- assessing personal leadership strengths and development areas

## Topics covered

self-awareness, leadership styles, personality assessment, emotional intelligence, ethics, team dynamics, conflict negotiation, power and influence, other-awareness, personality and skills, conflict and negotiation, ethical leadership.

## Tools used

canvas, slack, everest simulation, bfi personality assessment, bessi personality assessment, bfi, bessi.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grading is 40% participation (assessed by peers and professor), 35% Personal Development Plan, and 25% Team Paper.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes class participation, a personal development plan, and a team paper, alongside weekly readings, cases, online modules, and team activities.

oveis@ucsd.edu; Office hours: by appointment

Course description. In this course, students will learn and develop key leadership skills.
Learning methodology. Case analysis, articles, in-class exercises and discussion, written assignments.
Course objectives. (1) Apply research-based knowledge to challenges facing managers and leaders; (2)
Gain self-awareness of your current level of proficiency in key skills; (3) Accelerate development and
progress toward one individualized leadership skill/goal; (4) Apply course concepts to your study groups
throughout the MBA program.
Course website. rady.instructure.com (Canvas)
Course materials. (1) Course Reader; (2) Additional materials provided on Canvas; (3) Slack
Attendance and technology. Full on-time attendance and appropriate use of technology are required.
No AI-derived work (e.g., chatgpt) is permitted in this course.
How to prep cases. Read carefully; note important points you would like to discuss in class; put
yourself in the shoes of the main protagonist(s), and consider how your decision making and actions
would proceed under similar circumstances; consider analogies to your own work experiences, and
prepare to draw these parallels in class and/or on Slack.
How to prep readings. Consider how each article relates to its accompanying case; reflect on how the
lessons from the article might apply to your current/future work environment.
How you are graded. 40% participation, 35% PDP, 25% Team Paper.
Participation. Your class contributions are extremely important to your and your classmates’
learning. At the end of the course, your classmates (50%) and your professor (50%) will grade your
participation. Your professor will consult with your TA and consider attendance and completion of
ungraded assignments. Each of your classmates will respond to a prompt similar to the following:
“Please choose a fair class participation grade for each student in your class. Consider the following
factors as you see fit: How was the quality of the student’s contributions in full class discussions, small
group discussions, and on Slack? Were their comments useful, on point, and/or controversial in a
productive way? Did they share relevant experiences from their own background and work history? Did
they listen carefully to their classmates, and engage them in thoughtful discussions? Were they wellprepared for class? To what extent did they provide value to your and others’ experience in the class?”
Personal Development Plan (PDP). An individually-completed plan for your leadership
development. It contains (A) a self portrait, (B) a goal, and (C) an action plan for obtaining your goal.
Details are available on the course website.

advice for how to improve your group’s performance in the future.
