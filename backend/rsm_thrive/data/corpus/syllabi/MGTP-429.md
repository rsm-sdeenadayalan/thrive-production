# MGTP 429 — Advanced Topics in Accounting and Auditing

Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

Advanced accounting course examining complex external reporting issues under US GAAP,
including revenue recognition, share-based compensation, R&D costs, equity method
accounting, consolidations, segment and interim reporting, foreign currency translation,
and SEC reporting. Students apply concepts through problem sets and real-company
disclosure analysis, with references to IFRS differences. The course aligns with the BAR
section of the CPA exam.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- applying us gaap to complex accounting transactions
- preparing consolidated financial statements including noncontrolling interests
- performing intercompany eliminations
- determining consolidation outcomes for variable interest entities
- analyzing real-company disclosures
- explaining key differences between us gaap and ifrs
- applying sec reporting requirements

## Topics covered

revenue recognition, share-based compensation, business combinations and consolidation, intercompany transactions, variable interest entities, equity method accounting, segment and interim reporting, foreign currency translation, sec reporting requirements, us gaap and ifrs differences.

​DESCRIPTION​
​Advanced Accounting builds on intermediate accounting concepts and examines complex​
​external‑reporting issues that arise in practice. This course will further exam complex accounting topics​
i​ncluding revenue recognition, share based compensation, R&D costs and will also cover equity method​
​accounting, consolidation, segment and interim reporting, foreign‑currency translation, and SEC reporting​
​requirements. While this course is oriented toward US GAAP, we will make references to IFRS concepts​
​and highlight key differences.​
​The purpose of financial reporting is to provide meaningful information to investors, creditors, managers,​
​and other stakeholders so they can assess a company’s performance, prospects, and risk. Through​
​problem sets and real‑company disclosure analysis, students will learn to apply US GAAP and​
​understand how accounting policy choices shape financial reports and the stakeholder assessments.​
​This course is generally aligned with the contents of the BAR section of the CPA exam. However, we will​
​not be covering financial statements of employment benefit plans or government entities.​
​COURSE OUTCOMES/OBJECTIVES​
​By the end of this course, students will:​
​●​ ​Become familiar with the standard setting bodies (FASB, IASB, and the SEC) and the process by​
​which standards are set.​
​●​ ​Able to explain the conceptual framework for complex financial reporting analysis​
​●​ ​Understand the objectives of financial statements.​
​●​ ​Have an appreciation of certain key differences between US GAAP and IFRS.​
​●​ ​Possess an awareness of the lines of service within public accounting firms.​
​●​ ​Be confident with the contents of the BAR section of the CPA exam.​
​●​ ​Be able to prepare consolidated financial statements, including noncontrolling interests, and​
​perform intercompany eliminations.​
​●​ ​Determine consolidation outcomes for Variable Interest Entities (VIEs).​
​●​ ​Gain a deeper understanding of certain complex accounting issues including revenue recognition,​
​share-based compensation etc.​
​●​ ​Be knowledgeable of SEC reporting requirements.​
