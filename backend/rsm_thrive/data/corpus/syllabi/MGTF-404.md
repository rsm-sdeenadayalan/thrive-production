# MGTF 404 — Financial Econometrics and Empirical Methods

**4 units** · Rady MS Finance (MFin) · offered FA25, FA26

## What this course covers

This course bridges the gap between theoretical financial models and empirical financial
econometrics, covering major accomplishments and failures in empirical finance over the
last quarter century. Students read seminal works and complete empirical exercises that
require writing their own procedures and using real market data.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- implementing financial econometrics techniques
- building econometric routines from scratch
- analyzing real market data
- testing asset pricing models
- estimating volatility models
- conducting predictive regressions
- applying generalized method of moments
- interpreting seminal empirical finance papers

## Topics covered

time series analysis, ordinary least squares, predictive regressions, market efficiency, cross-sectional regressions, asset pricing models, volatility modeling, maximum likelihood estimation, generalized method of moments, cointegration.

## Tools used

python, matlab, r, canvas, zoom, chatgpt.

## Prerequisites

A quantitatively based “Investments” class is a prerequisite for the course. Familiarity with computers. Students should be able to download and manipulate data. Knowledge of a programming language or statistical package (e.g., Python, Matlab, R) is required. Knowledge of “canned” statistical programs such as Eviews, SAS, or Stata is not enough. Students will be expected to build their own library of routines and procedures from the ground up.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course is designed to help students bridge the gap between theoretical financial models
and the world of empirics and financial econometrics. The 10-week class will take students
through the major accomplishments (and failures) of empirical finance and financial
econometrics during the last quarter of a century. Students will have to read some of the truly
seminal works in financial econometrics. Empirical exercises will require students to write
their own procedures and to use real market data. These exercises will help us all truly
internalize the content of the course. Upon completion of this course, students should:
•
•
•
•

Have a good picture of the main financial econometrics techniques
Have a good understanding of the important topics in empirical finance
Be able to implement many of the widely used methods in modern empirical finance
Have an in-depth knowledge in an area of their choice.

Prerequisites:
• A quantitatively based “Investments” class is a prerequisite for the course.
• Familiarity with computers. Students should be able to download and manipulate
data.
• Knowledge of a programming language or statistical package (e.g., Python, Matlab,
R) is required. Knowledge of “canned” statistical programs such as Eviews, SAS, or
Stata is not enough. Students will be expected to build their own library of routines
and procedures from the ground up.

ASSIGNMENTS AND GRADING
The grading policy is as follows:
1. Written/Computer Exercises (40%) – should be done in pre-assigned groups of no more
than 5 people. One written assignment per group is to be turned in on time.
2. Weekly Quizzes (10%) – We will assign weekly quizzes after each lecture on Canvas.
They will help you keep up with the material.
3. Participation and/or peer evaluations (10%)
4. Final Examination (40%)—The final examination may involve manipulation of data. It will
be administered during Finals week. It may be in-person or online.
To prevent one bad performance from affecting your grade, I will drop the lowest written
exercise and weekly quiz grade.
GETTING TO KNOW YOU
High-paced lectures are not the ideal platform for interaction. I like to get to know my students.
I will schedule 20 mins appointments with each group outside of normal lecture hours. During
these appointments, I would like to get to know you better. Please be prepared to say a few
words about yourself -- your background, your interests, and your goals – anything that will
help me get to know you. These “get to know you” appointments will be scheduled during
weeks 2-7. Groups will be able to pick a time on a sign-up sheet. The appointments can be
done either in-person or via Zoom. First-come, first-served!
GENERATIVE AI POLICY
You can use ChatGPT or other generative AI tools to complete the coding or other parts of the
assignments. However, you are responsible for the final version of your assignments. Excuses
of the sort “I got it wrong because of ChatGPT” or “I copied the answer from ChatGPT even
though I cannot understand it” will not be accepted.
