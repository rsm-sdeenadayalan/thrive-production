# MGT 421 — The Board of Directors, the CEO, & Corporate Governance

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered SU26

## What this course covers

This course introduces the legal, political, and practical relationships that govern how
businesses operate at the highest level of management. It covers the evolution of the
modern corporation, the structure and duties of boards of directors, conflicts among
boards, CEOs, and shareholders, and how to research and evaluate corporate governance
practices. The hybrid format combines in-person sessions, video lectures, readings,
discussions, and a team proxy research project.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing proxy statements
- evaluating corporate governance practices
- researching company governance documents
- assessing board and ceo compensation
- forming voting recommendations
- understanding board duties and legal obligations
- discussing current governance developments

## Topics covered

corporate governance, boards of directors, ceo roles and responsibilities, shareholder relations, ceo compensation, esg, board independence, proxy analysis, startup boards, corporate governance best practices.

## Tools used

canvas, powerpoint, internet research, video streaming platforms.

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Graded on four written assignments, weekly discussion participation, and a team proxy research project and paper.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
The Board of Directors, the CEO and Corporate Governance is designed as a 30-hour course to
introduce students to the legal, political, and practical relationships that govern how businesses operate
at the highest level of management. Upon completing the course, students will have an understanding of
the myriad relationships, agendas, and conflicts that come into play in managing, overseeing, or investing
in a significant business enterprise as well as the ability to consider the appropriateness of the corporate
governance structure within public or private business entities. The course will include discussions of the
evolution of the modern corporation and corporate governance; the structure, duties and responsibilities
of boards of directors in primarily U.S. publicly-traded companies; the key areas of conflict between
boards of directors, Chief Executive Officers, and shareholders as each pursues its individual mission;
and, exercises in researching the sources to obtain information and evaluations on an individual
company’s corporate governance practices as well as determining the “best practices” in corporate
governance.
All class reading materials, lecture slides, and written assignments will be available in Canvas. All written
assignments are to be submitted through Canvas.
This class is a hybrid class, consisting of three in-person classes of four hours each (July 19, August 16,
and September 13). During each week between in-person classes, video lectures and other materials
have been assigned to promote engagement and continuous learning. Because our in-person classes
are quite long – from 8:00 am to noon - we will try to take short breaks every 90 minutes or so.

COURSE OBJECTIVES
The objective of the course is to provide students with an understanding of the relationships among
shareholders, managers, and boards and how to research and draw their own opinions on an
organization’s corporate governance structure and practices.

COURSE ASSIGNMENTS
Course assignments will include weekly readings and four written assignments of three to five questions
on the topics covered. The written assignments will be considered as part of the class grade. Questions
to be answered will be posted on Canvas at least one week prior to their due date. Students’ written
assignments will be submitted using the appropriate class drop box on Canvas prior to the
commencement of the class for which they are due.
The course will include a team research and evaluation project and paper to be completed and submitted
in the last week of the term. The team project will involve small teams of students who will select a
publicly traded company to use as their study subject in evaluating various areas of corporate
governance. Criteria for the selection of the company will include minimum market capitalization ($2
billion), U.S. filing status for at least two years on either the New York Stock Exchange, the American

student, member of the student’s immediate family, or the instructor. Throughout the course teams will
make presentations on various aspects of corporate governance. At the conclusion of the course, each
team will submit a presentation to the class as to how a client should vote on each issue in their
company’s proxy statement along with a written paper providing more detailed analysis of the “whys” for
their recommendations. Details on the team project are posted on Canvas.
This class is geared to maximum participation and interaction between students and the instructor. As a
result, class participation will have a significant bearing upon the final grade.

developing an independent opinion as to the appropriateness of an organization’s governance structure

THE USE OF AI FOR RESEARCH IS ENCOURAGED IN THIS CLASS
The use of AI tools such as ChatGPT, Bing Chat, Bard, Claude, etc. is encouraged in this class. These
tools have considerable potential to streamline and augment our work and you should use them for this
class with the following caveats and conditions:
1. These tools should augment your work, not replace it. For instance, large language models can
improve the quality of your writing, identify new sources, provide high-level summaries of complex
ideas or terms, and pressure test your arguments for logical inconsistencies. These tools can help
you come up with new ideas, overcome writer’s block, or refine your original ideas. You should not
use Generative AI tools to generate content that you simply put your name on. This constitutes an
academic integrity violation, much like taking another individual’s work and misrepresenting it as your
own. Note that I run all of the written assignment and final exam questions through AI engines and

3. Large language models have documented issues with hallucination and inaccuracy (e.g., generating
