# MGTF 420 — Financial Markets and Institutions

Rady MQF (Master of Quantitative Finance) · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course examines US and global financial markets and institutions, focusing on how
money and credit flow through the economy and how financial regulation shapes that flow.
It covers interest rates, exchange rates, asset prices, banking, shadow banking, and
financial crises, and is taught through lectures, discussions, case problems, individual
literature review, and a group research project.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing the structure and players of the us financial system
- forecasting interest rates and exchange rates
- forecasting asset prices and market turning points
- assessing credit default risk
- evaluating financial regulation and monetary policy impacts
- making investment and funding decisions
- analyzing financial crises and bank failures

## Topics covered

money supply and credit flow, structure of us financial markets and institutions, bond and money markets, interest rate determination and yield curve analysis, stock market valuation, foreign exchange markets, banking and shadow banking credit markets, financial regulation and monetary policy, derivative markets and risk management, financial crisis case studies.

DESCRIPTION
•

The USA is the largest economy in the world. The USA is the land of millionaires. While
there may be more than one reason for that, the number one reason is the supply of
money and credit in the economy through its financial markets and institutions.

•

This course is about US and global financial markets and institutions. Money is said to
be the fuel or the life blood of any business, family, and economy. Financial markets and
institutions create, move, and regulate the money flow in the economy. Each of the
business and economic variables like investment, employment, GDP, inflation, exchange
rate is a function, among others, of money supply and credit availability in the economy.
Financial Regulation plays an equally important role by influencing money supply and
credit flow in the economy and thereby helping manage the economic and financial
health of the economy.

•

As prospective business owners, executives, and finance specialists, it will be beneficial
to understand US and global financial markets and institutions. It is like knowing the air
strip before flying an airplane. The funding and investment decisions, in every walk of
life, are highly influenced by financial markets and institutions. Based on my global
experience with the developed and less developed economies, I am inclined to say that
a good index of the standard of living of the people in a country is the quality of assets of
the banking system of their country.

•

In this course we shall focus on questions like what is the state of money and credit in
the US economy? Who affects it and how? How could markets and institutions be

efficient and effective to help sustainably grow US economy? The 2007-09 global
financial crisis and events of COVID-19 have brought the role of financial markets and
institutions to the fore. Two US banks, namely, Silicon Valley Bank and Signature Bank
failed in 2023. We shall analyze these two bank failures as case studies for
understanding the importance and challenges of managing financial institutions.
•

The prime focus of this course is to help you master skills of forecasting interest rates,
exchange rates, asset prices, market downturns and upturns, credit defaults, etc. to help
manage the finances of your business better.

•

This course should also interest those who want to understand the sources of generating
passive income. You may have two sources of income, active and passive. Active
income you generate by actively pursuing a job or business. Passive income you may
generate by making your money work for you when you sleep. Bond markets, stock
markets, foreign exchange markets, real estate markets, derivatives markets can all be
sources of passive income. Rich become rich by generating passive income in addition
to active income. “The major fortunes in America have been made in land”, said John D
Rockefeller.

INTENDED LEARNING OBJECTIVES
At the end of this course, participants will be able to:
1. Analyze ‘big picture’ of the US financial system; its players and their role; and its global
context.
2. Analyze history, trends and developments in the US financial markets and institutions.
3. Foresee where the US financial system is moving up to, its challenges.
4. Foresee next economic upturns and downturns. (downturns are great time for wealth
building)
5. Facilitate financial decisions of investment and funding in businesses, government, and
families.
