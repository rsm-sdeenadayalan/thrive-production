# MGT 408 — Finance

Rady MBA · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course provides a foundation in fundamental finance concepts, including estimating
future cash flows, employing appropriate discount rates, and using them to value assets
and projects. Topics include present value, risk and return, leverage, free cash flows,
and capital budgeting using WACC and APV approaches. The course uses lectures, problem
sets, a case, quizzes, and a final exam.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- valuing assets and projects using discounted cash flows
- calculating present values of cash-flow streams
- estimating discount rates using the capital asset pricing model
- analyzing the effect of leverage on returns
- estimating free cash flows for project valuation
- applying wacc and apv approaches to capital budgeting
- evaluating real options

## Topics covered

present value and net present value, bond and stock valuation, mortgage valuation, risk-return tradeoff, diversification, capital asset pricing model, leverage and modigliani-miller theorem, free cash flow estimation, capital budgeting with wacc and apv, real options, capital structure, corporate governance.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course is designed to provide you with a strong foundation in the fundamental concepts of finance. Students
will learn how to estimate future cash-flows, employ appropriate discount rates, and use them to value assets and
projects.
Topics include: the present value framework and the net present value rule; applying present value calculations to
mortgages, bonds and stocks; the risk-return tradeoff, diversification and discount rates; leverage and its effect on
returns; estimation of free cash-flows for project valuation; and capital budgeting using the WACC and APV
approaches. Time permitting, the course will also introduce more advanced topics like real options, capital structure
and corporate governance.
Prerequisites: None

Assignment due dates are given on the course website and are non-negotiable – in the interest of fairness and
as required by the honor code, all students should face the same set of deadlines.
Problem Sets: The problem sets are a team assignment. Your answers should be very clear and show both the
solution and how you obtained it; an undocumented spreadsheet is not considered a sufficient solution.
The problem sets are meant to be challenging. They are an opportunity to learn the material and explore
it at a much deeper level. I expect you to try your best, but you don't have to get everything perfectly right to do
well on them.
Case report: This should have a maximum length of 3 pages of text with an additional 3 pages of exhibits (for a
maximum of 6 pages). The case report should use single space, 12 point type with 1 inch margins on all sides,
and should be written in essay form, not in the form of individually answering the questions that accompany the
case. Senior management is the intended audience. Finally, do not summarize the case situation in the report –
assume everyone has read the case.
Class Participation: You are expected to be prepared for class, and constructively participate in class
discussions. I will cold call in class, and your responses will affect your class participation grade. It is in your
interest to attend class and it may affect participation grade.
Peer review: peer evaluations conducted at the end of the quarter incentivize you to be constructive teammembers.
The course moves very quickly. Here are some suggestions that may be useful:


Before class: Read the course handouts/slides. These focus on the most important concepts and
provide examples to illustrate them. They are posted ahead of class, and reading through them is a great
way to prepare for class. They are also largely self-contained and cover the material you are responsible
for in the exams.



During class: Ask questions. If there is something that you do not understand during class - either the
specifics of an example, or terminology that you have not heard before - please stop me and ask. This
discussion will also help put the concepts into context and will help your classmates understand the
material more thoroughly.



After class: Review the course handouts and supplement with the textbook. The readings in the book
can deepen understanding and provide additional context. Reading the book and working through the
problems are a great way to master a specific topic or concept you may be struggling with.
