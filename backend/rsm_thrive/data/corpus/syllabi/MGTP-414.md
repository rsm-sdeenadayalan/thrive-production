# MGTP 414 — Business Law

**4 units** · Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered FA25, FA26

## What this course covers

This course provides a general understanding of core business law concepts and practical
exercises for evaluating how law influences business decision-making. It covers
regulation of business actors, contracts, risk creation and mitigation, and resolution
of legal disputes. The material also incorporates content tested on the CPA Exam's REG
section.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing contract provisions
- evaluating legal influence on business decisions
- applying business formation principles
- managing business risk
- resolving business disputes
- identifying intellectual property types
- understanding liability exposure
- reading legal documents

## Topics covered

business law, contract law, agency law, business formation, securities regulation, litigation and dispute resolution, risk management, intellectual property, data privacy, negligence and liability.

DESCRIPTION
This course is designed to provide you with a general understanding of core business law concepts and
provide practical exercises that will help you evaluate how the law influences business decision-making.
You will learn the basics of how the law shapes businesses as well as how it can be leveraged in a
commercial context. Specifically, we will spend significant time looking at how the law regulates the
behavior of actors within a business, significant issues with respect to contracts, how law can be utilized
to create and mitigate business risk, and how legal disputes are resolved. The subject matter of the class
incorporates content tested on the CPA Exam’s “REG” section, but is not comprehensive of tested
concepts. The course is intended to establish facility with core concepts and develop a practical
understanding of them as well as the ability to apply these concepts in the real world.

Group Project 1 will require students to collaborate for purposes of creating a business and addressing

strategy for the business.
Group Project 2 will require students to apply principles of risk management and dispute resolution to

for managing risk, and deploy that strategy against another group with conflicting interests.
A third smaller project on intellectual project can be done individually or as a group. The strength of your

feedback in evaluating your group work. In other words, your grades for group work depend on both your
collective and individual contributions.
Quizzes
Quizzes will be administered through Canvas. There will be five (5) quizzes throughout the course. Your
lowest quiz score will be dropped and only the highest four (4) scores will count toward your final grade in
the class.
You will have until 11:59 p.m. on the deadline to finish each quiz. The quizzes will be multiple choice. My
goal with the quizzes is to reinforce core concepts. You may assume that the amount of time spent
during lecture on a particular subject matter is indicative of its likelihood to appear on a quiz. Each
student will be allowed to drop his/her/their lowest score.
Final Exam
The final exam will be a combination of multiple choice and essay questions. I will be testing your ability
think critically and apply the material we have reviewed. You will receive guidance and instructions
regarding the format of the exams.
Class Participation and Preparation
Class participation comprises approximately 10% of the student’s grade. Participation is measured, in
part, by the student’s willingness to volunteer meaningful insights during class discussion or ask a
question that assists the understanding of other students. My expectation is every student will be familiar
with the assigned prepared for class. If there is a compelling reason why you are not prepared to discuss
the assigned material for class, you should let me know in advance.
Participation credit can also be earned by presenting for the class a news article or current event that is
relevant to the subject matter of the course. If you come across a recent news article, please email at
least twenty-four (24) hours before the beginning of class. If appropriate for discussion in class, the
student will briefly summarize and the news article and receive participation credit.
Note that each class session is an opportunity to participate in discussion and/or present a current event.
Given there are ten (10) class sessions, there are ample opportunities to ensure full credit toward your
final grade for class participation.
