# MGTP 434 — Financial Statement Analysis

Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered WI26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

Teaches students how to read and use financial statements to understand firms' current
and past performance, forecast future profitability, and estimate intrinsic value.
Covers a structured approach to measuring intrinsic value using accounting numbers,
along with the value and limitations of financial statement analysis. The course
includes lectures, case discussions, homework, and a final project valuing a publicly
traded company.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- reading financial statements
- analyzing profitability
- forecasting future profitability
- estimating intrinsic value
- assessing financial risk
- building financial forecast models
- valuing businesses
- applying residual income valuation

financial statement analysis, ratio analysis, profitability analysis, dupont decomposition, risk assessment, financial reporting risk, financial modeling, valuation methods, residual income model, business valuation.

DESCRIPTION AND OBJECTIVES
Companies communicate with stakeholders through their financial statements. The objective of this course
is to teach students how to read and use financial statements to understand firms’ current and past
performance, to forecast their future profitability, and to estimate their value.
To accomplish this objective, the course will provide:
• Experience and tools for reading and using financial statements to make business and
investment decisions;
• A structured approach to measure intrinsic value using accounting numbers;
• An appreciation for the value and limitations of financial statement analysis in specific contexts.

SCHEDULE
Week 1: Introduction to the course. Corporate objectives, strategy, and competitive environment
Week 2: Profitability and ratio analysis
Week 3: Profitability in the context of the business model. Analysis of financial ratios
Week 4: Advanced DuPont decomposition. Assessing risk from financial statements
Week 5: Midterm.
Week 6: Financial reporting risk.
Week 7: Financial modeling. A financial forecast model
Week 8: Valuation methods
Week 9: Valuing a business. Residual income model.
Week 10: Group project presentations

Reading Assignments

Class Engagement
I expect students to attend class and be prepared to participate. There are a number of ways for you to
engage: answer (and ask) questions in class. Student-led discussions of the cases and reading materials
are an integral part of this course.

Excellent participation means that you have been on time to all classes, have routinely volunteered good
comments indicating that you have prepared for the class, and have answered questions when the
opportunity arises. The class's overall learning experience has improved because of your comments.
Good participation means that you have been on time to class, have occasionally volunteered comments,
and have sometimes answered questions posed to the class.
Poor participation means that you have missed noticeable amounts of class time, or have had little or
nothing to say. You come in late, leave during class, or generally disrupt the class.
Homework
There are seven case assignments worth 5 points each. The assignments are to be submitted electronically
on Canvas before the beginning of class on the due date given below. Each assignment will specify whether
it is to be completed individually or in teams of up to 4. The assignments are intended to provide you with
practice at what I think will be most useful without “grade pressure,” and to prepare you for class
discussions. If your work reflects a strong effort, you will receive full credit. If you do not complete the
assignment on time, you will receive no credit. The assignments will not be returned to you; however, the
in-class discussions will provide you with feedback on the solution.
Homework is due at 11:59 p.m. the day before class, the week after it is assigned. I will drop the lowest
score.
Exams
The mid-quarter exam will take ~2 hours and will be held in class in Week 5.
The exam is independent. You will find it helpful to bring a calculator. I do not return exams, but students
can arrange a time to come to my office to go over them.
Final Project
The final project involves valuing a publicly traded company. Specific instructions and examples will be
provided in Week 6.
