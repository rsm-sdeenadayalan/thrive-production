# MGT 439 — Topics in Organizational Behavior: Leadership in Practice: Coaching and Inclusion

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered FA25, SP26, SU26, SP27

## What this course covers

This highly participatory course equips managers with skills to communicate
strategically in person and virtually, across team meetings, boardrooms, public forums,
and investor pitches. Through lectures, demonstrations, creative exercises,
improvisations, peer feedback, and individual coaching, students develop and rehearse
presentations for contexts such as attracting talent, gaining stakeholder buy-in,
pitching clients, and motivating teams.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- analyzing an organization's competitive arena and context
- identifying knowledge stocks and flows
- visualizing and applying dynamic knowledge
- devising bases for competitive advantage
- diagnosing knowledge management pathologies
- recommending knowledge management interventions
- leading case discussions
- presenting consulting findings professionally
- delivering persuasive presentations
- crafting authentic narratives

## Topics covered

knowledge competition, knowledge analysis, knowledge dynamics, knowledge technology, knowledge assessment, competitive advantage, machine knowledge, case analysis, knowledge management in business, knowledge management in government, knowledge management in nonprofit organizations, strategic communication.

## Tools used

powerpoint, chatgpt, bard, claude, large language models, canvas, studynet.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Grade is based on class engagement (10%), individual homework (15%), a partner coaching exam and reflection (15%), and final projects including a de-biasing project and leadership vision (50%); attendance at all weekend sessions is required to pass.

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes class participation, case leadership, a team consulting project with a 10-15 page report and presentation, and an individual presentation.

DESCRIPTION
Even more than money, knowledge is the most important asset in your organization. Without timely
knowledge to innovate powerfully, collaborate dynamically, and pivot preemptively, money is simply
wasted on the weak organization waiting to fail. The problem is that our most powerful knowledge—
associated with intelligent machines and people—is situated dynamically and must be managed actively
to flow where, when and how it can be applied best. The organization that learns to harness its
knowledge strategically can gain and maintain competitive advantage on that basis. In this course we
learn how to compete through knowledge. We analyze strategy and differing bases of competitive
advantage, and we discover how to mobilize the most powerful knowledge in the most appropriate
manner. Our learning develops through three integrated instruction methods: 1) We integrate wellaccepted frameworks for understanding strategy with state-of-the-art methods for analyzing, visualizing
and applying dynamic knowledge; 2) we interact intensively through group and class discussion to weave
in both students’ and other organizations’ experiences; and 3) we apply our learning through case
analysis.

ASSIGNMENTS
Reading Assignments & Class Participation
Students are encouraged to collaborate and exchange ideas with classmates. However, each person is
expected to complete every reading and be prepared to participate individually in class discussions and
exercises.
There is no substitute for participating in class, and every student without official university
accommodation is expected to attend every classroom session. However, many graduate students have
full and busy lives outside of the university, and work or family demands can interrupt class participation
at times. Hence this course has a no-fault attendance policy: You can miss one class session with a
relatively minor penalty; missing more than two class sessions brings a substantial penalty.
Minicase Discussion
For each minicase discussion, the Instructor or TA will lead a short summary and focus on the case
questions, with particular emphasis on what knowledge is required to succeed. This is part of class
participation.
Case Leadership
Coursework shifts to case applications in business, government and nonprofit organizations in Weeks 6,
7 & 8, respectively, with each corresponding chapter of the text (i.e., Ch 7, 8 & 9) summarizing three short

cases for analysis and class discussion. Think of these as practice for the team consulting project.
Students are to self-organize into small teams (size TBD) and use the exercises at the end of each case
to lead a short class discussion, but the fundamental question to address is: What knowledge is required
to succeed? Every student is expected to participate in leading one case, so the team size will be
approximately 9 (cases) / x (class size). PowerPoint or like presentation apps are appropriate, and
students should plan to use either the classroom computer or their personal device.
Team Consulting Project
Students are to self-organize, work together in small teams (size TBD), and turn in one deliverable for the
entire group. Please be inclusive in terms of encouraging students to join established teams, especially
classmates whom you may not know well. Team diversity is a strength, and very few of us will have total
control over our team composition in practice. The Instructor reserves the right to facilitate team
formation.
The assignment involves evaluating an operational organization in the field. Through the role of
consultant in a strategic context, use course principles to evaluate the subject organization’s knowledge
flows, diagnose any pathologies you detect, and recommend appropriate interventions. Submit a
professional written report, and lead a professional classroom presentation.
The report should include five sections: 1) introduction outlining the purpose and focal organization of the
evaluation; 2) background summarizing key elements of knowledge management and strategy; 3)
diagnosis of pathologies, accompanied by corresponding principles (e.g., from the textbook, induced from
analysis of the case) used for evaluation; 4) recommended interventions that correspond closely to the
pathologies and that identify corresponding principles used for evaluation; 5) summary of the factors and
activities that you feel will be critical to successful implementation of your recommendations, along with
suggested references for the organization leaders and managers to learn more about how to address and
manage knowledge.
The case analysis report should be roughly 10 – 15 pages in length (i.e., double spaced, 12 point font, 1inch margins) including all text, tables, graphics and references. The report should be: 1) written in a
professional manner; 2) based on factual observations and course principles; 3) persuasive; 4)
constructive; and 5) informative.
The case presentation will be the final class session. Each team should plan around 12 minutes for
prepared remarks and 3 minutes for Q&A. PowerPoint or like presentation apps are appropriate, and
students should plan to use either the classroom computer or their personal device. The presentation
should be 1) professional; 2) engaging; 3) persuasive; 4) responsive; and 5) complete.
Individual Presentation
For the Individual Presentation, students work individually and summarize very briefly one example of a
contemporary organization that is competing effectively on the basis of its knowledge. The summary
should describe the focal organization, its competitors and the competitive arena or environment. It
should also outline the key knowledge used by the focal organization and explain how such knowledge
enables competitive advantage. Please plan one minute max. Exceeding your time will result in a
deduction of points. Simply present from your seat. No slides or props. The presentation should be 1)
professional; 2) engaging; 3) current; 4) knowledge focused; and 5) competition focused.
Note: Students are encouraged to use one or more large language (AI) models (e.g., Chat GPT, Bard,
Claude) to facilitate this assignment.

Course Description:
In today’s lightning-fast global business environment, the ability to communicate strategically—
in person and virtually, in a team meeting, boardroom, public forum or a venture capital pitch—
is essential for impactful leadership. This highly participatory course equips current and aspiring
managers with skills and techniques to deliver strategic communications that inspire trust, build
relationships, influence decisions, and drive action.
Managers are called upon to rally teams, secure resources, pitch ideas, respond to challenges and
influence stakeholders. Whether you're addressing a single individual or a large group your
ability to present with clarity, confidence, and authenticity can determine success. The ability to
strategically communicate and invite sustained relationships and collaboration through
communications are invaluable capabilities of effective managers.
This course explores not only the external techniques of high-impact presentations—eye contact,
vocal projection, gesturing—but also the deeper, transformational skills that distinguish great
communicators. You’ll learn to speak from both the mind and heart to directly influence the
minds and hearts of others. You’ll develop confidence in utilizing emotional intelligence and
articulating your unique point of view. Innovative techniques drawn from both and theater and
leadership training will help you connect with audiences in ways that are strategic, memorable
and motivating.
Through a combination of lecture, demonstration, creative exercises, improvisations, peer
feedback, and individual coaching you will develop and refine your communications for a wide
range of business contexts, including:

Attracting top talent to your teams

The people working in a company are the biggest asset to the success of the business. But people are
complicated. Every employee is different and wants to be seen for their unique value. This complexity can be a
challenge. What practices help you develop the conditions where people do their best work?
COURSE OBJECTIVES

Through the two tools of coaching and inclusion, students build their leadership mindset and hands-on skillset. We
examine people-management practices to explore how, as a leader of people, do you:
1. Manage people in order to engage and get the best out of each employee, regardless of their style or
level?
2. Grow your employees and yourself, so that you scale your impact.
3. Create inclusive environments where people feel they belong and are comfortable with creative
friction so they leverage their work to deploy collective genius?

The course sets the stage with a foundational examination of the roles of the manager and the leader. Then
moving into skills development, we start with focus on the individual employee through the tool of coaching.
Students first learn the basic process of coaching others and receive coaching. Then through deliberate practice,
gradually students increase their comfort and agility into ambiguous coaching situations to problem-solve.
Next, looking at groups of people, we explore the paradox of diversity--that diversity among groups of people
leads to higher innovation yet also leads to conflict and exclusion. Students work with both behavior and process
to develop learning environments where everybody thrives. Outcomes such as whether employees feel they
belong, how the culture “feels” to them, and how to balance creative friction can be a result of process and
structure, as well as behavior.
This course also guides people managers to look at leadership through the lens of how they think about and
interact with their people. This examination culminates in a forward-looking leadership vision, values and practices
statement.
INSTRUCTIONAL METHODOLOGY

This class is highly experiential and requires your mental and physical focus. Come to class as well-rested, fed and
prepared as is possible (for a person going to graduate school and working full time).
We will work in three modes: preparation, performance, and reflection. This model comes from the theater, but is
essential in sports, business and any kind of human performance. In more detail:
1. Prepare: Preparation involves practice, routines, setting intention, planning, rehearsal, and experimentation. Part
of performance preparation is determining how you need to prepare.
2. Perform: Performance involves letting go of the preparation, to be present with what is happening in the
moment in order to respond authentically.

Regarding the act of performance, a tennis player cycles serve-by-serve, set-by-set, game-by-game, starting over
and over again. The actor gets on stage night after night and starts from scratch with a new audience. Push
yourself to make mistakes in performance so you learn.
3. Reflect: Reflection involves looking back at what happened, exploring what was meaningful about it and
assessing what you have learned/will do differently, so that you can integrate that learning “next time.”

“The debrief is as important as the game.” – Steve Ballmer
