# MGT 415 — New Venture Development

**4 units** · Rady MBA · offered WI26, WI27

## What this course covers

An action-based practicum focused on assessing the viability of venture ideas and
developing them into sustainable new enterprises, whether in entrepreneurial,
intrapreneurial, or social enterprise contexts. Students learn customer discovery, lean
business model development, and market, financial, and risk evaluation through lectures,
case studies, team projects, presentations, and guest speakers.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- assessing venture viability
- conducting customer discovery
- developing value propositions
- designing business models
- projecting market demand and profit potential
- writing executive summaries and business plans
- delivering new venture pitches
- evaluating growth strategies and valuations

## Topics covered

new venture development, customer discovery, lean business model development, market research, market segmentation and positioning, business model design, financial analysis, risk evaluation, growth strategies, team dynamics.

## Tools used

canvas, zoom.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

Students are evaluated on class participation (15%), homework and exams (20%), and the New Venture Project (65%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded on class participation (15%), homework and exams (20%), and a team New Venture Project (65%), with a fast-paced, project-heavy format.

COURSE DESCRIPTION
This course is designed to be an action-based, hands-on “practicum” that aims to equip you with the training and
knowledge to assess the viability of a venture idea or early stage venture and develop or grow it into a competitive
and sustainable new enterprise or new line of business, whether it is in an entrepreneurial (emerging business –
startup or small), intrapreneurial (established business) or social enterprise context. Students can propose a
venture, work on a classmate’s venture or choose to work with a campus or community innovator.
Specifically, the course provides instruction, practice, advice and mentoring through hands-on activities,
discussion of selected case studies and examples, hypothesis-driven market research, voices of experience, oneon-one advising sessions and team/project immersion time to determine the viability and sustainability of the
business concept or opportunity and develop the elements of a value-creating enterprise.
Emphasis will be placed on methods and approaches for conducting customer discovery and business case
development, hypothesis-driven lean business model development, and market, financial, and risk evaluation and
testing. Deliverables are expected to be data-driven and team-produced. This is a fast-paced course that is
intended to take your venture as far as possible or teach you as much about launching a venture as possible.
Students will learn how to perceive needs, foster innovation, and propose unique products or services in a
business concept that could satisfy those unmet needs. They will also learn about market segmentation and
positioning, as well as market research techniques to test their ideas in a real-world context and to identify
potential customers, suppliers and partners. From this they will learn the definition of a business model and how
it can represent the key operational design decisions that an organization makes to properly address a market
opportunity in a profitable and distinctive manner.
Through case studies, industry examples and discussions, participants will learn the nuances of different business
models, challenges, and opportunities faced by entrepreneurs as they evolve their businesses. Students will
demonstrate their learning of this material through written and oral presentations.
The course will utilize lectures, case studies, team projects, presentations, individual homework and guest
speakers from various innovative industries. The main project and several interim assignments will be completed
in your teams. This quarter-length course is designed for upper division undergraduate students.

CLASS AND TEAM PARTICIPATION
Every class session will involve interaction in the form of class discussion. I expect each student to be prepared
to comment in any class session. I will randomly (e.g. cold call) on students during the ensuing discussion, both
those who raise their hand and those who do not.

EVALUATING CLASS PARTICIPATION
You will be provided a name plaque with your first name by our TA. Please bring the name plaque with you to
every class. This allows our guest lecturers, professor, and TA to easily identify you when participating. Class
participation will be graded on your readiness, willingness and the quality of your comments and their contribution
to the discussion. You are expected to be an active participant throughout the entire class and to contribute to
the quality of the discussion.
TEAM PROJECTS
Every student will participate in a team project. Teams will be made up of small groups of approximately 4-5
students depending on class enrollment and areas of focus. You may request your team members (please
submit to the TA) otherwise students will be randomly assigned to a team.
The team will be graded as a group. Each team will be responsible for group assignments, as well as a final
presentation, Executive Summary, Business Plan. If your team cannot resolve a problem amongst yourselves,
please meet with the TA or me. Your team participation will be evaluated by your peers based upon a survey that
will be completed by your team members at the end of the quarter.
COMPUTER, DEVICE, & PHONE USAGE IN CLASS

to multi-task, this can be distracting to your fellow students. Please use devices in class to help you engage in this
class. No mobile phones or other mobile devices are allowed for use during class. If you violate this policy, you
will be asked to leave the class immediately. Breaks are provided during class to check mobile devices.
VIRTUAL CLASSES
It is possible a class may need to be taught virtually via Zoom due to unforeseen circumstances. If needed,
please make sure you watch all Zoom recordings. You are responsible for the material covered in class whether
you attend or not.
