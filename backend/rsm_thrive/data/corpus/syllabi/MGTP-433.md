# MGTP 433 — Current Issues in the Practice and Regulation of Auditing

Rady MPAc (Accountancy) · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course examines current issues facing private and public sector accounting
institutions and their regulators, including the PCAOB and SEC. It covers standard
setting bodies for accounting, auditing, and ESG, as well as audit quality, technology,
automation, and practice management. Students engage through lectures, guest speakers,
discussions, and a group project.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- understanding regulatory frameworks
- evaluating audit quality
- analyzing accounting and auditing standards
- assessing materiality
- communicating effectively in writing and presentations
- preparing for careers in public accounting
- applying current issues to business contexts

## Topics covered

us gaap hierarchy, accounting regulation, regulatory bodies (pcaob, sec), standard setting, audit quality, esg reporting, earnings management, materiality, practice management, technology and automation in auditing.

COURSE OVERVIEW AND JUSTIFICATION:
This course examines current issues facing private and public sector accounting institutions and their regulators, including
the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (“PCAOB”) and the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”). The
course will examine the challenges, trends and opportunities in the profession of auditing and accounting for those in
public accounting firms as well as the firms themselves. How these challenges and trends impact those professionals with
clients of public accounting firms will also be explored.
The purpose of MGTP 433 is to introduce students to the regulation and practice of public accounting and the operation
of public accounting firms. Topics include the standard setting bodies and the standard setting process relative to
Accounting, Auditing and ESG.
FOCUS:
Present, consider and discuss current issues facing public accounting firms including Audit quality, Regulatory and New
Accounting Standards, Technology, Automation and Artificial Intelligence, Practice Management, Risk Management and
Remote work.
COURSE OUTCOMES:
1. Become familiar with the standard setting bodies and the process by which standards are set by the various
bodies.
2. Be aware of how the underlying standards impact the public accounting firms and professionals, clients served
as well as other stakeholders.
3. Recognize the importance of and focus on Audit Quality; how Audit Quality is evaluated, measured and
reported.
4. Gain an understanding of the structure, operation and practice management of public accounting firms.
5. Possess an awareness of the lines of service within public accounting firms.
6. Develop fundamental knowledge of the focus areas and findings of regulators and evaluators in accounting,
reporting and auditing.
7. Be empathetic to the impact of the current issues facing the profession of auditing and public accounting on the
clients of the public accounting firms and professionals with whom the public accountant interacts.
8. Gain an appreciation of the impact current issues facing the profession have on global businesses.
9. Communicate effectively both in writing and presentations.
10. Commencing a career in public accounting, or the finance team of an organization, with an understanding of
how the current issues facing the profession of accounting and auditing will impact you and your organization,
currently and in the time to come.

PARTICIPATION:
You are expected to be prepared for class, constructively participate in class discussions and contribute to your group
project.
QUIZZES:
Each class will start with a very brief In-person quiz that covers (1) the lecture content from the prior class and (2) readings
assigned and due for that class. These quizzes are not expected to be tricky or exceedingly challenging; rather, meant to
simply ensure that you are reading the Assignments and reviewing the prior week’s notes. You may drop the lowest score.
SPEAKER ADVANCED PREP.:
In advance of a guest joining our class there will be an individual assignment to complete. The assignment will include
you researching the speaker’s experiences and accomplishments. Your findings will be posted to a class discussion board
along with topics you would like addressed by our guest as well as questions you have for the speaker.
SPEAKER REFLECTIONS:
Following presentations by our Guest you will be asked to submit a short paper (not to exceed 200 words). The paper
should include your reflection on what you heard and learned from the speaker(s).
GROUP PROJECT:
In summary, students will work in small groups to prepare a presentation to the class and lead a discussion addressing a
subject of interest to the group related to current issues in the practice and regulation of accounting and/or auditing.
More detailed project requirements will be provided in class and posted to Canvas.
FINAL EXAM:
Consistent with the requirements of the CPA exam, students will be given a closed-note, closed-book exam which will
ensure that they have achieved the learning objectives of the course.
