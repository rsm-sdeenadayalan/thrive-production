# MGTA 459 — Managerial Judgment and Decision Making

**4 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered FA25, FA25, FA26

## What this course covers

A two-part course on making better decisions and getting them implemented. Part one
examines judgment under uncertainty (heuristics, biases, noise, group decisions, models
vs. intuition, risk taking); part two covers building influence through coalitions,
networks, and persuasion, ending with a personal action plan.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- recognizing and mitigating decision biases
- designing decision processes
- structuring group decisions
- applying persuasion frameworks
- expanding personal influence

## Topics covered

heuristics and biases, judgment under uncertainty, group decision making, noise in judgment, models vs. intuition, risk taking and framing, coalitions and networks, persuasion and influence.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

25% participation, 35% take-home midterm, 40% final project (Personal Action Plan).

## Workload

Moderate. Heavy reading and case prep, frequent pre-class surveys/assignments, take-home midterm, open-format final project; no coding.

DESCRIPTION
This course is composed of two parts. They are quite distinct yet critically connected: the first is
about deriving better analyses and solutions, and the second is about implementing them.
In our initial sessions, we will examine how people make judgments and decisions, concentrating on
how people perceive and react to uncertainty and variability. We will lay out key implications for
business strategies. Consider a biotech start-up that is attempting to develop a new cancer drug.
Suppose its managers must choose between divergent research tracks and market niches, each of
which shows some promise but also carries some risks. How will they weigh the promise versus the
risks, evaluate relevant information, and settle on a way to proceed? The hope is that a savvy
understanding of the answers to such questions can inform your approach to many business
settings.
Of course, it is not enough to be skillful at analysis. To succeed in business, you must also be adept
at engaging others and getting their buy-in and follow through. For instance, the managers of the
biotech start-up may wish to motivate employees and other stakeholders to devote their energies to
a particular research approach. They may wish to rally investors around funding this approach.
Accordingly, in our latter sessions, we study how to best build, protect, and exert influence to
achieve your goals. A key idea is that developing multiple tools of influence is necessary because
different circumstances call for different tools.

OBJECTIVES
At the close of this course, you should better understand
 How people think about uncertainty and variability
 How to design effective decision processes
 What influence tactics you tend to rely on
 How to construct a plan for expanding the set of influence tactics you use

HOW THIS COURSE WORKS
During class, we will typically pair an exercise or case discussion with a lecture. Exercises and
cases illustrate and explore key concepts; lectures further develop and summarize these
concepts.

DELIVERABLES
There are four kinds of deliverables in this course: surveys, assignments, a midterm exam, and a final
project. In the schedule summary on the previous page, individual deliverables are highlighted in
yellow. For further information on each of them, follow the included link or see the session-bysession outline later in this syllabus.

GRADING
Your grade will be based on participation (25%), a midterm (35%) and a final project (40%). All
assignments are individual.
PARTICIPATION (25%)
Classroom discussion is an integral part of this course. Much of your learning will come from
the insights we are able to achieve by collectively mulling course concepts. Moreover, to fully
engage with the course material you need to be actively involved in the discussion.
Because it is vital to your learning and the learning of your colleagues, your participation is
assessed and factored into your grade. I make notes after each class regarding each student’s
role in the discussion. So does the teaching assistant. Near the end of the term, I will solicit
your input about which students best-facilitated your learning.
We are all jointly responsible for the quality of the discussion. I would like for you to focus on
being prepared, engaged and respectful of others. We want to cultivate a direct but friendly
environment that encourages honesty and risk-taking. Mistakes are okay, especially as long
as we are striving for insight. Irreverence is okay. Questions others know the answer to are
okay. As long as you are prepared, engaged and respectful, almost anything goes.

Everyone should participate. This means some of you will have to fight your natural tendencies
– those accustomed to speaking might need to back off, those accustomed to not speaking will
need to step up. This gives everyone a chance to learn from others’ experiences, as well as a
chance to work on effective communication.
Finally, your on-time and proper completion of the surveys and other assignments for each
class will facilitate our discussions and thus contribute substantially to what you and your
colleagues learn. It thus also factors into your grade.
MIDTERM EXAM (35%)
We will have a take-home midterm after the first part of the course. It will be open-book, opennote, individual work. It will consist of a combination of multiple choice and short answer
questions. Some of the material on the exam will appear both in class and in the readings, but
some of the material will appear either only in class or only in the readings.
FINAL PROJECT – PERSONAL ACTION PLAN (40%)
Your final project is to put together a personal distillation of concepts from the second part of
our course. This can take any form – paper, powerpoint, website, video, art project, song,
game, etc. Literally any form that works for you. You can think of it as one of those “cheat
sheets” that some stats/econ profs allow for their exams. The idea is to boil down as much as
possible of what you consider to be the most useful information from the course into a format
you can carry forward. The goal is for you to use these ideas in the future. This is intended as a
vehicle for that.

ACADEMIC INTEGRITY
Integrity of scholarship is essential for an academic community. As members of the Rady School, we
pledge ourselves to uphold the highest ethical standards. The University expects that both faculty and
students will honor this principle and in so doing protect the validity of University intellectual work. For
students, this means that all academic work will be done by the individual to whom it is assigned,
without unauthorized aid of any kind.
The complete UCSD Policy on Integrity of Scholarship can be viewed at:
http://senate.ucsd.edu/Operating-Procedures/Senate-Manual/Appendices/2

USE OF AI TOOLS
The use of AI tools such as ChatGPT is allowed in this course, with the following condition:
Consistent with our emphasis on academic integrity, if you use an AI tool in completing an
assignment or exam, please make sure to be transparent about how you have done so. You should
include a footnote or citation that summarizes what you have done (e.g., a link to the ChatGPT chat
you created).

A student who has a disability or special need and requires an accommodation in order to have equal
