# MGTA 495 — Special Topics: AI & Prescriptive Analytics

**4 units** · MSBA (Rady MS Business Analytics) · offered WI26

## What this course covers

An integrated introduction to AI and prescriptive analytics: first half covers deep
learning (MLPs, CNNs, RNNs/LSTMs, transformers, NLP, LLMs) plus interpretability and
bias; second half covers discrete-event simulation, mixed-integer programming, and
dynamic programming for data-driven decisions. Hands-on Python labs apply methods to
operations, finance, and healthcare.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- building deep learning models
- evaluating AI fairness and interpretability
- simulation modeling with SimPy
- solving MIP problems with DOcplex
- sequential decision problems

## Topics covered

deep neural networks, computer vision with CNNs, NLP and LLMs, explainable AI and bias, discrete-event simulation, mixed-integer programming, dynamic programming.

## Tools used

Python, SimPy, DOcplex.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

30% homework, 20% attendance/participation, 20% take-home midterm, 30% final exam.

Heavy. Five homework assignments plus take-home midterm and final exam over 10 weeks, with mandatory attendance.

DESCRIPTION
This course provides an integrated introduction to Artificial Intelligence (AI), optimization, and simulation
techniques that form the foundation of prescriptive analytics. In the first half of the course, students
explore core AI and deep learning methods that enable data-driven prediction, pattern recognition,
computer vision, natural language processing (NLP), and large language models (LLMs). We will also
discuss model interpretability, fairness, and the mitigation of algorithmic bias in AI models. The second
half of the course transitions to prescriptive analytics and discusses how simulation and optimization
methods can guide data-driven analytics and decision-making in business. Topics include mixed-integer
programming (MIP) for resource allocation and planning, dynamic programming for sequential decisionmaking under uncertainty, and discrete-event simulation for modeling complex systems in operations,
finance, and healthcare. Through a combination of lectures, real-world examples, and hands-on coding
labs, students will gain a practical and conceptual understanding of how modern AI, simulation, and
mathematical modeling techniques can be applied to optimize processes, improve outcomes, and support
strategic decision-making in various business domains.

Understand foundational concepts of artificial intelligence (AI), mathematical optimization, and

Critically assess analytical models and results, understanding the underlying assumptions,
strengths, and limitations of AI, optimization, and simulation methods when applied to complex,
real-world decision problems.

COURSE POLICIES
Course Format. The course will be offered in person and will consist of 10 weekly sessions. Sessions will
include interactive lectures and hands-on coding exercises in Python. Students are encouraged to bring
their laptops to follow along with lecture notes and coding exercises. All lectures will be recorded.
Attendance Policy. In-person attendance is mandatory for all students. If you must miss a class due to a
valid reason (e.g., illness or family emergency), you must email the instructor with appropriate
documentation (proof of valid reason) and watch the lecture recording in full within 7 days of the missed
class to receive the attendance credit. Missing more than 3 classes will result in a failing grade (“F”) for
the course – no exceptions.
Expectations and Late Submissions. Students will be expected to complete five homework
assignments, participate in class discussions, and take a midterm exam (take-home) and a final exam. All
assignments must be turned in via Canvas by the specified deadline. Late submission will incur a penalty
of 10% of the total points for each 24-hour period (or part thereof) past the deadline, and submissions are
not accepted after solutions are posted on Canvas (usually 2-3 days after the due date). Students are
encouraged to submit their assignments early if they anticipate delays closer to the due date.
Use of AI Tools such as ChatGPT. Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT) can be used to brainstorm ideas for
your assignments and assist in debugging your code. However, you should understand and explain every
step of your analysis in your submission. You should also fully understand and be able to explain all
details of every line of code that you submit as your own work. Any other use of GenAI requires
preapproval and must be documented and submitted along with the relevant assignment. Failure to
submit documentation or identify use of AI is considered an integrity violation:
https://academicintegrity.ucsd.edu/excel-integrity/gen-ai/index.html
Changes. The instructors reserve the right to make changes during the term to any aspect of the syllabus
that, in their judgment, are needed to achieve the learning objectives of the course.
