# MGT 456 — Innovative Product and Program Management (iPM)

Rady MBA · offered SP26 · _unit count not stated in the material supplied_

## What this course covers

This course covers the knowledge, skills, and competencies needed to become a creative,
high-potential product and program manager in the AI age. It addresses product strategy,
portfolio and program management, agile product development, product lifecycle
management, and the responsibilities of product managers, with a focus on digitally-
enriched products. Classes include instructor-led discussions, in-class exercises, guest
speakers, team exercises, and a hands-on team project with a Silicon Valley company.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- creating product strategy and roadmaps
- conducting customer discovery interviews
- writing product requirements documents
- building wireframes and prototypes
- designing and analyzing a/b tests
- prioritizing product features and trade-offs
- managing product lifecycle and launches
- pitching product ideas and results

## Topics covered

product management roles and responsibilities, product strategy and vision, roadmapping and portfolio management, product definition and opportunity identification, customer interviews and user studies, product design and prototyping, agile product and program management, product lifecycle management, pricing and product launch, product pitching and presentations.

## Tools used

canvas, zoom, google keyword tool, ifttt, wireframing tools, user testing resources, ai tools.

## Prerequisites

None stated in the syllabus.

## How it is graded

The final grade is based on homework/case critiques (15%), class participation (25%), a midterm exam (30%), and a team class project (30%).

## Workload

Heavy. Graded work includes team and individual homework/case critiques, active class participation every session, a midterm exam, and a team project with intermediate deliverables and a tested prototype.

COURSE OBJECTIVES
The Innovative Product and Program Management (iPM) class deals with the knowledge, skills,
and competencies needed to become a creative, high-potential Product and Program Manager in
the AI age. While we focus on increasingly ubiquitous digitally-enriched products and projects,
we also consider management of conventional products and programs (life sciences and
electromechanical offerings) in a digital, high-velocity environment. Key aspects of product
strategy, portfolio and program management, agile product development, and product lifecycle
management are discussed, as well as the associated responsibilities of the product manager.
This course is intended for students seeking a PM position in either a new or established
enterprise and is based on management development programs developed for top companies by
the Professor and taught at MIT and UC San Diego. The course augments and enriches content
on business strategy, marketing, entrepreneurship, and operations management- so counts
towards Marketing and Supply Chain certificates at UC San Diego’s Rady School.. Class
sessions consist of instructor-led discussions, in-class exercises and guest speakers on PM topics.
Students work on team-based exercises, and a team-based hands-on PM project in the field with
a silicon valley company.

Homework/Case Critique Assignments (15%):
A combination of team papers and individual homeworks (briefs, as announced before hand) and
submitted through the Canvas dropbox, these assignments will help practice critical and creative
thinking skills needed for product management.
Class Participation (25%):
Your class participation (contribution to class discussions and/or response to in-class
questions/quizzes) will count for 25% of the class grade. We may also use Zoom/canvas online

discussion forums for class participation. You are expected to be an active participant throughout
the entire class and to contribute to the quality of the discussion. Please note that the frequency
(i.e., the quantity) of your interventions in class is not a key criterion for effective class
contribution.
Some criteria used to evaluate class contribution are as follows:
1. Is the participant deeply engaged in class? Use of laptops and electronic devices for
non-class purposes can negatively affect class participation and learning experience.
2. Is the participant a good listener? Are the points made relevant to the discussion? Are
they linked to the comments of others? Do the comments show evidence of analysis of
the case?
3. Is there willingness to test new ideas, or are all comments "safe"? (For example,
repetition of case facts without analysis and conclusions).
4. Do comments clarify or build upon the important aspects of earlier comments and lead to
a clearer understanding of the case?
Note: You are expected to attend EVERY class session; you are responsible for the material
covered in class whether or not you attend.
Midterm Exam (Week 7/8; In-class)

30% (Individual)

There will be an in-class exam on Week 7/8, whose goal is to review the material learned and
learn to apply them in real-life settings. The exam will be partly analytical and the exact
modality will be discussed in class two weeks prior to the exam.
Class Project (with Intermediate Milestones): 30%
The class project is a key element of this class, in which you will focus as a team on a digital
product you like, understand the roadmap and product strategy, identify unmet needs and define
a key feature not yet offered in the product, develop requirements, complete a testable prototype,
and collect customer feedback. You will learn to use AI-based tooks introduced in the class.
At the mid-term milestone, each team will submit their product strategy/roadmap and unmet
needs. There will be a project deliverable due the last class session which will count for 20% of
the class grade, and will include a prototype tested with user testing resources. As a team, you
would present your project test results and lessons learned. There is no final exam in this class.
